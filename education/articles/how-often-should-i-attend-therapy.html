How Often Should I Attend Therapy

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Most people attend therapy once a week when they first start, then often move to every other week or monthly as they make progress. Weekly sessions help build momentum, trust, and skills in the early stages, while less frequent sessions can work well once things are more stable. The right frequency depends on what you are working on, how intense your symptoms are, the type of therapy, your budget and schedule, and your own preferences. It is not fixed, and you and your therapist can adjust it as your needs change.

How It Works

Frequency is usually discussed in the first session or two and revisited along the way. Common patterns include:

  1. Weekly to start. Weekly sessions are common for the first several weeks or months, especially for structured approaches such as CBT, where skills build from one session to the next.
  2. More often during a crisis. Some people need two sessions a week for a short period during a crisis or intensive treatment, such as some forms of exposure therapy.
  3. Every other week as things improve. When symptoms ease and skills are in place, many people move to biweekly sessions to practise independently.
  4. Monthly or as needed for maintenance. Some people continue with monthly check-ins or booster sessions to stay on track.

Couples therapy often follows a similar pattern, with weekly sessions at first and less frequent sessions later.

Practical Details

What affects frequency. Intense symptoms, safety concerns, recent trauma, or complex difficulties may call for more frequent sessions. Milder concerns or maintenance phases often work well with less frequent sessions. Some approaches, such as comprehensive DBT, have set formats that include weekly individual therapy and a skills group.

Cost and coverage. Cost is a real factor for many people. Extended health benefits often have an annual maximum, so some people space sessions to make coverage last. It can help to check your plan and discuss options with your therapist. The clinic can answer questions about fees and scheduling.

Time and energy. Work, caregiving, and travel can affect how often sessions are realistic. Virtual sessions can make regular attendance easier for some people.

Too far apart. When sessions are spaced too widely early on, progress can stall because there is little time to build on each session. Consistency often matters more than the exact interval.

How frequency relates to the overall length of therapy is covered on the page about how long therapy usually takes.

How to Decide With Your Therapist

  • Share your goals, symptoms, and any practical limits, such as budget or schedule, at the start.
  • Ask what frequency they recommend for your concerns and why.
  • Check in every few months about whether the current frequency still fits.
  • If you notice you are struggling between sessions, consider asking for more frequent sessions for a while.
  • If things are going well, ask whether spacing sessions out would help you practise skills independently.

Signs that therapy is helping, which can guide decisions about spacing, are discussed on the page about how to know if therapy is working. If weekly sessions do not feel like enough, more intensive options may be considered. If you are in crisis between sessions, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About Therapy Frequency

Is weekly therapy necessary when I first start?

Weekly sessions are common at the start of therapy because they help build trust, momentum, and skills, though frequency can be adjusted.

When can I move from weekly to every-other-week sessions?

Many people shift to biweekly therapy sessions once symptoms ease and they can practise skills more independently.

Is monthly therapy enough to make progress?

Monthly sessions often work well for maintenance, but spacing therapy that widely early on can slow progress.

Are there times when therapy sessions twice a week make sense?

Some people attend twice a week for a short period during a crisis or intensive treatment, then return to weekly sessions.

Can I space out therapy sessions so my extended health coverage lasts all year?

Some people space sessions to stretch an annual maximum. Check your plan and discuss a realistic session frequency with your therapist.

Does the type of therapy affect how often I should go?

Yes. Structured approaches like CBT often start weekly, and some programs, such as comprehensive DBT, have set weekly formats.

How often do couples usually attend therapy sessions?

Couples often start with weekly sessions and move to less frequent sessions as communication improves.

What if I feel I need therapy more often than I am going?

Talk with your therapist about increasing session frequency for a while, especially if you are struggling between sessions.

Who decides how often I attend therapy?

You and your therapist decide session frequency together, based on your goals, symptoms, and practical limits.

Does going to therapy less often mean I am finished?

Not necessarily. Spacing sessions out is often a step toward ending therapy, with check-ins or booster sessions if needed.
