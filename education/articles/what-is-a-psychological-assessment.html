What Is a Psychological Assessment

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

A psychological assessment is a structured evaluation, usually conducted by a registered psychologist, that uses interviews, questionnaires, standardized tests, and sometimes information from family members or teachers to answer specific questions about a person's thinking, emotions, behaviour, learning, or development. Common reasons include clarifying a diagnosis such as ADHD, autism, a learning disorder, or depression, understanding strengths and challenges, and guiding treatment, school supports, or workplace accommodations. The process ends with a written report and a feedback session that explains the findings and recommendations. It is more detailed than a typical therapy intake and is designed to give a clear, evidence-based picture.

How It Works

  1. Clarifying the questions. The psychologist discusses what you want to understand and whether an assessment is the right step.
  2. Clinical interview. A detailed conversation about current concerns, developmental and medical history, school or work, relationships, and mental health.
  3. Questionnaires and rating scales. Completed by you and sometimes by a partner, parent, or teacher.
  4. Standardized testing. Depending on the questions, tests may measure attention, memory, intellectual abilities, academic skills, executive functioning, or social communication.
  5. Integration and report. The psychologist combines the information, considers alternative explanations, and writes a report, as described on the page about what is included in an assessment report.
  6. Feedback session. The findings, any diagnosis, and recommendations are reviewed with you.

Practical Details

Types of assessment. Common types include ADHD assessments, autism assessments, psychoeducational assessments for learning, diagnostic assessments for mood, anxiety, or personality, and neuropsychological assessments after injury or illness. The page about how autism is diagnosed in adults describes one example.

Time involved. Assessments may involve several appointments over a few weeks, with testing sessions lasting a few hours. Report writing adds time afterward.

Referrals. Private assessments in BC can usually be booked without a doctor's referral.

Cost. Private psychological assessments are not covered by MSP. Fees vary depending on the type and complexity of the assessment, and some extended health plans cover part of the cost. Ask the clinic about fees and what is included.

Virtual or in person. Some parts can be done virtually, while certain standardized tests are best done in person. Ask how the assessment will be delivered.

How to Prepare

  • Write down your questions. What do you hope to learn from the assessment?
  • Gather records. School report cards, past assessments, medical information, and work evaluations can be helpful.
  • Ask others for input if requested. A parent, partner, or teacher may be asked to complete questionnaires.
  • Look after yourself on testing days. Get rest, eat beforehand, bring glasses or hearing aids, and take any usual medications unless told otherwise.
  • Be honest. There are no right or wrong answers, and accurate information leads to more useful results.

For ADHD or autism specifically, the page about preparing for an ADHD or autism assessment offers more detail. After the assessment, the feedback session is where results are explained.

Common Questions About Psychological Assessments

Why might someone need a psychological assessment?

A psychological assessment is used to clarify diagnoses such as ADHD, autism, or learning disorders, understand strengths and challenges, and guide treatment and supports.

Who conducts a psychological assessment in BC?

A psychological assessment in BC is usually conducted by a registered psychologist trained in standardized testing and diagnosis.

What happens during psychological testing?

During psychological testing, you complete standardized tasks and questionnaires that may measure attention, memory, reasoning, academic skills, or emotional wellbeing.

How long does a psychological assessment take?

A psychological assessment often involves several appointments over a few weeks, with testing sessions lasting a few hours, plus time for report writing.

Is a psychological assessment covered by MSP?

No. Private psychological assessments in BC are not covered by MSP, though some extended health plans cover part of the cost.

What types of psychological assessment are there?

Types include ADHD, autism, psychoeducational, diagnostic, and neuropsychological assessments, each designed to answer different questions.

Can a psychological assessment be done virtually?

Some parts of a psychological assessment can be done virtually, but certain standardized tests are best completed in person.

How is an assessment different from a therapy intake?

An assessment is a structured, detailed evaluation with standardized testing and a written report, while a therapy intake is a shorter conversation to plan therapy.

How should I prepare for psychological testing?

Before testing, rest well, eat beforehand, bring glasses or hearing aids, take usual medications unless told otherwise, and gather relevant records.

Can I fail a psychological assessment?

No. You cannot pass or fail a psychological assessment. Honest answers and genuine effort simply lead to more accurate, useful results.
