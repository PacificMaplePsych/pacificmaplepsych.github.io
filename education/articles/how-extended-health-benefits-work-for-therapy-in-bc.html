How Extended Health Benefits Work for Therapy in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, therapy with a private psychologist, registered clinical counsellor, or other therapist is not covered by MSP, the provincial health plan. Many people use extended health benefits, usually through an employer, a union, a spouse's plan, or an individual policy, to cover part of the cost. Plans typically set an annual maximum for each type of provider, may reimburse a percentage of each session, and list which professional designations are eligible. Some plans require a physician's referral. Because plans vary widely, the most important step is to read your plan details or contact your provider before booking, so you know what to expect.

How It Works

  1. Check eligible providers. Plans often cover registered psychologists and may also cover registered clinical counsellors, clinical social workers, or other designations. Coverage depends on the provider's credentials, not just the type of therapy.
  2. Check the annual maximum. Many plans set a yearly dollar limit per provider type, which can be used up quickly with regular sessions.
  3. Check the reimbursement rate. Some plans reimburse 100 percent up to a per-session limit, while others cover a set percentage.
  4. Check referral requirements. Some plans ask for a physician's referral before reimbursing psychology sessions.
  5. Pay and submit. You usually pay the clinic and submit an official receipt to your insurer, often through an app or website. Some clinics offer direct billing.

Practical Details

Health spending accounts. Some employers offer a health spending account, which can be used for eligible health expenses, including therapy, beyond the regular plan limits.

Coordination of benefits. If you and a spouse or partner both have coverage, you may be able to claim through one plan first and the other for the remaining amount.

Couples therapy. Some plans cover couples sessions under one partner's coverage. Check how your plan handles joint sessions.

Assessments. Psychological assessments may be covered under the same psychology maximum, which can use up much of a year's coverage. Ask your plan, as discussed on the page about private versus public assessment.

Employee assistance programs. Many employers offer a limited number of free, short-term counselling sessions through an employee assistance program, separate from extended health benefits.

Tax credits. Fees for services from certain eligible health practitioners, including psychologists, may qualify for the federal medical expense tax credit. Check current CRA rules.

Privacy. Insurers usually receive basic information such as provider, date, and fee, not the content of sessions, as explained on the page about whether therapy is confidential.

Other funding sources. WorkSafeBC, ICBC, crime victim assistance programs, or Indigenous health benefits may cover therapy in specific situations.

How to Make the Most of Your Benefits

  • Read your plan booklet or call your provider. Ask which designations are covered, the annual maximum, the reimbursement rate, and referral rules.
  • Ask the clinic about the clinician's designation. This helps you confirm eligibility.
  • Plan session frequency. Consider how far your coverage will go, and talk with your therapist, as discussed on the page about how often to attend therapy.
  • Keep receipts. Submit claims promptly, since plans often have deadlines.
  • Get a referral if required. See the page about referrals to see a psychologist.

Common Questions About Extended Health Benefits for Therapy in BC

Is private therapy in BC paid for by the provincial health plan?

No. MSP does not cover private therapy with a psychologist or counsellor in BC, so many people use extended health benefits.

How do extended health benefits work for therapy?

Extended health benefits usually reimburse part of each therapy session, up to an annual maximum, for eligible provider types listed in your plan.

Which therapists are covered by extended health plans?

Extended health plans often cover registered psychologists and may also cover registered clinical counsellors or clinical social workers. Check your plan.

What is an annual maximum for therapy coverage?

An annual maximum is the yearly dollar limit your plan pays for a provider type, such as psychology, which regular sessions can use up quickly.

Do I need a doctor's referral for my benefits to cover therapy?

Some benefit plans require a physician's referral to reimburse therapy sessions, while others do not. Check your plan details.

Can I use a health spending account for therapy?

Yes. A health spending account can often be used for therapy, including costs beyond your regular extended health plan limits.

Can I claim therapy on both my plan and my spouse's plan?

Often yes. Coordination of benefits may let you claim therapy through one plan first and your spouse's plan for the remaining amount.

Does my insurer see what I talk about in therapy?

No. Your insurer usually receives basic claim information such as provider, date, and fee, not the content of therapy sessions.

Can therapy costs not covered by my plan be claimed as a medical expense?

Fees for certain eligible practitioners, including psychologists, may qualify for the federal medical expense tax credit. Check current CRA rules.

Besides extended health benefits, what else might pay for therapy in BC?

Employee assistance programs, WorkSafeBC, ICBC, crime victim assistance, or Indigenous health benefits may cover therapy in specific situations.
