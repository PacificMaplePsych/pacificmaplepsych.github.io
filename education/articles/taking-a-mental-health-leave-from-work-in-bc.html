Taking a Mental Health Leave From Work in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

If you are having thoughts of suicide or feel unable to keep yourself safe, call or text 9-8-8 (Canada's Suicide Crisis Helpline) at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

In BC, employees can take time off work for mental health reasons in much the same way as for a physical illness. Depending on how long you need, options may include paid and unpaid sick days under the Employment Standards Act, short-term or long-term disability benefits through your workplace, and Employment Insurance (EI) sickness benefits for up to 26 weeks. Longer leaves usually require documentation from a health professional, such as your family doctor. Employers also have a duty under the BC Human Rights Code to accommodate employees with mental health conditions, up to the point of undue hardship. A leave is often most helpful when it includes active treatment and a plan for returning.

When a Leave Might Help

A leave may be worth considering if symptoms of depression, anxiety, burnout, or trauma are making it very hard to concentrate, make decisions, or get through the day, or if work itself is a major source of harm. The page about burnout describes common signs. Sometimes, adjusted duties or reduced hours work better than a full leave, which is worth discussing with your health care provider.

Your Options in BC

Sick Days

Most employees covered by the BC Employment Standards Act are entitled to five paid sick days per year after 90 days of employment, plus additional unpaid days for illness or injury. Some workplaces offer more under their own policies or collective agreements.

Short-Term and Long-Term Disability

Many employers provide disability insurance that replaces part of your income during a longer illness. These plans usually require medical documentation and ongoing updates.

EI Sickness Benefits

If you do not have workplace disability coverage, or it does not cover your situation, you may qualify for EI sickness benefits for up to 26 weeks. You will need a medical certificate from a doctor or other approved medical practitioner.

Accommodation

Under the BC Human Rights Code, mental health conditions are protected, and employers must accommodate employees' needs to the point of undue hardship. Accommodations might include a gradual return, flexible hours, or changes to duties.

Steps to Take

  1. See your family doctor to discuss your symptoms, documentation, and treatment.
  2. Review your benefits and workplace policies, or speak with human resources.
  3. Share only what is needed. Employers usually need to know about your limitations and expected time away, not your diagnosis.
  4. Keep copies of forms, notes, and correspondence.
  5. Start treatment, such as therapy, as early as possible.
  6. Plan your return before your leave ends, as described on the page about returning to work after a mental health leave.

Making the Most of a Leave

Rest is important, but recovery usually involves more than time away. Therapy can help you understand what led to the leave, rebuild routines, and develop strategies to prevent a return of symptoms. The page about what rest actually looks like when you are burnt out explains why structured rest matters, and the page about time off vs. therapy explores how they work together.

What It Is Not

Taking a mental health leave is not a sign of weakness, and it is not the same as quitting. It is a legitimate health measure. If the leave relates to a workplace injury, bullying, or a traumatic event at work, WorkSafeBC may also be involved. The page about workplace stress describes related pressures. This page offers general information, not legal advice, so for specific questions about your rights, speak with your union, an employment lawyer, or the Employment Standards Branch.

Common Questions About Mental Health Leave in BC

Can I take a mental health leave from work in BC?

Yes. In BC, you can take time off for mental health reasons through sick days, disability benefits, or EI sickness benefits, depending on your situation.

How many paid sick days do BC employees get?

Most employees covered by the BC Employment Standards Act get five paid sick days per year after 90 days of employment.

How long can EI sickness benefits last?

EI sickness benefits can last up to 26 weeks for eligible workers.

Do I need a doctor's note for a mental health leave in BC?

Longer mental health leaves usually require documentation from a doctor or other approved health professional.

How much medical information does my employer need for a mental health leave?

Usually, an employer needs to know your limitations and expected time away, not your specific diagnosis, for a mental health leave.

Can my employer fire me for taking a mental health leave in BC?

Mental health conditions are protected under the BC Human Rights Code, and employers have a duty to accommodate to the point of undue hardship.

What is the duty to accommodate in BC?

The duty to accommodate means employers must adjust work, such as hours or duties, for employees with disabilities, including mental health conditions, unless it causes undue hardship.

Should I go to therapy during a mental health leave?

Yes. Therapy during a mental health leave can help address what led to it, rebuild routines, and plan a sustainable return.

Can burnout qualify for a mental health leave?

Burnout often overlaps with depression or anxiety, and a health professional can assess whether time off or accommodations are needed.

Does WorkSafeBC cover mental health leave?

WorkSafeBC may cover mental health conditions caused by significant work-related stressors or traumatic events at work.
