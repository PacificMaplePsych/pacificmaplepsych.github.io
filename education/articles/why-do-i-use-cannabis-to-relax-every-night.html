Why Do I Use Cannabis to Relax Every Night

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

It started as a way to take the edge off after work. Now it is part of your nightly routine. You look forward to it through the afternoon, and evenings without it feel restless or incomplete. Falling asleep without it has become difficult. You might wonder whether it is really a problem, since cannabis is legal and many people use it, but you have noticed you rely on it more than you used to.

Many adults use cannabis occasionally without problems. When it becomes something you need every night to relax or sleep, it is worth looking at what it is doing for you and what it might be costing you.

If you notice paranoia, hearing or seeing things others do not, or losing touch with reality after using cannabis, stop using and seek medical care. Call 911 in an emergency. If you are having thoughts of suicide, call or text 9-8-8.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Stress relief. Cannabis can offer quick relief from daily stress. Over time, it can become the main way you unwind, similar to relying on alcohol to unwind.

Anxiety. Some people use cannabis to quiet anxious thoughts. In the long run, regular use can worsen anxiety for some, and some people feel more on edge on days without it.

Sleep problems. Cannabis can help some people fall asleep, but regular use can reduce sleep quality, and stopping can cause vivid dreams and poor sleep for a while. If sleep is the main reason you use, see Why Can't I Sleep Even When I'm Exhausted.

ADHD. Some adults with ADHD use cannabis to calm a restless mind, especially if they have not been diagnosed or treated.

Low mood or trauma. Cannabis can be a way to numb painful feelings or memories, which can delay getting support.

Habit. Over time, the routine itself becomes a cue, so the evening feels wrong without it.

When It Looks More Like a Cannabis Use Problem

Cannabis use disorder exists on a spectrum, from mild to severe. Research suggests a significant minority of people who use cannabis regularly develop problems with it. Signs include:

  • Using more or for longer than intended, or finding it hard to cut back, a pattern also seen when people drink more than they planned to.
  • Strong cravings or feeling irritable, anxious, or restless without it.
  • Trouble sleeping or low appetite when you stop.
  • Needing more to get the same effect.
  • Use affecting work, relationships, motivation, or memory.
  • Giving up activities you used to enjoy.

Canada's Lower-Risk Cannabis Use Guidelines suggest limiting frequency, choosing lower-THC products, and avoiding use when driving. People with a personal or family history of psychosis are advised to avoid cannabis, as high-THC products can increase risk.

What Can Help

  • Track your use. Note when, how much, and why you use, to see patterns.
  • Build other ways to unwind. Exercise, hobbies, baths, and time with others can replace some of what cannabis provides.
  • Try cannabis-free nights. Starting with a few nights a week can show you how you feel without it.
  • Expect withdrawal. Irritability, poor sleep, and vivid dreams are common for a week or two after stopping, and they pass.
  • Address the underlying issue. Treating anxiety, sleep problems, or ADHD directly can reduce the pull to use.

Common Questions About Nightly Cannabis Use

Is it bad to use cannabis every night?

Not everyone who uses cannabis nightly develops problems, but daily use increases the risk of dependence, sleep changes, and effects on mood, memory, and motivation.

Can you become dependent on cannabis?

Yes. A significant minority of regular users develop cannabis use disorder, which can include cravings, difficulty cutting back, and withdrawal symptoms.

What are cannabis withdrawal symptoms?

Common symptoms include irritability, anxiety, restlessness, trouble sleeping, vivid dreams, and reduced appetite. They usually peak within the first week and fade over a few weeks.

Does cannabis help with anxiety?

Some people feel short-term relief, but regular use can worsen anxiety over time, and high-THC products can trigger anxiety or panic in some people.

Does cannabis improve sleep?

Cannabis may help some people fall asleep, but regular use can reduce sleep quality. Stopping can temporarily cause poor sleep and vivid dreams.

What are Canada's lower-risk cannabis use guidelines?

The guidelines recommend delaying use until later in life, choosing lower-THC products, limiting frequency, avoiding smoking, not driving after use, and avoiding cannabis with a personal or family history of psychosis.

Can cannabis cause psychosis?

High-THC cannabis can increase the risk of psychosis in some people, especially those with a personal or family history. Paranoia or hallucinations after use need medical attention.

Why do adults with ADHD use cannabis?

Some adults with ADHD use cannabis to calm restlessness or racing thoughts. Treating ADHD directly can reduce the pull to self-medicate.

How can I cut back on cannabis?

Track your use, set cannabis-free nights, find other ways to relax, expect temporary withdrawal, and address underlying stress, anxiety, or sleep problems.

Can therapy help with cannabis use?

Yes. Cognitive behavioural therapy and motivational approaches can help people understand their use, manage cravings, and build other coping strategies.
