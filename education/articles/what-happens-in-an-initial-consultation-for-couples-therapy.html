What Happens in an Initial Consultation for Couples Therapy

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

An initial consultation for couples therapy is a first meeting, sometimes a short call and sometimes a full session, where you and your partner describe what brings you in, hear how the therapist works, and decide together whether couples therapy and this therapist feel like a good fit. The therapist will usually ask about your main concerns, what you hope will change, how long difficulties have been present, and whether there are any safety concerns. They may also explain their approach, such as the Gottman Method or emotionally focused therapy, and how the assessment phase will unfold. It is a chance for both partners to ask questions before committing to ongoing sessions.

What to Expect

  1. Introductions and logistics. Fees, scheduling, cancellation policies, and how sessions are structured.
  2. Confidentiality and policies. Including how the therapist handles information shared individually, sometimes called a "no secrets" policy.
  3. Your reasons for coming. Each partner shares their view of the main concerns.
  4. Goals. What each of you would like to be different, and whether you are both committed to working on the relationship.
  5. Safety screening. Questions about conflict, fear, abuse, or coercive control, since joint sessions may not be safe in some situations.
  6. The therapist's approach. How they work, what the assessment phase looks like, and what to expect in the coming sessions.
  7. Your questions. About training, experience, and anything else that matters to you.

Practical Details

Length and format. Some clinics offer a brief consultation call, while others start with a full session of 50 to 90 minutes. Consultations can be in person or virtual, as described on the page about virtual couples therapy.

After the consultation. Many therapists follow up with individual sessions with each partner, then joint sessions, as explained on the page about the first couples therapy session.

When one partner is unsure. If one person is leaning out of the relationship, the therapist may suggest discernment counselling or individual sessions first.

Safety. If there is abuse or fear, the therapist may recommend individual support and safety planning instead of couples therapy. See the abuse resources page.

Fees and benefits. Ask the clinic about fees and check whether your extended health plan covers couples sessions.

Choosing between approaches. If you are not sure whether couples or individual therapy fits, see the page about individual versus couples therapy.

How to Prepare Together

  • Each write down your main concerns. And one or two hopes for therapy.
  • Agree on logistics. Who will book, when you can both attend, and childcare if needed.
  • Prepare questions. About the therapist's approach, experience with your concerns, session frequency, and expected length.
  • Avoid rehearsing the argument. You do not need to agree on what the problem is before attending.
  • Notice how you each feel afterward. Talk about whether the therapist seemed balanced and easy to talk to.

Understanding how often and how long couples usually attend can help you plan, as covered on the page about how long couples therapy takes.

Common Questions About Couples Therapy Consultations

What do partners discuss during a couples therapy consultation?

In a couples therapy consultation, both partners share their concerns and goals, the therapist explains their approach and policies, and safety is screened.

Is a couples therapy consultation a full session?

It depends on the clinic. Some offer a brief consultation call, while others start couples therapy with a full 50- to 90-minute session.

Do we have to agree on the problem before a couples consultation?

No. Partners often see the problem differently, and a couples consultation is a place for both perspectives to be heard.

What questions should we ask in a couples therapy consultation?

Ask about the therapist's approach, experience with your concerns, session frequency, expected length, fees, and how private information is handled.

Why does a couples consultation include safety screening?

A couples consultation includes safety screening because joint sessions may not be safe when there is abuse, fear, or coercive control.

What if one partner is unsure about staying in the relationship?

If one partner is unsure, the therapist may suggest discernment counselling or individual sessions before starting full couples therapy.

What comes after a couples therapy consultation?

After a couples consultation, many therapists schedule individual sessions with each partner, followed by regular joint couples sessions.

Can a couples therapy consultation be done online?

Yes. A couples therapy consultation can usually be held by secure video, ideally with both partners together in a private space in BC.

What signs suggest a couples counsellor is right for both partners?

A couples therapist may be a good fit if both partners feel heard, the therapist seems balanced, and you are comfortable with their approach.

How should couples prepare for a therapy consultation?

Each partner can write down concerns and hopes, agree on logistics, and prepare questions before a couples therapy consultation.
