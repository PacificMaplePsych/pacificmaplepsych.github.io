How Borderline Personality Disorder Is Treated

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

If you are having thoughts of suicide or self-harm, call or text 9-8-8 (Canada's Suicide Crisis Helpline) at any time. If you are in immediate danger, call 911.

Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is treated mainly with specialized psychotherapy, such as dialectical behaviour therapy, mentalization-based treatment, schema therapy, and transference-focused psychotherapy. These approaches help people manage intense emotions, reduce self-harm and impulsive behaviour, strengthen relationships, and build a more stable sense of self. Medication is not a primary treatment for BPD itself, but a physician may prescribe it for co-occurring conditions or specific symptoms. One of the most hopeful facts about BPD is that many people improve significantly over time, and research shows that a large proportion no longer meet criteria for the diagnosis after several years.

The Main Treatment Options

Dialectical behaviour therapy. DBT is the most studied therapy for BPD. It balances acceptance and change and teaches skills in mindfulness, distress tolerance, emotion regulation, and interpersonal effectiveness, often through individual therapy, a skills group, and phone coaching.

Mentalization-based treatment. This approach helps people understand their own and others' thoughts and feelings, especially in emotionally charged moments, which can reduce misunderstandings and impulsive reactions.

Schema therapy. Schema therapy addresses deep patterns formed in childhood, such as fears of abandonment or feeling defective, and has good research support for BPD.

Transference-focused psychotherapy. This psychodynamic approach uses the therapy relationship to explore and integrate conflicting views of self and others.

Good psychiatric management and structured clinical care. These more general approaches, which combine education, case management, and supportive therapy, can also be effective and are often more accessible.

Medication. No medication is approved specifically for BPD. A physician or psychiatrist may prescribe medication for depression, anxiety, sleep, or other co-occurring conditions.

How a Treatment Plan Is Chosen

A clinician will explore symptoms, history, safety, co-occurring conditions such as depression, PTSD, eating disorders, or substance use, and personal goals. The condition is described on the page about borderline personality disorder, and the page about emotional intensity and BPD explains a core feature. When self-harm or suicidal behaviour is present, safety and skills are usually the first focus, often through DBT. Access matters too, since comprehensive programs are not available everywhere, and DBT-informed individual therapy or general approaches may be practical alternatives. Personal fit with the therapist is especially important, because a stable, trusting relationship is central to BPD treatment.

What Progress Usually Looks Like

Early progress often involves fewer crises, less self-harm, and a growing ability to pause before reacting to intense emotions. Over months, many people notice more stable relationships, fewer extreme mood shifts, and a clearer sense of who they are. Specialized therapies often run for six months to a year or longer. Setbacks, especially during stress or relationship changes, are common and are part of recovery. Over the longer term, research suggests many people with BPD experience substantial improvement, and some features, such as impulsivity, often ease more quickly than others.

Supporting Your Own Progress

Practising skills between sessions, such as those on the page about DBT skills for emotional regulation, is one of the most helpful things you can do. Tracking emotions and urges can reveal patterns. Keeping regular sleep, meals, and movement reduces emotional vulnerability. Limiting alcohol and drugs helps, since they can intensify emotions and impulsivity. Having a crisis plan and knowing who to contact in difficult moments supports safety. Treating yourself with compassion, rather than shame, makes it easier to keep going.

Common Questions About BPD Treatment

Which therapies have the strongest research behind them for BPD?

Specialized therapies such as DBT, mentalization-based treatment, schema therapy, and transference-focused psychotherapy are effective for BPD.

Why might my psychiatrist prescribe something if no medication treats BPD itself?

No medication targets BPD itself, but a physician may prescribe for co-occurring depression, anxiety, sleep problems, or specific symptoms.

Can people recover from borderline personality disorder?

Yes. Research shows many people with BPD improve significantly over time, and many no longer meet criteria after several years.

What is mentalization-based treatment for BPD?

Mentalization-based treatment helps people with BPD understand their own and others' thoughts and feelings, especially in emotional moments.

Why do specialized BPD programs often last a year?

Learning and practising new emotional and relationship skills takes time, so specialized BPD therapies often run six months to a year or longer.

Why is DBT often recommended for BPD?

DBT is the most studied therapy for BPD and teaches skills for managing emotions, crises, and relationships.

What if I cannot access a full DBT program for BPD?

DBT-informed individual therapy, schema therapy, or general approaches such as good psychiatric management can also help with BPD.

Why does the therapy relationship matter so much in BPD treatment?

A stable, trusting relationship with a therapist is central to BPD treatment and helps people practise new ways of relating.

Which BPD symptoms tend to improve first?

Impulsivity and self-harm often ease earlier in BPD treatment, while emotional sensitivity may take longer to change.

What daily habits make BPD symptoms easier to manage?

Practise DBT skills, track emotions and urges, protect sleep, limit alcohol, keep a crisis plan, and treat yourself with compassion.
