Do I Need a Referral to See a Psychologist

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, you usually do not need a doctor's referral to see a psychologist or counsellor in private practice. You can contact a clinic directly and book an appointment yourself, which is called self-referral. A referral may still be useful, or required, in some situations. Some extended health benefit plans ask for a physician's referral before they reimburse sessions, and seeing a psychiatrist usually requires a referral from a physician. Publicly funded services through health authorities also often need a referral. Checking your benefits plan and asking the clinic are good first steps.

How It Works

  1. Decide what kind of support you want. Therapy, a psychological assessment, or both.
  2. Contact the clinic directly. Many private clinics in BC accept self-referrals through their website, email, or phone.
  3. Check your benefits plan. Find out whether your extended health plan covers psychologists or other providers, how much, and whether a referral is required for reimbursement.
  4. Book an initial appointment. The first session usually involves discussing your concerns and goals, as described on the page about initial consultations.
  5. Involve your doctor if helpful. Even without a required referral, your family doctor can share relevant health information, discuss medication, or coordinate care, with your consent.

Practical Details

Private psychologists and counsellors. Services from psychologists and clinical counsellors in private practice are not covered by MSP, BC's public health insurance. They are paid privately or through extended health benefits, and self-referral is common.

Psychiatrists. Psychiatrists are medical doctors, and their services are usually covered by MSP. They generally require a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner.

Benefits plans. Some plans require a physician's referral or note for psychology sessions to be reimbursed, while others do not. Plans also differ in which providers they cover, so check the details before booking.

Assessments. Private psychological assessments, such as for ADHD or autism, can usually be booked without a referral, though a referral may be needed for publicly funded assessment programs.

Workplace and other programs. Employee assistance programs, WorkSafeBC, ICBC, or other insurers may have their own referral and approval processes.

Fees, coverage, and booking details vary by clinic, so it is worth asking directly. Understanding how long therapy may take, as described on the page about how long therapy usually takes, can help you plan.

How to Prepare

  • Read your benefits plan. Look for which providers are covered, annual limits, and any referral requirements.
  • Write down your concerns and goals. This helps you explain what you are looking for when you contact a clinic.
  • Gather relevant information. Past assessments, medication lists, or reports from other providers can be useful.
  • Ask the clinic questions. For example, about fees, availability, whether they provide receipts for insurance, and whether sessions are virtual or in person.
  • Talk to your doctor if useful. Especially if you are considering medication, need a referral for coverage, or want coordinated care.

Once therapy begins, goals are usually set out in a treatment plan. If you are in crisis and cannot wait for an appointment, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About Referrals to See a Psychologist

Do I need a doctor's referral to see a psychologist in BC?

Usually not. In BC, you can self-refer to a psychologist in private practice by contacting the clinic directly.

Why might my benefits plan ask for a referral to a psychologist?

Some extended health plans require a physician's referral before reimbursing psychologist sessions, so check your plan details.

Is seeing a psychiatrist different from a psychologist when it comes to referrals?

Yes. Psychiatrists are medical doctors usually covered by MSP, and they generally require a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner, unlike private psychologists.

Does MSP cover sessions with a private psychologist?

No. Private psychologist sessions in BC are not covered by MSP and are paid privately or through extended health benefits.

Can I book an ADHD or autism assessment without a referral?

Private ADHD and autism assessments can usually be booked without a referral, though publicly funded programs may require one.

Should I tell my family doctor I am seeing a psychologist?

It can help. With your consent, your doctor and psychologist can coordinate care, especially if medication is involved, even without a referral.

What does self-referral to a psychologist mean?

Self-referral means contacting a psychologist or clinic directly to book an appointment, without a doctor's referral.

What should I ask a clinic before my first psychologist appointment?

Ask about fees, availability, insurance receipts, referral requirements, and whether sessions with the psychologist are virtual or in person.

Do employee assistance programs need a referral?

Employee assistance programs and some insurers have their own referral and approval processes, so check with the program directly.

What if I need help before I can see a psychologist?

If you are in crisis before you can see a psychologist, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.
