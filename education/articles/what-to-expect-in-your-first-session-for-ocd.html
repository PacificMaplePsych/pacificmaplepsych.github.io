What to Expect in Your First Session for OCD

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In your first therapy session for OCD, the therapist will usually ask about your intrusive thoughts or obsessions, the compulsions or mental rituals you use to reduce distress, what you avoid, how much time OCD takes up, and how it affects your daily life. They may use a structured questionnaire to measure severity. They will also explain what OCD is, why compulsions keep it going, and how treatment such as exposure and response prevention (ERP) works. You will not be asked to start exposures in the first session. A therapist experienced with OCD will not be shocked by the content of your thoughts, however disturbing they feel to you.

What to Expect

  1. Getting to know you. Your background, health, current stress, and goals.
  2. Understanding your obsessions. The themes of your intrusive thoughts, such as contamination, harm, relationships, morality, or symmetry. The page about intrusive thoughts versus OCD explains the difference.
  3. Identifying compulsions. Visible behaviours like checking or washing, and hidden ones like reassurance seeking, reviewing, or mental compulsions.
  4. Measuring impact. Time spent, distress, avoidance, and effects on work, relationships, and self-care. A tool such as the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale may be used.
  5. Screening for related concerns. Depression, anxiety, tics, hoarding, body-focused repetitive behaviours, or other conditions.
  6. Explaining treatment. How ERP works and what the next sessions might involve.

Practical Details

Treatment approach. ERP, a form of cognitive behavioural therapy, is the leading psychological treatment for OCD, as explained on the page about ERP for OCD. Some approaches also use acceptance and commitment therapy or cognitive work.

Pace. Exposure work starts gradually, usually after a few sessions of assessment and planning. You help decide what to work on and when.

Medication. Certain antidepressants can help with OCD and are prescribed by a physician or psychiatrist. Therapy and medication are often combined, as described on the page about how OCD is treated.

Frequency. Weekly sessions are common at first, and some people benefit from more frequent or intensive ERP.

Virtual sessions. ERP can work well virtually, and sometimes being at home makes it easier to practise exposures in real situations.

Choosing a therapist. Look for someone with specific OCD and ERP training, as explained on the page about finding an OCD-informed therapist.

How to Prepare

  • Make a list of obsessions and compulsions. Include mental rituals and things you avoid.
  • Estimate the time OCD takes. A rough daily figure is helpful.
  • Write things down if saying them feels hard. You can hand your therapist a note or read from it.
  • Notice reassurance seeking. Including asking others, searching online, or checking your feelings.
  • Think about goals. What would you do with more time and less fear?
  • Expect some anxiety. Talking about OCD can trigger urges to seek reassurance during the session, which is normal.

If OCD is accompanied by thoughts of suicide or hopelessness, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About the First OCD Therapy Session

What does an OCD therapist cover at the first appointment?

In the first OCD session, the therapist asks about obsessions, compulsions, avoidance, and impact, may measure severity, and explains how treatment works.

Will I start exposures in my first OCD session?

No. Exposure work for OCD usually begins after a few sessions of assessment and planning, and it starts gradually.

Do OCD specialists judge disturbing or taboo thoughts?

No. A therapist experienced with OCD has heard many types of intrusive thoughts and understands they do not reflect your values or intentions.

What is the Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale?

The Yale-Brown Obsessive Compulsive Scale is a structured tool clinicians use to measure the severity of OCD symptoms.

What if it feels too hard to say my intrusive thoughts out loud?

You can write intrusive thoughts down and hand the note to your therapist or read from it. Many people with OCD do this at first.

Should I tell my therapist about mental compulsions?

Yes. Mental compulsions, such as reviewing or neutralizing thoughts, are an important part of OCD to share with your therapist.

Can ERP for OCD be done virtually?

Yes. ERP for OCD can work well virtually, and being at home can make it easier to practise exposures in real situations.

How often are OCD therapy sessions at the start?

OCD therapy sessions are commonly weekly at the start, and some people benefit from more frequent or intensive ERP.

Is it normal to want reassurance during an OCD session?

Yes. Talking about OCD can trigger urges to seek reassurance during a session, and your therapist will help you notice and manage this.

How should I prepare for my first OCD appointment?

List your obsessions, compulsions, and avoidance, estimate how much time OCD takes, and think about your goals before your first OCD appointment.
