Understanding Relationship Anxiety

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Relationship anxiety is persistent worry, doubt, or insecurity about a romantic relationship, even when things are going well. People with relationship anxiety may worry that their partner will leave, question whether they are truly loved, or wonder whether the relationship is right. Some uncertainty is a normal part of getting close to someone. Relationship anxiety becomes a problem when worries are frequent, intense, and affect wellbeing or the relationship itself. It is common, understandable, and something that can ease with awareness and support.

What Relationship Anxiety Looks Like

  • Frequently wondering whether your partner really loves you.
  • Feeling anxious when your partner does not text back quickly.
  • Seeking constant reassurance about their feelings.
  • Reading hidden meaning into small changes in tone or behaviour.
  • Fearing that you are not good enough for your partner.
  • Doubting whether you love your partner enough, or whether they are the right person.
  • Struggling to trust your partner without a clear reason.
  • Pulling back or testing the relationship to protect yourself from hurt.
  • Difficulty enjoying good moments because you are waiting for something to go wrong.

Why It Happens

Relationship anxiety often has roots in attachment patterns. People with anxious attachment may have learned early that love can be unpredictable, making them more alert to signs of rejection. Past relationships involving betrayal, infidelity, or abrupt endings can make it harder to feel safe. Low self-esteem can lead people to believe they are unlovable or that a partner will eventually lose interest. General anxiety can also focus on relationships, since they are important and involve uncertainty.

Relationship dynamics play a role too. Inconsistent communication, unresolved conflict, or a partner who is emotionally distant can understandably increase anxiety. In some cases, persistent intrusive doubts about the relationship, with urges to check feelings or seek certainty, may reflect relationship-focused OCD, which responds to specific treatment.

Relationship anxiety can create a cycle. Worry leads to reassurance-seeking, which brings brief relief but reinforces the need for reassurance. A partner may feel pressured or frustrated, which can increase distance and, in turn, anxiety. Recognizing this cycle can help both partners respond differently.

Relationship anxiety can also appear in the early stages of dating, when uncertainty is naturally high. Some people notice it only once a relationship becomes serious, as closeness raises the stakes. Others feel it most during transitions, such as moving in together, long-distance periods, or after an argument. Noticing when anxiety spikes can help you plan support for those moments.

What It Is Not

  • Not proof that the relationship is wrong. Anxiety can occur in healthy, loving relationships.
  • Not a character flaw. It is often shaped by past experiences and attachment.
  • Not something a partner can fix alone with reassurance. Lasting change usually comes from within.
  • Not the same as justified concern. Clear signs of disrespect, dishonesty, or control deserve attention in their own right.

How It Is Addressed and Supported

Noticing anxious thoughts and naming them as anxiety, rather than facts, is a helpful starting point. Reducing reassurance-seeking gradually, and tolerating the discomfort of uncertainty, can weaken the cycle over time. Building a sense of worth outside the relationship, through friendships, interests, and goals, reduces pressure on it. Clear, calm communication about needs helps partners understand each other. Individual therapy can explore attachment patterns, past experiences, and self-esteem, using approaches such as cognitive behavioural and attachment-focused therapy. Couples therapy, including emotionally focused therapy, can help partners build security and respond to each other's needs.

Common Questions About Relationship Anxiety

Why do I keep doubting my partner's feelings when things are going well?

Relationship anxiety can persist even in healthy relationships, often shaped by attachment patterns or past hurt.

How common is anxiety about romantic relationships?

Very common. Many people experience some relationship anxiety, especially early on or after past hurt.

Does relationship anxiety mean I am with the wrong person?

Not necessarily. Anxiety can occur in loving, healthy relationships and does not by itself mean something is wrong.

Why does a partner's silence feel so threatening to me?

When the brain is primed for rejection, uncertainty such as a slow reply can trigger strong fear of being left.

How is relationship anxiety linked with anxious attachment?

People with anxious attachment are more alert to signs of rejection, which can fuel ongoing relationship worries.

What if my doubts about the relationship feel intrusive and constant?

Persistent, distressing doubts with urges to check feelings may reflect relationship-focused OCD, which a clinician can assess.

How can my partner support me without endless reassurance?

A partner can offer warmth and consistency while supporting you to tolerate uncertainty rather than answering every worry.

How can I calm relationship worries in the moment?

Name the thought as anxiety, pause before seeking reassurance, breathe slowly, and focus on something present.

Can past betrayal cause relationship anxiety?

Yes. Infidelity or painful breakups can make it harder to feel safe and trust a new partner.

Does couples therapy help with relationship anxiety?

Yes. Approaches such as emotionally focused therapy help partners build security and respond to each other's needs.
