2SLGBTQ+-Affirming Therapy: What It Means

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

2SLGBTQ+-affirming therapy is an approach in which the therapist recognizes Two-Spirit, lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, and other sexual and gender identities as natural and valid, and works on the client's own goals rather than trying to change who they are. Affirming therapists understand the effects of stigma and discrimination, use correct names and pronouns, do not assume a person's identity is the cause of their difficulties, and are knowledgeable about issues such as coming out, family acceptance, and minority stress. Many 2SLGBTQ+ people come to therapy for the same reasons as anyone else, such as anxiety or relationships, and want a therapist they do not have to educate or defend themselves to.

What Affirming Therapy Looks Like

  • Respect for identity. Sexual orientation and gender identity are treated as valid, not as problems to fix.
  • Correct language. The therapist uses your name and pronouns and asks rather than assumes.
  • Understanding context. The therapist understands how stigma, discrimination, and family or community reactions can affect wellbeing, as described on the page about minority stress.
  • Your goals lead. Therapy focuses on what you want to work on, which may or may not relate to identity.
  • Knowledge and humility. The therapist is informed about 2SLGBTQ+ experiences while recognizing you are the expert on your own life.
  • Inclusive relationship work. Couples and family therapy respect diverse relationship structures and family forms.

Common Reasons People Seek Affirming Therapy

  • Anxiety, depression, and stress
  • Coming out or exploring identity, as described on the page about anxiety about coming out
  • Family rejection or conflict
  • Experiences of discrimination, harassment, or violence
  • Relationship concerns
  • Gender identity exploration and support
  • Religious or cultural conflict, as described on the page about religious trauma

Why It Matters

2SLGBTQ+ people experience higher rates of anxiety, depression, and suicidal thoughts, largely because of stigma, rejection, and discrimination rather than identity itself. Supportive, affirming relationships, including with a therapist, are associated with better mental health. Past negative experiences in health care can make people hesitant to seek help, so feeling safe with a therapist is especially important.

Questions to Ask a Potential Therapist

Many people find it helpful to ask a few questions during an initial consultation, such as: What experience do you have working with 2SLGBTQ+ clients? How do you approach identity in therapy? How would you respond if my family does not accept who I am? Do you work with diverse relationship structures in couples therapy? A good therapist will welcome these questions and answer openly. It is also fine to notice how you feel during the conversation. Feeling respected, understood, and not judged is an important sign that the therapist may be a good fit.

What It Is Not

Affirming therapy is not conversion therapy. Practices that try to change a person's sexual orientation or gender identity, or to repress gender expression, are harmful and have been prohibited under the Criminal Code of Canada since 2022. Affirming therapy is also not about pushing any particular identity or decision. It supports people in exploring and understanding themselves at their own pace.

Finding an Affirming Therapist

  • Look for therapists who state that they are affirming or have experience with 2SLGBTQ+ clients.
  • Ask questions during a consultation, such as how they approach identity and what experience they have.
  • Notice how you feel. You should feel respected and not need to justify who you are.

Common Questions About 2SLGBTQ+-Affirming Therapy

What is 2SLGBTQ+-affirming therapy?

2SLGBTQ+-affirming therapy recognizes diverse sexual orientations and gender identities as valid and focuses on the client's goals rather than changing who they are.

Which identities are included in the acronym 2SLGBTQ+?

2SLGBTQ+ stands for Two-Spirit, lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, queer, and other sexual and gender identities.

How do I find an affirming therapist?

Look for therapists who describe themselves as affirming, ask questions during a consultation, and notice whether you feel respected.

Is conversion therapy legal in Canada?

No. Conversion therapy has been prohibited under the Criminal Code of Canada since 2022.

Do I have to talk about my identity in affirming therapy?

No. Affirming therapy follows your goals, which may or may not relate to your identity.

Why do 2SLGBTQ+ people have higher rates of mental health concerns?

Higher rates are largely linked to stigma, rejection, and discrimination, often called minority stress, rather than identity itself.

How does an affirming therapist address minority stress?

An affirming therapist recognizes how stigma and discrimination affect wellbeing and helps clients cope, build support, and work through internalized shame.

Can affirming therapy help with coming out?

Yes. Affirming therapy can help people explore identity, plan for coming out, and cope with family or community reactions.

Is affirming couples therapy available?

Yes. Affirming couples therapy respects diverse relationships and family forms.

Does affirming therapy push a particular identity?

No. Affirming therapy supports people in exploring and understanding themselves at their own pace, without pushing any identity.
