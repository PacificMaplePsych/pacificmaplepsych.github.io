Individual Therapy vs. Coaching for Burnout

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Individual therapy for burnout is provided by a regulated mental health professional who can assess for conditions such as depression or anxiety, address underlying patterns like perfectionism or people-pleasing, and support recovery when exhaustion has affected your health and functioning. Coaching is usually more goal- and future-focused, helping with career direction, workload strategies, and work habits, and coaches are not regulated as health professionals in BC. Therapy is generally the better fit when burnout comes with low mood, anxiety, sleep problems, or difficulty functioning. Coaching may suit someone who is generally well and wants practical guidance to prevent burnout or make career changes.

How Each Works

Individual therapy. A therapist explores how burnout developed, including workload, values conflicts, boundaries, and personal patterns. Therapy may use CBT, ACT, compassion-focused work, and nervous system regulation strategies. It can also screen for and treat depression, anxiety, trauma, or ADHD that may be contributing. The page about burnout versus depression explains why this matters.

Coaching. A coach helps you set goals, develop action plans, improve time management, navigate career decisions, and hold yourself accountable. Coaching typically focuses on the present and future rather than on emotional history or mental health symptoms.

Where they overlap. Both can help with boundaries, priorities, and sustainable work habits, as covered on the page about preventing burnout. A similar distinction applies in ADHD, described on the page about ADHD coaching versus therapy.

Practical Details

Training and regulation. Psychologists, registered clinical counsellors, and other regulated therapists have formal training and professional oversight. Coaching is not a regulated profession, so training varies widely. Some coaches hold voluntary certifications.

Coverage. Extended health plans often cover therapy with eligible providers such as psychologists or counsellors. Coaching is rarely covered by health benefits, though some employers fund it separately.

Medical documentation. If you need time off work or a return-to-work plan, a physician typically provides medical notes. Therapists can support the process and, depending on their role, may provide information with your consent.

Confidentiality. Regulated therapists follow professional confidentiality standards. If an employer arranges coaching, clarify what, if anything, is reported back.

Using both. Some people start with therapy to recover, then use coaching later for career planning.

How to Decide

  • Choose therapy if you feel persistently low, anxious, numb, or hopeless, are struggling to function, have sleep or health problems, or notice deep patterns driving overwork.
  • Consider coaching if you are generally well, want practical strategies and accountability, or are planning a career change.
  • Think about what has not worked. If practical strategies alone have not helped, deeper therapeutic work may be needed.
  • Talk to your doctor if symptoms are affecting your health or you may need time off. The page about time off versus therapy explores this.
  • Ask about qualifications. Whichever you choose, ask about training, approach, and experience with burnout.

If burnout has led to thoughts of suicide or hopelessness, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About Therapy and Coaching for Burnout

How do a burnout coach and a therapist differ?

Therapy for burnout is provided by regulated professionals who can address mental health and underlying patterns. Coaching focuses on goals, strategies, and career direction.

Is therapy or coaching better for burnout recovery?

Therapy is usually better when burnout comes with low mood, anxiety, sleep problems, or trouble functioning. Coaching may suit people who are generally well.

Are burnout coaches regulated in BC?

No. Coaching is not a regulated health profession in BC, so a burnout coach's training varies. Some hold voluntary certifications.

Do benefits plans cover coaching for burnout?

Coaching for burnout is rarely covered by health benefits, though some employers fund it. Therapy with eligible providers is more often covered.

Can a therapist tell if my burnout is actually depression?

Yes. A therapist can assess whether burnout symptoms overlap with depression or anxiety, which helps guide the right treatment.

Can I use both therapy and coaching for burnout?

Yes. Some people start with therapy to recover from burnout and later use coaching for career planning and work habits.

What does a burnout coach typically help with?

A burnout coach typically helps with goal setting, time management, workload strategies, boundaries, and career decisions.

What topics come up in burnout therapy sessions?

Therapy for burnout explores how it developed, addresses patterns such as perfectionism, treats related symptoms, and builds recovery strategies.

Who provides medical notes for time off due to burnout?

A physician usually provides medical notes for time off due to burnout. A therapist can support the process with your consent.

Is employer-arranged burnout coaching confidential?

Ask before starting. With employer-arranged burnout coaching, clarify what information, if any, is shared with your workplace.
