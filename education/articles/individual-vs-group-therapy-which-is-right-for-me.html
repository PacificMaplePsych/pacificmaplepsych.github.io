Individual vs. Group Therapy: Which Is Right for Me

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Individual therapy is one-on-one work with a therapist, tailored entirely to your concerns, pace, and goals. Group therapy brings together several people, usually with a shared concern, led by one or more trained facilitators. Both can be effective, and research shows group therapy works as well as individual therapy for many concerns, such as depression, anxiety, grief, and skills-based programs like DBT. Individual therapy may suit you better if you need privacy, have complex or sensitive concerns, or want a fully personalized approach. Group therapy may suit you if you would benefit from feeling less alone, learning from others, practising social skills, or a lower-cost option. Many people use both.

How Each Works

Individual therapy. Sessions are usually 50 to 60 minutes, focused entirely on you. The therapist adapts the approach to your history, needs, and progress.

Group therapy. Groups often meet weekly for 60 to 120 minutes with around 6 to 12 members. Some are structured and skills-based, such as DBT skills groups or CBT groups for anxiety. Others are process-oriented and focus on sharing experiences and relating to one another. Some groups run for a fixed number of weeks, while others are ongoing. The page about group therapy for grief describes one example.

Combined care. Some treatments, such as comprehensive DBT, intentionally combine individual sessions with a skills group.

Practical Details

Privacy. In individual therapy, confidentiality is between you and the therapist, within legal limits. In groups, members agree to keep what is shared confidential, but the facilitator cannot fully guarantee others will. You choose how much to share.

Cost. Group therapy is often less expensive per session than individual therapy. Coverage under extended health plans varies, so check your plan.

Availability. Groups start on set dates and may have waitlists. Individual therapy can usually begin when a therapist has an opening.

Screening. Group facilitators usually meet with potential members beforehand to make sure the group is a good fit.

Virtual options. Both individual and group therapy may be offered virtually, as discussed on the page about virtual versus in-person therapy.

Public programs. Some health authority programs in BC offer group-based treatment, often with a referral.

How to Decide

  • Consider individual therapy if your concerns are complex, sensitive, or involve trauma you are not ready to share with others, or if you want a fully personalized pace.
  • Consider group therapy if you feel isolated, would benefit from hearing others' experiences, want to practise skills with people, or are looking for a lower-cost option.
  • Consider both if you want personalized support plus the connection and practice a group offers.
  • Think about your comfort level. Social anxiety can make groups feel daunting, though groups can also help with it over time.
  • Ask about the specific group. Its focus, size, structure, length, and facilitator training.

Common Questions About Individual and Group Therapy

How does one-on-one counselling compare with a therapy group?

Individual therapy is one-on-one with a therapist, while group therapy brings several people together with a facilitator, often around a shared concern.

Is group therapy as effective as individual therapy?

Research shows group therapy works as well as individual therapy for many concerns, including depression, anxiety, grief, and skills-based programs.

Which people tend to benefit most from joining a therapy group?

Group therapy can be a good fit for people who feel isolated, want to learn from others, practise social skills, or need a lower-cost option.

When is individual therapy better than group therapy?

Individual therapy may be better for complex or sensitive concerns, trauma you are not ready to share, or when you want a fully personalized pace.

Is group therapy confidential?

Group members agree to keep what is shared confidential, but a facilitator cannot fully guarantee it, so you choose how much to share in group therapy.

How many people are usually in a therapy group?

Therapy groups usually have around 6 to 12 members, meeting weekly for 60 to 120 minutes.

Do group sessions usually cost less per session?

Group therapy is often less expensive per session than individual therapy, though extended health coverage for groups varies.

Can I do individual and group therapy at the same time?

Yes. Many people combine individual and group therapy, and some treatments, like comprehensive DBT, are designed to include both.

What if I have social anxiety and feel nervous about a group?

Social anxiety can make a group feel daunting, but groups can also help with it. Talk with the facilitator or start with individual therapy.

What should I ask before joining a therapy group?

Before joining a group, ask about its focus, size, structure, length, cost, and the facilitator's training.
