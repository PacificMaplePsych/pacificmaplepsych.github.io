What an Autism Assessment Appointment Looks Like

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

An autism assessment appointment usually involves a detailed conversation about your development, social experiences, communication, sensory sensitivities, routines, and interests, along with questionnaires and sometimes structured activities that let the clinician observe social communication. For adults, much of the assessment focuses on your history and current life, often with input from a parent or someone who knew you as a child. Appointments are usually spread over several sessions rather than one long day. A good assessment is respectful and collaborative, and you can ask for breaks or adjustments to lighting, noise, or communication style.

What to Expect

Clinical interview. The clinician asks about early development, childhood friendships, school, family, work, relationships, communication preferences, sensory experiences, routines, special interests, and how you cope in social situations. They will also ask about masking, which is explained on the page about autism masking.

Developmental history. With your consent, a parent, sibling, or long-time friend may be interviewed or asked to fill out questionnaires about what you were like as a child.

Standardized questionnaires. These ask about social communication, sensory processing, and related experiences.

Structured observation. Some assessments include semi-structured activities and conversation designed to observe communication and social interaction. These are not tests of intelligence and there are no wrong answers.

Other areas. Depending on your needs, the clinician may assess ADHD, anxiety, depression, or cognitive abilities, since these often occur alongside autism.

Feedback. After all appointments, the clinician reviews the findings with you in a feedback session and provides a written report.

Practical Details

Number of appointments. Assessments typically take several appointments over a few weeks. Individual sessions may last one to a few hours.

Virtual or in person. Interviews can often be done virtually. Some observation activities work best in person, depending on the measures used.

Sensory and communication needs. You can ask in advance for dimmer lighting, fewer interruptions, written questions, extra processing time, or breaks. You can bring fidget items, sunglasses, or headphones.

Support person. Some people bring a support person to parts of the assessment. Ask the clinic.

Fees. Private autism assessments are not covered by MSP. Ask the clinic about fees and what is included.

Age. Adults of any age can be assessed, as discussed on the page about autism assessment for adults.

How to Prepare

  • Complete intake forms carefully. They help the clinician plan.
  • Write down examples. Social, sensory, and communication experiences from childhood and adulthood.
  • Ask a family member for help. Someone who knew you as a child may remember useful details.
  • Gather records. School report cards and any previous assessments.
  • Request accommodations. Let the clinic know what helps you feel comfortable.
  • Plan recovery time. Assessments can be tiring, so schedule some rest afterward.

More preparation tips are on the page about preparing for an ADHD or autism assessment. What clinicians look for is described on the page about how autism is diagnosed in adults.

Common Questions About Autism Assessment Appointments

What happens during an autism assessment appointment?

An autism assessment appointment usually includes a detailed interview, questionnaires, and sometimes structured activities to observe social communication.

How many appointments does an autism assessment take?

An autism assessment typically takes several appointments over a few weeks, with individual sessions lasting one to a few hours.

Will my parents be involved in my adult autism assessment?

With your consent, a parent or someone who knew you as a child may share developmental history for your adult autism assessment.

What are the structured activities in an autism assessment?

Structured activities in an autism assessment are conversations and tasks designed to observe communication and social interaction. There are no wrong answers.

Can I ask for sensory accommodations during an autism assessment?

Yes. You can request dimmer lighting, breaks, written questions, or extra processing time, and bring headphones or fidget items to an autism assessment.

Which autism assessment activities are usually done face to face?

Some structured observation activities in an autism assessment work best face to face, while interviews can often be done by video.

Why does an autism assessment ask about masking?

Many autistic adults mask or camouflage their traits, so asking about masking helps the clinician understand what may be hidden in an autism assessment.

Does an autism assessment test intelligence?

Not necessarily. An autism assessment focuses on social communication and related traits, though cognitive testing may be added if useful.

Can I bring a support person to my autism assessment?

Some clinics allow a support person for parts of an autism assessment. Ask the clinic before your appointment.

Is an autism assessment appointment tiring?

An autism assessment can be tiring, so it helps to plan rest afterward and ask for breaks during the appointment.
