Finding an Eating-Disorder-Informed Therapist

If you are having thoughts of suicide, call or text 9-8-8 (Canada's Suicide Crisis Helpline) at any time. Eating disorders can cause serious medical complications. If you feel faint, have chest pain, or feel very unwell, seek medical care or call 911. In British Columbia, you can call 8-1-1 to speak with a nurse.

An eating disorder-informed therapist is a mental health professional with specific training and experience in treating eating disorders and disordered eating. They understand the medical risks, use evidence-based approaches, work alongside doctors and dietitians, and avoid messages that reinforce diet culture. Finding the right therapist can make a real difference, because eating disorders are complex, affect both body and mind, and can be misunderstood by providers without this background. Looking for someone with the right experience, asking a few key questions, and making sure medical care is in place are important steps toward recovery.

Why Eating Disorder Experience Matters

Eating disorders, including anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, binge eating disorder, and other patterns, involve thoughts, behaviours, and physical effects that interact in complicated ways. Well-meaning providers without eating disorder training may miss warning signs, focus only on weight, or give advice that unintentionally feeds the disorder, such as praising weight loss or recommending restrictive diets.

Eating disorder-informed therapists understand that eating disorders occur in people of all body sizes, that medical monitoring is often needed, and that recovery involves both behaviours and underlying emotions. They use approaches with research support, such as cognitive behavioural therapy adapted for eating disorders, family-based treatment for young people, and dialectical behaviour therapy skills. They also recognize how diet culture shapes beliefs about food and bodies.

What to Look For

Relevant Training and Experience

Look for therapists who describe specific training or experience with eating disorders, not just general interest. Many list this in their profiles.

A Team Approach

Good eating disorder care usually involves a team, including a family doctor or other physician for medical monitoring and often a registered dietitian. An informed therapist will ask about medical care and coordinate with other providers where you agree.

A Weight-Inclusive, Non-Judgmental Stance

Look for a therapist who treats people of all sizes with respect, avoids focusing on weight as the only measure of health, and does not promote dieting.

Evidence-Based Methods

Ask which approaches they use and why. A good fit may combine structured methods with attention to emotions, relationships, and identity.

Questions to Ask

  • What experience do you have treating eating disorders?
  • Which approaches do you use?
  • How do you work with doctors and dietitians?
  • How do you decide whether someone needs more intensive care?
  • How do you involve family members, if at all?

Feeling comfortable with a therapist also matters. It is fine to meet with a few before deciding.

Common Obstacles

Specialized therapists can be harder to find, and wait times or costs may be barriers. Virtual therapy can widen your options within BC. Your family doctor can help with referrals, including to publicly funded eating disorder programs, which in BC are accessed through health authorities and may have waitlists. Some people feel unsure whether their struggles are serious enough to seek specialized help. Disordered eating that causes distress deserves attention, whether or not it fits a specific diagnosis, as described on the page about disordered eating patterns. Ambivalence about recovery is also common and is something an informed therapist expects and can work with.

When More Intensive Care Is Needed

Outpatient therapy is not always enough. Signs that a higher level of care may be needed include rapid weight change, fainting or dizziness, heart palpitations, frequent vomiting or laxative use, being unable to eat enough to stay medically stable, or thoughts of suicide. Intensive programs, day treatment, or hospital care may be recommended. A physician should assess medical stability, and an informed therapist can help you understand options.

Common Questions About Finding an Eating Disorder Therapist

What makes a therapist eating disorder-informed?

They have specific training and experience with eating disorders, understand medical risks, and use evidence-based approaches.

Why should I look for an eating disorder specialist instead of any therapist?

Providers without this training may miss warning signs or give advice that unintentionally reinforces the disorder.

What questions should I ask a therapist about eating disorder experience?

Ask about their training, the approaches they use, how they work with doctors and dietitians, and when they recommend more intensive care.

Do I need a doctor involved as well as a therapist for an eating disorder?

Yes. Medical monitoring is often needed, and good care usually involves a physician and often a dietitian.

What does a weight-inclusive therapist mean?

It means treating people of all sizes with respect and not using weight as the only measure of health.

Are my eating struggles serious enough to see a specialist?

Disordered eating that causes distress deserves attention, whether or not it fits a specific diagnosis.

How do I access publicly funded eating disorder care in BC?

Your family doctor can refer you to health authority eating disorder programs, which may have waitlists.

Can I see an eating disorder therapist virtually?

Yes. Virtual therapy can widen your options, often alongside in-person medical care.

Can I see an eating disorder therapist if I am not sure I want to recover yet?

Mixed feelings about recovery are common, and an informed therapist expects and works with them.

When is outpatient therapy not enough for an eating disorder?

Signs such as fainting, rapid weight change, frequent purging, or medical instability may mean more intensive care is needed.
