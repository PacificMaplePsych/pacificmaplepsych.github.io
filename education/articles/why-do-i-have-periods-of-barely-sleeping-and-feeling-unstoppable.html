Why Do I Have Periods of Barely Sleeping and Feeling Unstoppable

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

For a few days or weeks, something shifts. You sleep three or four hours and wake up feeling fine, even great. Ideas come quickly, you talk faster, you start several projects at once, and you feel more confident and capable than usual. Other people might say you seem wired or different. Then, often, the energy fades, and sometimes a low period follows.

Many people enjoy these stretches and do not see them as a problem. But a clear pattern of needing much less sleep while feeling energized is worth understanding, because it can be a sign of something that benefits from proper assessment.

Seek help right away by calling 911 or going to your nearest emergency department if you or someone close to you notices hearing or seeing things others do not, beliefs that seem out of touch with reality, extreme risk-taking, or thoughts of harming yourself or others. You can also call or text 9-8-8 at any time in Canada.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Excitement or a big deadline. A new relationship, an exciting project, or a crunch at work can temporarily reduce sleep and raise energy. In these cases the change is usually tied to the situation and eases once it passes.

Stimulants and substances. High doses of caffeine, energy drinks, stimulant medications, cocaine, amphetamines, and some other substances can cause reduced sleep, racing thoughts, and a sense of invincibility.

Medications. Corticosteroids, some decongestants, and occasionally antidepressants can trigger periods of high energy and poor sleep in some people. Any sudden change after starting a medication is worth raising with the prescriber.

Thyroid problems. An overactive thyroid can cause restlessness, reduced sleep, a racing heart, and feeling revved up.

Anxiety. Anxiety can also keep people up at night, but the difference is important. With anxiety-related insomnia, you usually feel exhausted and tense. With the pattern described here, you feel energized despite little sleep, and you may be restless without feeling tired.

When It Looks More Like Bipolar Disorder

A distinct period of elevated or irritable mood, with a reduced need for sleep and increased energy, is a core feature of hypomania and mania, the "up" phases of bipolar disorder. The key phrase is reduced need for sleep: not struggling to sleep, but simply not feeling the need.

Other features that often come along with it include:

  • Talking more or faster than usual, and thoughts that jump quickly from idea to idea.
  • Inflated confidence, big plans, or feeling unusually important or gifted.
  • Starting many projects or activities at once.
  • Risky or impulsive choices, such as spending sprees or sudden decisions, as described in Why Do I Make Impulsive Decisions I Later Regret.
  • A noticeable change that other people comment on.

Hypomania is milder and may even feel productive, while mania is more severe and can lead to serious consequences or psychosis. Both are typically followed or preceded by periods of depression, which is why the overall pattern of dramatic mood swings matters. Bipolar II disorder, which involves hypomania and depression, is often missed for years because people seek help only during the low periods.

What Can Help

  • Track sleep and mood. A simple daily log of hours slept, energy, and mood over several weeks is one of the most useful things to bring to a doctor or psychologist.
  • Ask someone you trust. Friends and family often notice changes before you do. Their observations can help clarify the pattern.
  • Protect sleep anyway. Even if you do not feel tired, keeping a regular bedtime and limiting stimulants can help prevent an escalation.
  • Delay big decisions. During high-energy periods, wait before making large purchases, quitting jobs, or other major commitments.
  • See a doctor. A medical check can rule out thyroid and medication effects, and a psychiatrist can advise on treatment if bipolar disorder is suspected.

Common Questions About High-Energy Periods With Little Sleep

What does reduced need for sleep mean?

Reduced need for sleep means feeling rested and energized after much less sleep than usual, rather than struggling to fall asleep and feeling exhausted. It is a key feature that helps distinguish hypomania or mania from ordinary insomnia.

Is sleeping very little and feeling great a sign of bipolar disorder?

It can be, especially when it comes with elevated or irritable mood, racing thoughts, increased activity, and changes others notice. Other causes such as stimulants, medications, and thyroid problems should also be considered.

How does hypomania compare to full mania?

Hypomania is a milder elevated state that lasts at least several days and is noticeable to others but does not cause severe problems. Mania is more intense, lasts longer, can seriously disrupt life, and may involve psychosis or require hospital care.

How is this different from anxiety keeping me awake?

With anxiety-related insomnia, people usually feel tired, tense, and worried. During hypomania or mania, people often feel energized, confident, and unusually productive despite very little sleep.

Can stimulants or energy drinks cause high-energy periods with little sleep?

Yes. Large amounts of caffeine, energy drinks, prescribed stimulants, and recreational drugs can all reduce sleep and create a revved-up feeling. Tracking intake alongside sleep can help clarify whether substances are involved.

Can antidepressants cause periods of high energy?

In some people, particularly those with an undiagnosed tendency toward bipolar disorder, antidepressants can trigger hypomanic or manic symptoms. Any sudden high-energy period after starting or changing medication should be reported to the prescriber.

Why is bipolar II disorder often missed?

People with bipolar II usually seek help during depressive episodes, and hypomanic periods can feel like simply having a good or productive stretch. Without asking specifically about these periods, a diagnosis of depression alone may be given.

What should I track if I think I have high-energy episodes?

Daily hours of sleep, energy level, mood, spending, and any unusual activity or impulsive decisions are all helpful. Noting what others say about changes in you can also be valuable.

When is a high-energy period an emergency?

Seek immediate help if there are signs of psychosis such as hearing or seeing things others do not or beliefs out of touch with reality, if behaviour is extremely risky, or if there are thoughts of harming yourself or others.

Who diagnoses bipolar disorder?

Psychologists and psychiatrists can both assess and diagnose bipolar disorder. Medication, which is often an important part of treatment, is prescribed by a psychiatrist or family doctor, while therapy supports routines, early warning signs, and coping.
