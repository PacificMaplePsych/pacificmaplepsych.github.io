Private vs. Public ADHD and Autism Assessment in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, publicly funded ADHD and autism assessments are available in some situations, such as autism assessments for children and youth through the provincial assessment network, and psychiatric assessments covered by MSP with a referral. Public options are free at the point of care but can involve long waits, and options for adults are limited. Private assessments with a psychologist can usually be booked without a referral and are often available sooner, but they are paid privately or partly through extended health benefits. Both can lead to a valid diagnosis when completed by a qualified professional. The best choice depends on age, urgency, cost, and what you need the assessment for.

How It Works

Public assessments for children and youth. Autism assessments for young people are offered through the provincial network, usually with a physician's referral. Children may also be assessed for ADHD by a paediatrician or child psychiatrist, and schools may provide some psychoeducational testing, though availability varies.

Public assessments for adults. A family doctor can refer to a psychiatrist, who may assess ADHD and sometimes autism. Dedicated public adult autism assessment services are limited, and waits can be long.

Private assessments. Registered psychologists in private practice offer ADHD, autism, and combined assessments. These typically include interviews, questionnaires, standardized measures, a written report, and feedback, as described on the page about psychological assessments.

Practical Details

Cost. Public assessments are covered by provincial health insurance. Private assessments are not covered by MSP, and fees vary by clinic and complexity. Some extended health plans cover part of the cost, and psychological services may qualify for the medical expense tax credit. Check current CRA rules.

Wait times. Public waits can be lengthy, while private clinics often have shorter waits, though this varies.

Referrals. Public assessments generally need a referral. Private assessments usually do not, as explained on the page about referrals for ADHD and autism assessments.

Depth and format. Private psychological assessments often include detailed testing and a comprehensive report. Psychiatric assessments may be shorter and more focused on diagnosis and medication.

Recognition. A diagnosis from a qualified professional, whether public or private, is generally accepted for purposes such as accommodations and, for children, provincial autism funding. Confirm specific requirements with the organization involved.

Medication. After a private ADHD diagnosis, you will need a physician or nurse practitioner to prescribe medication if you choose it.

How to Decide

  • Consider urgency. If difficulties are significantly affecting school, work, or wellbeing, a shorter wait may matter.
  • Consider cost. Check your benefits coverage and any health spending account.
  • Consider your purpose. Accommodations, funding, medication, or self-understanding may each have different requirements.
  • Talk to your doctor. They can explain local public options and wait times.
  • Ask private clinics questions. About fees, what is included, timing, and whether ADHD and autism can be assessed together, as explored on the page about being assessed for both.

Common Questions About Private and Public ADHD and Autism Assessment in BC

What is the difference between private and public ADHD assessment in BC?

Public ADHD assessment in BC is funded but usually needs a referral and may involve long waits. Private assessment is faster to access but paid privately or through benefits.

Are public autism assessments available for adults in BC?

Public adult autism assessment options in BC are limited, so many adults choose a private autism assessment with a psychologist.

How do children get a publicly funded autism assessment in BC?

Children and youth can access publicly funded autism assessment through the provincial network, usually with a referral from a physician or nurse practitioner.

Is a private ADHD diagnosis as valid as a public one?

Yes. A private ADHD diagnosis from a qualified professional is generally as valid as a public one for most purposes, including accommodations.

Does MSP cover private ADHD or autism assessments?

No. MSP does not cover private ADHD or autism assessments, though some extended health plans cover part of the cost.

Can private assessment costs be claimed as a medical expense?

Psychological services may qualify for the medical expense tax credit in Canada. Check current CRA rules for your private assessment costs.

Why are wait times longer for public ADHD and autism assessments?

Public ADHD and autism assessment services have limited capacity and high demand, which often leads to long waits.

Can a private autism diagnosis be used to access funding for my child?

A private autism diagnosis from a qualified professional may be used to apply for provincial funding for children. Confirm current requirements.

How is a private psychological assessment different from a psychiatric assessment?

A private psychological assessment often includes detailed testing and a comprehensive report, while a psychiatric assessment may focus more on diagnosis and medication.

How do I choose between private and public assessment?

Consider urgency, cost, benefits coverage, and what you need the assessment for, and talk with your doctor about local public options.
