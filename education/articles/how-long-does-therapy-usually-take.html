How Long Does Therapy Usually Take

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Therapy can take anywhere from a few sessions to a few years. Many people working on a specific concern, such as anxiety, a phobia, or a recent stressor, see meaningful change within about eight to twenty sessions, often over three to six months. Long-standing patterns, complex trauma, or personality-related difficulties often take longer, sometimes a year or more. The length depends on your goals, the type of therapy, how often you attend, and what is happening in your life. It is not set in stone, and you and your therapist can review progress and adjust the plan along the way.

How It Works

Different approaches tend to have different typical lengths. These are general patterns, not rules:

  1. Single sessions or very brief work. Some people come for one to three sessions to solve a specific problem or get practical guidance.
  2. Short-term structured therapy. Approaches such as CBT, solution-focused therapy, and some trauma-focused therapies often run around eight to twenty sessions.
  3. Medium-term therapy. Work on depression with other life stressors, relationship patterns, or couples concerns may take several months to a year.
  4. Longer-term therapy. Approaches aimed at deep patterns, such as schema therapy, psychodynamic therapy, or complex trauma work, may last one to three years.
  5. Maintenance or booster sessions. Some people continue with occasional sessions after the main work is done.

Practical Details

What affects the timeline. The nature and severity of concerns, how long they have been present, co-occurring conditions, life stress, support at home, and how consistently you can attend and practise between sessions all play a role.

Frequency matters. Weekly sessions often lead to faster progress early on than sessions spaced far apart. The page about how often to attend therapy explains this further.

Costs and coverage. Extended health plans often have annual limits, which can affect how many sessions people attend in a year. It helps to check your coverage and discuss a realistic plan with your therapist. The clinic can answer questions about fees.

Goals shape length. Therapy focused on one clear goal tends to be shorter. Therapy that explores many areas, or deep personal growth, is often longer.

Progress is not always linear. Some weeks feel like big steps, while others feel slow. Setbacks are part of change.

How to Plan for It

  • Set goals early. Agreeing on what you want to change gives a way to measure progress. A treatment plan can help.
  • Ask about typical timelines. Your therapist can give a rough sense of how long similar work usually takes.
  • Review regularly. Every few months, check in about what has improved and what is left.
  • Plan the ending. Many therapists gradually space sessions out toward the end and talk about how to maintain progress.
  • Know you can return. Many people come back for a few sessions during later stressful periods.

Signs that therapy is on track are discussed on the page about how to know if therapy is working. If you are in crisis at any point, call or text 9-8-8, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About How Long Therapy Takes

For one specific concern, how many weeks of therapy is typical?

Many people working on one specific concern see meaningful change within about eight to twenty weekly sessions, or roughly three to six months, though needs vary.

Why does therapy for long-standing patterns take longer?

Deep patterns built over many years, such as those addressed in schema or psychodynamic therapy, often take a year or more of sessions to shift.

Can therapy be helpful in just a few sessions?

Yes. Some people benefit from one to three sessions focused on a specific problem or practical guidance.

Does attending therapy more often shorten how long it takes?

Weekly sessions often lead to faster early progress than sessions spaced far apart, which can shorten the overall timeline.

How do I know when therapy is finished?

Therapy often ends when your goals are met and you feel confident maintaining progress, usually with sessions spaced out toward the end.

Will my extended health plan cover all the sessions I need?

Extended health plans often have annual limits, so check your coverage and discuss a realistic number of sessions with your therapist.

Can I go back to therapy after I have finished?

Yes. Many people return for a few sessions during later stressful periods, sometimes called booster sessions.

Why does progress in therapy feel slow some weeks?

Progress in therapy is rarely linear. Some sessions bring big steps and others feel slow, which is a normal part of change.

Should I ask my therapist how long therapy will take?

Yes. Your therapist can give a rough timeline based on similar work and review it with you over the months of therapy.

What is the typical length of couples therapy compared with individual therapy?

Couples therapy often lasts several months, similar to medium-term individual therapy, though both vary with goals and concerns.
