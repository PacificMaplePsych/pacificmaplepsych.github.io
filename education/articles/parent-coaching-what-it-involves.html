Parent Coaching: What It Involves

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Parent coaching is a form of support in which a trained professional works with parents, usually without the child present, to help them understand their child's behaviour and emotions and build practical strategies for common challenges, such as tantrums, anxiety, sibling conflict, screen time, bedtime struggles, or school difficulties. The focus is on the parent-child relationship and on changes parents can make at home. Many approaches are grounded in research on child development and behaviour. Parent coaching can be short-term and targeted, and it often helps parents feel more confident and less alone. It is not a sign of failure. It is a way of investing in the family.

How Parent Coaching Works

Parent coaching is based on the idea that parents have a powerful influence on their children's behaviour and wellbeing, and that small, consistent changes in how parents respond can lead to meaningful improvement. Common elements include:

  • Understanding behaviour. Looking at what might be driving a child's behaviour, such as unmet needs, big emotions, developmental stage, anxiety, or neurodivergence.
  • Strengthening connection. Building positive time together, which makes children more responsive to guidance.
  • Emotion coaching. Helping parents name and validate children's feelings while guiding behaviour.
  • Clear, consistent routines and expectations. Predictable structure helps children feel secure.
  • Effective limits. Setting calm, clear limits and consequences.
  • Parent self-regulation. Helping parents manage their own stress and reactions, which influences how children respond.

Some coaching draws on evidence-based parenting programs, such as behavioural parent training, while other coaching integrates attachment and emotion-focused ideas. For children with anxiety, parents may learn how to reduce accommodation and support brave behaviour, as explained on the page about anxiety in children and teens.

What Sessions Are Like

Parent coaching usually begins with an intake to understand the family, the child's development, current challenges, and what parents hope will change. Parents may be asked to track behaviours or situations for a week or two. Sessions then focus on specific goals, such as smoother mornings or calmer bedtimes. The coach suggests strategies, helps parents practise them, and troubleshoots what happens at home.

Sessions are often weekly or every other week and may last a few months. Some parents attend together, while others come alone, and co-parents in separate households can also take part, as described on the page about co-parenting after separation. Sessions can be in person or virtual. Between sessions, parents practise strategies at home and note what works.

Parent coaching differs from child therapy, where the child meets with a therapist directly. Sometimes both are used together.

Who It Can Help

Parent coaching can help parents of young children and teens facing everyday challenges, such as tantrums, defiance, anxiety, sleep problems, screen time, and conflict. It can support parents of neurodivergent children, including those with ADHD or autism, and parents going through transitions such as a new baby, separation, or a move. It can also help parents who feel overwhelmed or burnt out, as described on the page about parental burnout.

When a child shows significant or persistent distress, such as ongoing low mood, self-harm, severe anxiety, or trauma symptoms, direct support for the child from a mental health professional is important, often alongside parent support. Common Questions About Parent Coaching

What happens in a parent coaching session?

In parent coaching, parents discuss specific challenges, learn strategies suited to their child, practise them, and review what happens at home.

Does my child need to attend parent coaching?

Usually not. Parent coaching typically works with parents alone, focusing on changes they can make at home with their child.

How is parent coaching different from child therapy?

Parent coaching works with parents on strategies at home, while child therapy involves the child meeting a therapist directly. Both can be combined.

What parenting challenges can parent coaching help with?

Parent coaching can help with tantrums, defiance, child anxiety, sleep, screen time, sibling conflict, and school difficulties.

How long does parent coaching usually last?

Parent coaching is often weekly or every other week for a few months, depending on the family's goals.

Can parent coaching help if my child has ADHD or autism?

Yes. Parent coaching can help parents of neurodivergent children understand their child's needs and build supportive routines.

Can separated co-parents do parent coaching together?

Yes. Co-parents in separate households can take part in parent coaching to align approaches for their child.

What is emotion coaching for parents?

Emotion coaching teaches parents to name and validate a child's feelings while guiding behaviour, which helps children learn to regulate.

Does needing parent coaching mean I am a bad parent?

No. Parent coaching is a way of investing in your family and gaining new tools, not a sign of failure as a parent.

When does a child need their own therapist instead of parent coaching?

If a child shows persistent low mood, self-harm, severe anxiety, or trauma symptoms, direct support for the child is important, often with parent support.
