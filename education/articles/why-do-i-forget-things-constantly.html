Why Do I Forget Things Constantly

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You walk into a room and cannot remember why. You miss an appointment you were sure you would remember without writing down. You forget someone's name moments after being introduced. If forgetfulness has become a near daily frustration, it is worth understanding the range of things that can cause it, some temporary, some longer standing, before assuming the worst about your memory.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Stress and overload. When there is too much competing for your attention, working memory, the mental space that holds information temporarily while you use it, gets crowded. Under chronic stress, things genuinely do not get encoded properly in the first place, which can feel like forgetting even though the information may never have fully registered.

Sleep deprivation. Sleep plays a critical role in consolidating memories, and insufficient or poor quality sleep measurably impairs both the formation and retrieval of memories, independent of any other cause.

Depression and anxiety. Memory complaints are extremely common in both depression and anxiety. Depression reduces the mental engagement needed to encode new information well, while anxiety pulls attention toward worry, leaving less capacity for the task of actually registering and holding onto information.

Hormonal changes. Perimenopause and menopause are commonly associated with noticeable memory and concentration changes, often described as brain fog, related to fluctuating hormone levels affecting cognitive function.

Medical factors. Thyroid dysfunction, certain medications, and some other medical conditions can all affect memory. If forgetfulness appeared suddenly, worsened quickly, or came with other new physical symptoms, it is important to have it checked out medically rather than assuming a psychological explanation.

Autism. Some autistic people experience working memory differences and executive functioning difficulties that can look similar to what is described in ADHD, sometimes alongside it.

When It Looks More Like ADHD

ADHD-related forgetfulness is primarily rooted in working memory differences rather than a problem with long-term memory storage itself. Working memory is what allows you to hold a piece of information in mind long enough to actually use it, remembering an instruction on your way to carry it out, holding onto what you walked into a room to get, or keeping track of a thought until you can act on or write it down. When working memory is less reliable, information can slip away within seconds, not because it was poorly stored, but because it was never held onto long enough to be used or properly filed away in the first place.

This distinction matters because it explains a pattern that confuses a lot of people: someone can have a razor sharp memory for detailed trivia, a favourite subject, or a story from years ago, while consistently forgetting to reply to a text, pick up milk, or where they put their keys five minutes ago. This is not a contradiction. Long term memory for genuinely engaging or well rehearsed information can be excellent, while working memory, the moment to moment holding and using of new information, is where the actual difficulty lies.

ADHD-related forgetfulness also tends to be lifelong and to show up across many different types of situations, rather than being tied to a specific period of stress or a hormonal transition. If this pattern goes back as far as you can remember and shows up alongside other ADHD features like inattention or disorganization, that combination is worth exploring further.

What Can Help

The most effective strategies for working memory difficulties tend to focus on externalizing memory rather than trying to strengthen it directly. Writing things down the moment they occur to you, keeping consistent designated places for frequently lost items like keys and wallets, using reminder apps with alarms rather than relying on remembering to check a list, and reducing unnecessary cognitive load wherever possible can all meaningfully reduce the day to day impact.

Common Questions About Forgetfulness

Is forgetfulness always about memory, or could it be about attention in the first place?

Often it is genuinely about attention rather than memory storage. Information that was never fully registered because attention was elsewhere cannot really be forgotten in the usual sense, since it was never properly encoded to begin with. This is a very common pattern in ADHD.

Could hormonal changes explain memory lapses?

Yes, particularly during perimenopause and menopause, when fluctuating hormone levels are commonly associated with noticeable memory and concentration changes, often described as brain fog. This is a well recognized and legitimate cause worth considering.

When should forgetfulness be checked out medically rather than psychologically?

If forgetfulness appeared suddenly, is worsening quickly, or is accompanied by other new physical symptoms, it is important to see a doctor to rule out medical causes such as thyroid dysfunction or medication side effects before assuming a purely psychological explanation.

Is “working memory” the same thing as long-term memory?

No, they are distinct systems. Working memory is what holds information temporarily so you can use it right now, while long-term memory is where information gets stored for later retrieval. ADHD primarily affects working memory, which is why long-term memory for engaging topics can remain excellent.

Can chronic stress really make someone this forgetful?

Yes. Chronic stress crowds working memory and can prevent new information from being properly encoded in the first place, which produces genuine, noticeable forgetfulness independent of any underlying attention condition.

Why do I forget things I care about but remember trivia effortlessly?

This reflects the difference between working memory and long-term memory. Well rehearsed or highly engaging information can be stored effectively in long-term memory, while everyday tasks that rely on working memory, like remembering to reply to a message, are more vulnerable to slipping away.

Does depression affect memory the way people think it does?

Yes, memory complaints are a genuine and common feature of depression. Reduced engagement and motivation affect how well new information gets encoded, which can produce real, noticeable forgetfulness rather than simply feeling like it.

Are reminder apps and lists a real solution or just a workaround?

They are a genuinely effective strategy, not just a workaround. Externalizing memory, moving information out of an unreliable internal system and into a reliable external one, is one of the most evidence-supported approaches for managing working memory difficulties.

Is forgetfulness in adulthood ever a new-onset sign of ADHD?

ADHD itself is present from childhood, so the underlying working memory difference is not new. What is common is that the forgetfulness becomes more noticeable or harder to compensate for later in life, once demands increase or previous coping strategies stop being enough.

What does an assessment look at to explain memory complaints?

An assessment considers how long the pattern has been present, whether it shows up alongside other ADHD features, whether stress, mood, sleep, or hormonal factors could better explain it, and whether a medical evaluation is also warranted to rule out other causes.
