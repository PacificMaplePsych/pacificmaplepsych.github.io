Can We Do Couples Therapy Virtually

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Yes, couples therapy can be done virtually, usually through a secure video platform, and research suggests online couples therapy can be as helpful as in-person sessions for many couples. Partners can join from the same room or, in some cases, from different locations. Virtual sessions can make therapy easier to fit around work, childcare, and travel, and they widen the choice of therapists across British Columbia. They do require some preparation, such as a private space, a reliable connection, and an agreement about what happens if a conversation gets heated. Some situations, such as safety concerns in the relationship, may need a different approach.

How It Works

  1. Booking. You schedule a session and receive a link to a secure video platform.
  2. Getting set up. Partners usually sit together in front of one device, with the camera showing both faces. If partners are in different places, each joins separately.
  3. The session. The therapist guides conversations much as in person, helping each partner speak and listen, pointing out patterns, and coaching new ways of communicating. The process is described on the page about how couples therapy works.
  4. Breaks and pauses. If a conversation becomes too heated, the therapist may suggest a short pause or help each partner calm down before continuing.
  5. Between sessions. Couples may receive exercises to try at home, just as with in-person therapy.

Many approaches, including emotionally focused therapy and the Gottman Method, can be delivered by video.

Practical Details

Privacy. Choose a room where you will not be overheard, especially by children. Headphones may help if others are home, though partners sitting together usually use a shared speaker.

Technology. A laptop or tablet with a stable internet connection works best. Phones can be harder to use for two people. Test your setup before the first session.

Location in BC. Therapists are generally licensed or registered to practise in particular provinces, so clients usually need to be located in BC during sessions. Ask the clinic if you will be travelling.

Cost and coverage. Fees and extended health coverage for virtual sessions are often the same as for in-person sessions, but it is worth checking with your plan and the clinic.

Frequency. Virtual couples sessions often follow the same rhythm as in-person sessions, as described on the page about how often to attend therapy.

When virtual may not fit. If there is abuse, coercive control, or fear of a partner, joint sessions in a shared home may not be safe. Individual support and safety planning come first, and resources are available on the page about abuse resources. Some couples also find in-person sessions easier for very intense conflict.

How to Prepare

  • Find a quiet, private space and arrange childcare if needed.
  • Test the video platform, camera, and audio in advance.
  • Sit so that both of you are visible and can hear clearly.
  • Turn off notifications and close other apps.
  • Agree together on what you will do if things get heated, such as taking a short break.
  • Plan a few minutes after the session to decompress, rather than jumping straight into other tasks.

Common Questions About Virtual Couples Therapy

Is virtual couples therapy as effective as in-person sessions?

Research suggests online couples therapy can be as helpful as in-person sessions for many couples.

Do we need to sit together for virtual couples therapy?

Partners usually sit together in front of one device for virtual couples therapy, though some join from separate locations.

What equipment do we need for online couples therapy?

A laptop or tablet with a stable internet connection, a camera that shows both partners, and clear audio work best for online couples therapy.

Can we do virtual couples therapy if we live in different places?

Often yes. Partners in different locations can join virtual couples therapy separately, as long as both can participate privately.

How do we keep virtual couples sessions private at home?

Choose a room where you will not be overheard, arrange childcare if needed, and turn off notifications during virtual couples sessions.

What happens if an argument gets heated during a video couples session?

The therapist may suggest a pause or help each partner calm down. Agreeing on a break plan before virtual couples sessions helps.

Can we keep our virtual couples sessions going while travelling outside BC?

Therapists are usually registered to practise in particular provinces, so you generally need to be in BC during virtual couples sessions. Ask the clinic before travelling.

Is virtual couples therapy covered by extended health plans?

Extended health coverage for virtual couples therapy is often the same as for in-person sessions, but check your plan.

Is video couples therapy safe if one partner is afraid of the other?

Usually not. If there is abuse, coercive control, or fear of a partner, joint video couples sessions at home may not be safe, and individual support comes first.

How should we prepare for our first virtual couples therapy session?

Test your setup, find a private space, sit so both partners are visible, and agree on a break plan before your first virtual couples session.
