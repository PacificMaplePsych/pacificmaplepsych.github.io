Why Do I Have Physical Symptoms Doctors Can't Explain

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Having physical symptoms that doctors cannot explain is common, and it does not mean the symptoms are imagined. Pain, fatigue, dizziness, digestive problems, and other symptoms can be very real even when tests come back normal. Often they involve how the nervous system processes signals, how stress affects the body, or conditions that are diagnosed by their pattern rather than a specific test. When the symptoms bring significant worry and take over much of life, they may be understood as somatic symptom disorder. Both the symptoms and the distress around them can be helped.

People often feel frustrated and dismissed, having seen several specialists, undergone many tests, and heard "everything looks fine" while still feeling unwell.

New, worsening, or changing symptoms should always be checked by a doctor. Call 911 for chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, sudden weakness or numbness, or a sudden severe headache.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

A condition that takes time to diagnose. Some illnesses, such as autoimmune conditions, can take years to identify. Ongoing follow-up with a trusted doctor matters.

Functional disorders. Conditions such as irritable bowel syndrome, fibromyalgia, chronic fatigue, and functional neurological symptoms involve real changes in how body systems work, even though standard tests look normal.

Stress. Long-term stress affects muscles, digestion, sleep, and pain sensitivity. Many people notice headaches or stomach aches when stressed without feeling emotionally anxious.

Anxiety and depression. Both commonly show up through the body, with fatigue, aches, dizziness, and digestive changes, sometimes before emotional symptoms are noticed.

Past trauma. Traumatic experiences can leave the nervous system in a state of alert that shows up as pain, tension, and other physical symptoms.

Worry about illness. For some people, the main difficulty is fear of what symptoms mean. The page on constantly worrying about being seriously ill covers this.

When It Looks More Like Somatic Symptom Disorder

Somatic symptom disorder describes a situation where physical symptoms are accompanied by thoughts, feelings, and behaviours that take up a great deal of time and energy. The symptoms themselves are genuine. The diagnosis focuses on how much distress and disruption surround them. Signs include:

  • Symptoms that have lasted many months and cause significant distress.
  • Persistent, high levels of worry about what symptoms mean.
  • Spending a lot of time and energy on symptoms, appointments, or research.
  • Daily life, work, or relationships being shaped around the symptoms.
  • Feeling that no explanation or treatment has helped, and that others do not understand.

The mind and body influence each other continuously. Attention, fear, and stress can amplify how strongly symptoms are felt, while symptoms in turn increase stress. This is not the same as saying symptoms are "all in your head." It means that treating the whole system, including the nervous system's response, often reduces symptoms and improves quality of life. Somatic symptom disorder can occur alongside medical conditions too.

What Can Help

  • Build a partnership with one doctor. Regular follow-up with a family doctor you trust helps catch real changes without endless testing.
  • Focus on function. Setting goals around what you want to be able to do, rather than eliminating every symptom, often helps life expand again.
  • Pace activity. Gradual, steady activity tends to work better than pushing hard on good days and crashing afterward.
  • Calm the nervous system. Slow breathing, relaxation, and gentle movement can reduce symptom intensity.
  • Keep a symptom and stress log. Patterns between stress, sleep, and symptoms can guide what helps.

Common Questions About Medically Unexplained Symptoms

Does it mean my symptoms are fake if tests come back normal?

No. Many real symptoms do not show up on standard tests. Pain, fatigue, and digestive problems can come from changes in how the nervous system and body function, which tests may not detect.

What are medically unexplained symptoms?

They are physical symptoms that persist without a clear cause found on examination and testing. They are common in primary care and are real experiences, even when their source is not fully understood.

What is somatic symptom disorder?

Somatic symptom disorder describes genuine physical symptoms accompanied by intense worry and time spent on them, to the point that daily life is significantly affected. It is treatable with psychological approaches.

What is a functional disorder?

A functional disorder involves a problem with how a body system works rather than visible damage. Examples include irritable bowel syndrome and functional neurological disorder. They are diagnosed by their pattern of symptoms.

Can stress really cause physical pain without anxiety?

Yes. Many people experience stress mainly through their body, with pain, tension, or digestive symptoms, without feeling consciously anxious. The body often signals stress before the mind recognizes it.

Why do doctors suggest therapy for physical symptoms?

Therapy is suggested because the nervous system, stress, and attention all influence symptoms. It is not a dismissal. Psychological treatment can reduce symptom intensity and help people function better.

Can trauma show up as unexplained physical symptoms?

It can. Trauma can keep the nervous system on high alert, contributing to pain, fatigue, and other symptoms. Trauma-informed therapy may help when this is part of the picture.

How do I cope when doctors cannot find what is wrong?

Keep working with one trusted doctor, focus on improving daily function, pace activities, and look after sleep and stress. Seeking support for the frustration and worry is also helpful.

Should I keep asking for more tests?

New or changing symptoms deserve assessment. When extensive testing has been reassuring, repeated tests may add stress without new answers. Discussing a follow-up plan with your doctor is usually more helpful.

How does CBT help with physical symptoms that have no clear cause?

Cognitive behavioural therapy helps people understand how stress, attention, and behaviour affect symptoms, reduce unhelpful checking and avoidance, and gradually rebuild activities and confidence.
