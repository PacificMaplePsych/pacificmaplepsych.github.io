Why Do I Get Sweaty Palms Before Talking to People

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You are about to shake someone's hand, walk into an interview, or introduce yourself at a gathering, and your palms are suddenly damp. You wipe them on your pants, hold a cold drink, or keep your hands in your pockets. For some people it is a minor nuisance. For others, the worry that someone will notice becomes a reason to avoid handshakes, meetings, dates, or social events altogether.

Sweaty palms are one of the most common physical signs of nerves. They can also come from causes unrelated to anxiety. Understanding which is at play helps you decide what is worth addressing.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Normal nerves. The palms have a high concentration of sweat glands that respond to emotional arousal, not just heat. A bit of sweating before an important conversation is simply your body preparing for something that matters.

Hyperhidrosis. Some people sweat heavily from the palms, feet, or underarms regardless of mood or temperature. This condition, called primary focal hyperhidrosis, often starts in childhood or adolescence and can run in families. Doctors and dermatologists have several effective treatments for it.

Hormones and medical factors. Perimenopause, an overactive thyroid, low blood sugar, and some medications can increase sweating.

Caffeine and environment. Coffee, energy drinks, warm rooms, and heavy clothing can all push sweating higher, especially when combined with a little nervousness.

Excitement. Meeting someone you are interested in or talking to a person you admire can produce the same physical reaction as fear.

When It Looks More Like Anxiety

When sweaty palms show up mainly in social situations and come with a sense of dread about being judged, social anxiety may be involved. Social anxiety is an intense fear of being evaluated negatively, embarrassed, or rejected by others. It often comes with physical symptoms such as sweating, blushing, trembling, a shaky voice, or a nervous stomach.

A particular loop tends to keep sweating going. People with social anxiety often fear that others will see their physical symptoms and judge them for it. So before and during a conversation, attention turns inward to monitor for sweat. That heightened self-focus increases anxiety, which increases sweating, which confirms the fear. The more you try not to sweat, the more you tend to.

Signs that anxiety is the main driver include:

  • Sweating that appears before or during social interactions but much less when you are alone and relaxed.
  • Worry beforehand about people noticing, followed by replaying the interaction afterward.
  • Safety behaviours such as avoiding handshakes, keeping tissues ready, wearing certain clothes, or holding objects to hide your hands.
  • Skipping or cutting short social situations because of the symptoms, a pattern described in Why Do I Avoid Situations That Make Me Nervous.

It is worth knowing that other people notice physical signs of anxiety much less than the anxious person expects. Research on social anxiety consistently finds that people overestimate how visible their symptoms are.

What Can Help

  • Shift attention outward. Focusing on the other person, what they are saying and what you are curious about, reduces the self-monitoring that fuels sweating.
  • Drop safety behaviours gradually. Try one handshake without wiping first, or one meeting without holding a cold drink. Noticing that nothing bad happens weakens the fear.
  • Calm the body beforehand. Slow breathing and cool water on your wrists before a meeting can take the edge off physical arousal.
  • Talk to a doctor if sweating is heavy, constant, or started in childhood, since hyperhidrosis treatments can make a real difference.

Common Questions About Sweaty Palms and Social Nerves

Why do my palms sweat even when I'm not hot?

The sweat glands on the palms respond strongly to emotional arousal, not just temperature. Nerves, excitement, and stress can all trigger palm sweating. If your palms sweat heavily even when you feel calm, a condition called hyperhidrosis may be involved.

What is hyperhidrosis, and how is it different from nervous sweating?

Primary focal hyperhidrosis is a condition where specific areas, often the palms, feet, or underarms, sweat excessively regardless of emotional state or temperature. It usually begins in childhood or adolescence. Nervous sweating, by contrast, rises and falls with stress and social situations.

Do other people actually notice sweaty palms during a conversation?

Much less than most people fear. Research on social anxiety shows that people consistently overestimate how noticeable their physical symptoms are to others. Most people are focused on the conversation, not on your hands.

Can social anxiety cause sweating, blushing, and trembling together?

Yes. Social anxiety commonly activates the body's stress response, which can produce sweating, blushing, trembling, a shaky voice, and a racing heart at the same time. These symptoms often feel much more obvious from the inside than they look from the outside.

Why does trying not to sweat make it worse?

Trying hard to control a stress symptom keeps attention locked on it, which signals threat to the brain and increases arousal. That makes sweating more likely. Shifting attention outward, onto the conversation, tends to work better than trying to suppress the symptom.

Is avoiding handshakes a sign of social anxiety?

It can be, if you avoid them because you fear being judged for sweaty hands. This is an example of a safety behaviour. It reduces discomfort in the moment but can keep the fear going by preventing you from learning that others rarely react the way you expect.

Can perimenopause or thyroid issues cause sweaty palms?

Yes. Hormonal changes during perimenopause and an overactive thyroid can both increase sweating. If sweating has increased along with other changes such as hot flashes, weight changes, or a racing heart, it is worth checking with a doctor.

What treatments help with heavy palm sweating?

For hyperhidrosis, doctors may suggest prescription antiperspirants, iontophoresis (a treatment using a mild electrical current through water), medications, or injections. When sweating is mainly driven by social anxiety, therapy targeting the anxiety often reduces it.

Can cognitive behavioural therapy help with social anxiety symptoms like sweating?

Yes. CBT for social anxiety focuses on reducing self-focused attention, dropping safety behaviours, and testing feared predictions about how others will react. As anxiety decreases, physical symptoms like sweating usually become less intense and less distressing.

How can I calm sweaty palms right before a job interview?

Run cool water over your wrists, slow your breathing with a longer exhale, and remind yourself that some nerves show you care. Shifting your focus to the interviewer and the questions, rather than your hands, also helps reduce the self-monitoring that fuels sweating.
