Psychological Support After a Car Crash in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

If you were injured in a car crash in BC, ICBC's Enhanced Care coverage generally pays for counselling and psychology treatment to help with the emotional effects of the crash. In the first 12 weeks after a crash, you can attend a set number of pre-approved sessions with a registered psychologist or clinical counsellor without a referral from a doctor and without asking ICBC for approval. You will need your ICBC claim number and your Personal Health Number. Many providers, including Pacific Maple Psych Centre, can bill ICBC directly, so you do not pay up front for covered sessions. Treatment can continue after the first 12 weeks when ICBC approves it.

Why Emotional Recovery Matters After a Crash

Physical injuries are only part of recovering from a collision. Many people also notice anxiety in cars, trouble sleeping, irritability, low mood, or memories of the crash that will not settle. For some, these reactions develop into PTSD after a car crash, driving anxiety, or depression. Pain, time off work, and dealing with an insurance claim can add stress on top of the crash itself.

Getting support early can make a real difference. Therapy can help you understand your reactions, manage sleep and stress, cope with pain, and return to driving and daily activities at a pace that feels manageable.

How ICBC Coverage Generally Works

ICBC's Enhanced Care model, in place since May 2021, provides care and recovery benefits to people injured in crashes in BC, generally including drivers, passengers, pedestrians, and cyclists. For psychological care, the key points are:

  1. Report your claim. Open an injury claim with ICBC and keep your claim number handy.
  2. Book with a registered provider. You can go directly to a registered psychologist or clinical counsellor. No doctor's referral is needed for the pre-approved sessions in the first 12 weeks.
  3. Bring your details. Your provider will ask for your claim number and Personal Health Number.
  4. Billing. Providers who bill ICBC directly send the invoice to ICBC. If a provider does not bill directly, you pay first and submit for reimbursement up to ICBC's approved rate.
  5. Continuing care. If you need more support after the first 12 weeks, treatment can continue with ICBC approval, usually supported by an update from your provider about your progress and goals.

ICBC sets its own rates and rules, and these can change from year to year, so it is worth confirming details with ICBC or your provider when you start.

What Therapy After a Crash Can Include

Therapy is tailored to what you are experiencing. It may focus on:

  • Trauma symptoms. Flashbacks, nightmares, and feeling on edge, using approaches such as cognitive processing therapy and prolonged exposure.
  • Getting back on the road. Gradual steps toward being a passenger and driving again, as described on the page about driving again after a crash.
  • Pain and sleep. Psychological strategies for living with pain and restoring sleep, as described on the page about psychological approaches to chronic pain.
  • Mood and adjustment. Coping with frustration, loss of independence, or changes in work and family roles.
  • Returning to work. Planning a gradual return and managing symptoms on the job.

Assessments and Other Situations

Sometimes an insurer, employer, or lawyer requests an independent psychological opinion about how a crash has affected someone. This is different from therapy, and it is explained on the page about what to expect at an independent medical evaluation. If the crash happened at work, WorkSafeBC may be involved instead of, or as well as, ICBC.

Common Questions About ICBC Coverage for Therapy

Does ICBC cover counselling or psychology after a car accident?

Yes. ICBC Enhanced Care generally covers counselling and psychology treatment for people injured in a crash in BC.

How many psychology sessions does ICBC pre-approve after a crash?

ICBC pre-approves a set number of psychology sessions in the first 12 weeks after a crash, currently 12. More can be approved after that.

Can I start ICBC-covered psychology sessions without ICBC approval?

Yes. In the first 12 weeks after a crash, pre-approved psychology sessions do not need a referral or ICBC approval, only your claim number and Personal Health Number.

What do I need to book ICBC-covered therapy?

To book ICBC-covered therapy, you need your ICBC claim number and your Personal Health Number.

Will I have to pay up front for ICBC therapy sessions?

Not if your provider bills ICBC directly. Pacific Maple Psych Centre can bill ICBC directly for covered therapy sessions.

Can I get ICBC-covered therapy after the first 12 weeks?

Yes. ICBC-covered therapy can continue after 12 weeks with ICBC approval, usually supported by a progress update from your provider.

Are passengers and pedestrians covered for therapy by ICBC?

Generally, yes. ICBC Enhanced Care benefits usually apply to drivers, passengers, pedestrians, and cyclists injured in a crash in BC.

Can I do ICBC-covered therapy virtually?

Yes. Many providers, including Pacific Maple Psych Centre, offer ICBC-covered therapy virtually as well as in person.

What kinds of problems can therapy after a car crash help with?

Therapy after a car crash can help with PTSD, driving anxiety, sleep problems, low mood, pain-related stress, and returning to work.

Is an independent medical evaluation the same as therapy after a crash?

No. An independent medical evaluation gives an objective opinion for an insurer or legal matter, while therapy after a crash focuses on your recovery.
