Therapy vs. Counselling: What's the Difference

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In everyday use, therapy and counselling mean much the same thing: talking with a trained professional to understand and work through emotional, mental health, or relationship difficulties. Some people use "counselling" for shorter-term, more focused support on a specific problem, such as a life transition or stress, and "therapy" or "psychotherapy" for longer or deeper work on patterns, trauma, or mental health conditions. In practice, there is a lot of overlap, and the terms are often used interchangeably. What matters more than the label is the provider's training, credentials, approach, and experience with your concern, and whether you feel comfortable working with them.

How the Terms Are Used

Counselling. Often describes support for a specific issue, such as grief, work stress, a breakup, or adjustment to change. It may be shorter term and focused on coping and problem-solving. Examples include grief counselling and premarital counselling.

Therapy or psychotherapy. Often describes structured treatment for mental health conditions, such as depression, anxiety, OCD, or PTSD, or longer-term work on relationship patterns and personal history. It frequently uses specific approaches, such as CBT, DBT, or psychodynamic therapy, as described on the page about types of therapy.

The overlap. Many counsellors provide evidence-based therapy, and many therapists offer short-term, practical support. The skill and fit of the individual provider matter more than the word used.

Practical Details

Who provides it in BC. Registered psychologists, registered clinical counsellors, clinical social workers, and some other professionals provide therapy and counselling. Their training and regulation differ, as explained on the page about psychologists, clinical counsellors, and psychotherapists in BC.

Titles. In BC, some titles, such as "psychologist," are legally protected, while words like "counsellor," "therapist," and "psychotherapist" have not been restricted in the same way. Checking credentials is important.

Assessment and diagnosis. Psychologists can diagnose and conduct standardized psychological assessments. Other providers may offer screening or work alongside a diagnosis made elsewhere.

Coverage. Private therapy and counselling are not covered by MSP. Extended health plans cover specific designations, so check which ones your plan lists, as described on the page about extended health benefits for therapy.

Cost. Fees vary by provider type and experience.

How to Decide What Fits

  • Think about your concern. A specific stressor may need shorter-term support, while a diagnosed condition or long-standing pattern may benefit from specialized therapy.
  • Check credentials. Look for a regulated or registered professional with relevant training.
  • Ask about approach and experience. Especially with your concern.
  • Consider whether you need an assessment. For ADHD, autism, or learning concerns, a psychologist is often the right choice.
  • Check your benefits. Make sure the provider's designation is covered if cost is a factor.
  • Trust the fit. Feeling understood and respected is one of the strongest predictors of benefit, whatever it is called.

Common Questions About Therapy and Counselling

What is the difference between therapy and counselling?

Therapy and counselling overlap a great deal. Counselling sometimes means shorter, focused support, while therapy often means treatment for conditions or deeper patterns.

Is counselling shorter than therapy?

Counselling is sometimes shorter and focused on a specific issue, but many counsellors also provide longer-term therapy, so the label alone does not decide length.

Is psychotherapy the same as therapy?

Yes. Psychotherapy is the formal term for talk therapy, and the two words are generally used interchangeably.

Who provides counselling and therapy in BC?

In BC, registered psychologists, registered clinical counsellors, clinical social workers, and some other professionals provide counselling and therapy.

Are counsellor and therapist protected titles in BC?

In BC, the title psychologist is protected, but words like counsellor and therapist have not been restricted in the same way, so checking credentials matters.

Can a counsellor diagnose mental health conditions?

Psychologists can diagnose and conduct standardized assessments. Counsellors may screen for concerns or work alongside a diagnosis made by another professional.

Does extended health cover counselling as well as therapy?

Extended health plans cover specific provider designations rather than the words counselling or therapy, so check which designations your plan lists.

Should I see a counsellor or a psychologist?

A counsellor may suit focused support or ongoing therapy, while a psychologist is often the right choice if you need a formal assessment or diagnosis.

Does the label matter when choosing therapy or counselling?

Not much. A provider's training, credentials, approach, and fit with you matter more than whether it is called therapy or counselling.

What questions should I ask a counsellor before starting?

Ask a counsellor about their credentials, training, approach, experience with your concern, fees, and whether your benefits plan covers their designation.
