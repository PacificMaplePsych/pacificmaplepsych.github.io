What to Expect in Your First Couples Therapy Session

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In your first couples therapy session, the therapist will usually ask what brings you in, hear each partner's perspective, learn a little about your history together, and begin to identify the patterns that keep coming up. They will also explain how they work, how confidentiality applies, and how sessions will be structured, including whether there will be individual meetings with each partner. You are not expected to solve anything in the first session. The goal is for the therapist to understand your relationship and for both of you to feel heard. Many couples feel nervous beforehand and relieved afterward.

What to Expect

  1. Introductions and consent. The therapist explains their approach, confidentiality and its limits, fees, and policies, such as how they handle information shared privately.
  2. What brings you in. Each partner describes the main concerns. The therapist makes sure both people have space to speak.
  3. Your story as a couple. How you met, what drew you together, major life events, and what has changed.
  4. Current patterns. How conflicts usually start and end, how you communicate, and what each of you does when you feel hurt or distant.
  5. Strengths. What still works in the relationship and what you value in each other.
  6. Goals and next steps. What you each hope will change, and what the next few sessions will look like.

Practical Details

Session length. First sessions are often 50 to 90 minutes. Some therapists book a longer first meeting.

Individual meetings. Many couples therapists meet each partner individually after the first session, as described on the page about whether both partners need to attend every session.

Questionnaires. Some approaches, such as the Gottman Method, use relationship questionnaires as part of the assessment.

Virtual sessions. First sessions can be held by secure video, ideally with both partners in the same private space. See the page about virtual couples therapy.

Safety. Therapists screen for abuse, coercive control, and fear in the relationship. If joint sessions are not safe, they may recommend individual support first. Resources are on the abuse resources page.

Fees. Ask the clinic about fees and whether your extended health plan covers couples therapy.

How to Prepare Together

  • Talk briefly about why you are going. Without trying to settle disagreements beforehand.
  • Each think about your goals. What would be different if therapy helped?
  • Agree on logistics. Travel, childcare, or a private room for virtual sessions.
  • Expect some discomfort. Talking about problems in front of a third person can feel awkward at first.
  • Plan for afterward. Give yourselves a calm evening rather than continuing heated discussions.

After the first session, most couples continue with regular sessions, as explained on the pages about how often couples attend therapy and how long the process usually takes.

Common Questions About the First Couples Therapy Session

What does a first couples therapy session usually cover?

In the first couples therapy session, the therapist hears each partner's perspective, learns your history, identifies patterns, explains how they work, and discusses goals.

Will we solve our problems in the first couples session?

No. The first couples session is about understanding your relationship and making sure both partners feel heard, not solving everything.

Is the opening couples appointment longer than later ones?

Sometimes. Couples sessions are often 50 to 90 minutes, and some therapists book a longer initial meeting to cover history and goals.

Will the couples therapist take sides in the first session?

No. A couples therapist aims to stay balanced and make sure both partners have space to share their perspective.

Does the couples therapist ask about how we met?

Often yes. Couples therapists usually ask about how you met, what drew you together, and important events in your relationship.

Why is screening for abuse part of starting couples counselling?

Couples therapists routinely screen for abuse, coercive control, and fear, because joint sessions may not be safe in those situations.

Can our first couples session be virtual?

Yes. A first couples session can be held by secure video, ideally with both partners in the same private space in BC.

What can partners do the week before starting couples therapy?

Each partner can think about goals, agree on logistics, expect some discomfort, and plan a calm evening after the first couples session.

Is it normal to feel nervous before couples therapy?

Yes. Many partners feel nervous before couples therapy, and many feel relieved after the first session.

Do partners usually get one-on-one meetings after the opening couples session?

Often yes. After the first couples session, many therapists schedule individual meetings with each partner, then regular joint sessions.
