Anxiety and Physical Symptoms You Might Miss

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Always have new, severe, or changing physical symptoms checked by a doctor, especially chest pain, trouble breathing, fainting, or sudden weakness. Call 911 in an emergency. Once medical causes have been ruled out, anxiety may be part of the picture.

Anxiety is often thought of as worry, but it is also a whole-body experience. Many people notice physical symptoms before they recognize that they are anxious, and some visit doctors repeatedly for symptoms that turn out to be linked to anxiety. These symptoms are real, not imagined. They come from the body's stress response, which prepares you to deal with threat. Understanding the physical side of anxiety can reduce fear of the symptoms themselves and help people get the right kind of support.

Physical Symptoms of Anxiety

  • Heart. Racing, pounding, or skipped beats, as described on the page about a heart that races for no reason.
  • Breathing. Shortness of breath, tight chest, or feeling like you cannot breathe.
  • Dizziness. Lightheadedness, feeling faint, or unsteadiness, explored on the page about dizziness when anxious.
  • Digestion. Nausea, stomach pain, diarrhea, or loss of appetite.
  • Muscles. Tension, aches, jaw clenching, headaches, or trembling.
  • Skin and temperature. Sweating, hot flashes, chills, or flushing.
  • Nerves. Tingling, numbness, or pins and needles, often in hands, feet, or face.
  • Sleep. Difficulty falling asleep, waking often, or feeling unrested.
  • Energy. Fatigue and exhaustion from being on edge.
  • Throat. A lump in the throat or difficulty swallowing.

Why Anxiety Causes Physical Symptoms

When the brain senses threat, it activates the stress response. Adrenaline speeds up the heart and breathing, redirects blood to muscles, tenses the body, and slows digestion. These changes help in real emergencies. With anxiety, the stress response can activate when there is no danger, or stay switched on for long periods. Fast or shallow breathing can cause dizziness, tingling, and chest tightness. Ongoing muscle tension leads to aches and headaches. Chronic stress affects digestion, sleep, and energy.

Physical symptoms can also create a cycle. Noticing a racing heart or dizziness can trigger fear that something is seriously wrong, which increases anxiety and intensifies the symptoms. This cycle is central to panic attacks and health anxiety. Paying close attention to body sensations can make them feel stronger.

Physical symptoms can appear even when people do not feel consciously worried. Someone may say they are fine but notice stomach problems during stressful periods, or tension headaches after demanding weeks. Recognizing these patterns can help people connect body signals with stress and respond earlier.

What It Is Not

  • Not all in your head. The symptoms are real physical changes.
  • Not usually dangerous. Anxiety symptoms are uncomfortable but typically harmless once medical causes are ruled out.
  • Not a reason to skip medical care. A doctor should check new or changing symptoms.
  • Not permanent. Symptoms often ease as anxiety is treated.

How It Is Addressed and Treated

After medical causes have been ruled out, treating anxiety usually reduces physical symptoms. Cognitive behavioural therapy helps people understand the link between thoughts, the body, and behaviour, and reduces fear of physical sensations. Interoceptive exposure, which involves safely bringing on sensations such as a fast heartbeat, can reduce fear of them. Slow breathing with longer exhales calms the stress response. Progressive muscle relaxation eases tension. Regular exercise, sleep, and limiting caffeine and alcohol help. Medication may be considered for some people. Learning that symptoms are anxiety-related, rather than signs of danger, often brings significant relief.

Common Questions About the Physical Side of Anxiety

Which body symptoms can anxiety produce?

Anxiety activates the stress response, causing a racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness, stomach upset, tension, and more.

Where does the pins and needles feeling during panic come from?

Fast or shallow breathing can change blood chemistry slightly, causing tingling or numbness in the hands and face.

Can anxiety cause chest pain?

It can cause chest tightness or discomfort, but chest pain should always be checked by a doctor first.

How is my gut affected when I feel nervous?

The stress response slows digestion and affects gut function, causing nausea, pain, or changes in bowel habits.

Why do I feel lightheaded in stressful moments?

Rapid breathing and changes in blood flow can cause lightheadedness and unsteadiness.

Is it possible to have physical anxiety without feeling worried?

Yes. Some people notice body symptoms during stress even when they do not feel consciously anxious.

Why would a therapist ask me to make my heart race on purpose?

This technique, called interoceptive exposure, safely brings on body sensations, such as a fast heartbeat, to reduce fear of them.

How can I calm physical anxiety quickly?

Slow breathing with longer exhales, relaxing tense muscles, and grounding in your surroundings can help.

Why do anxiety symptoms feel so real?

They are real physical changes caused by the stress response, even though they are not dangerous.

Does treating anxiety reduce physical symptoms?

Yes. As anxiety is treated, physical symptoms often ease significantly.
