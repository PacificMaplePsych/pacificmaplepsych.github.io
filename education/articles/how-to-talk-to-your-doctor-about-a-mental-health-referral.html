How to Talk to Your Doctor About a Mental Health Referral

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

To talk to your doctor about a mental health referral, book an appointment specifically for this concern, describe your symptoms with a few concrete examples, explain how they affect your daily life, and say clearly what kind of help you are hoping for, such as therapy, an assessment, or a psychiatry referral. It can help to bring written notes, since it is easy to downplay things in the moment. Family doctors and nurse practitioners talk about mental health often, and asking for help is a normal part of health care. In BC, you do not need a referral to see a private psychologist or counsellor, but a referral is usually needed for psychiatry and many public programs.

How It Works

The conversation. Your doctor will usually ask about your symptoms, how long they have lasted, sleep, appetite, substance use, and safety. They may use brief questionnaires for depression or anxiety.

Physical health. Your doctor may check for medical causes of symptoms, such as thyroid problems, anemia, medication side effects, or hormonal changes, sometimes with blood tests.

Options they may discuss.

  • Referral to a psychiatrist, which is covered by MSP.
  • Referral to public mental health and substance use services through your health authority.
  • Suggestions for private therapy with a psychologist or counsellor, which you can usually book yourself.
  • Medication, which a family doctor or nurse practitioner can prescribe and monitor.
  • Referral for specialized assessment or programs, such as for eating disorders or early psychosis.

Practical Details

When a referral is needed. Psychiatry, many publicly funded services, and some specialized programs require a referral. Some extended health benefit plans also ask for a physician's referral before reimbursing psychology sessions. The page about whether you need a referral to see a psychologist explains more.

Assessments. Public autism and ADHD assessments generally need a referral, while private ones usually do not, as covered on the page about referrals for ADHD and autism assessments.

No family doctor. Walk-in clinics, urgent and primary care centres, nurse practitioners, and virtual care services can also discuss mental health and make some referrals. You can call 8-1-1 for health advice and help finding services.

Appointment length. Doctor appointments can be short. Booking a dedicated appointment for mental health, rather than adding it to another concern, gives more time.

Coordinating care. With your consent, your doctor and therapist can share information, which is especially helpful if medication is involved.

How to Prepare

  • Write down your main symptoms. When they started, how often they happen, and specific examples.
  • Describe the impact. How symptoms affect work, school, relationships, sleep, and self-care.
  • Note what you want. For example, "I would like a referral to a psychiatrist" or "I am hoping to discuss therapy options."
  • List medications and supplements. Including anything you have tried before.
  • Be honest about safety. If you have had thoughts of self-harm or suicide, tell your doctor. This helps them respond appropriately.
  • Bring a support person if helpful. They can help you remember what was said.
  • Ask about next steps. How long a referral may take, and what to do while waiting.

If wait times are long, private therapy or more intensive options may be worth discussing. If you are in crisis, do not wait for an appointment. Call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About Asking a Doctor for a Mental Health Referral

How do I ask my doctor for a mental health referral?

Book a dedicated appointment, describe your symptoms with examples, explain how they affect daily life, and say clearly what kind of referral you are hoping for.

What should I tell my doctor about my mental health?

Tell your doctor about your main symptoms, how long they have lasted, how they affect your life, medications, and any thoughts of self-harm.

Why is a doctor's referral needed before seeing a psychiatrist in BC?

Psychiatrists are specialists covered by MSP, so a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner is generally required to see one in BC.

Can my doctor refer me to a private psychologist?

Your doctor can suggest a private psychologist, but you can usually book one yourself in BC. Some benefit plans ask for a physician's referral.

Why might my doctor order blood tests for mental health symptoms?

Your doctor may order blood tests to check for physical causes of mental health symptoms, such as thyroid problems, anemia, or hormonal changes.

Where can I get a mental health referral without a regular GP?

Walk-in clinics, primary care centres, nurse practitioners, and virtual care services can discuss mental health and make some referrals. You can also call 8-1-1.

Should I book a separate doctor's appointment for mental health?

Yes. A separate doctor's appointment for mental health gives more time than adding it to another concern.

Is it okay to bring notes to my doctor's appointment?

Yes. Bringing notes to a doctor's appointment helps you remember symptoms and examples, since it is easy to downplay things in the moment.

Should I tell my doctor about thoughts of suicide?

Yes. Telling your doctor about thoughts of suicide or self-harm helps them respond appropriately and connect you with the right support.

What can I do while waiting for a mental health referral?

While waiting for a referral, ask your doctor about interim options, consider private therapy, and use crisis services such as 9-8-8 if needed.
