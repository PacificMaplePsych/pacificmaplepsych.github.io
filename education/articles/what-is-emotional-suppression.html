What Is Emotional Suppression

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Emotional suppression is the act of pushing down, hiding, or avoiding feelings rather than noticing, expressing, or working through them. It can be deliberate, such as holding back tears at work, or so habitual that people barely notice they are doing it. Everyone suppresses emotions sometimes, and in certain moments it is useful. When suppression becomes the main way of handling feelings, though, it tends to backfire. Research on emotion regulation suggests that chronic suppression is linked with more stress, lower wellbeing, and difficulties in relationships. The good news is that other ways of handling emotions can be learned.

What Emotional Suppression Looks Like

  • Avoiding talking about how you really feel.
  • Saying you are fine when you are not.
  • Keeping busy to avoid feelings coming up.
  • Feeling numb or disconnected, or numb instead of sad.
  • Sudden outbursts when feelings that have built up finally spill over.
  • Physical tension, headaches, or stomach aches when stressed.
  • Difficulty identifying what you feel.
  • Relying on food, alcohol, screens, or work to avoid emotions.

Why It Happens

People learn to suppress emotions for many reasons. In some families, certain feelings, such as anger, sadness, or fear, were discouraged, punished, or ignored. Children in these environments learn that hiding feelings keeps them safe or accepted. This is common after childhood emotional neglect. Cultural norms, gender expectations, and workplace cultures can also reward emotional restraint. Some people suppress feelings because they fear being overwhelmed or burdening others. Trauma can lead people to shut down emotions as a way of coping.

In the short term, suppression can help people get through difficult situations. Over time, though, emotions that are pushed down do not simply disappear. Research suggests that suppression does not reduce the internal experience of emotion and can increase physiological stress. It also takes mental effort, which can affect memory and concentration. In relationships, suppressing feelings can make people seem distant, and partners may feel shut out.

Suppression is different from choosing when to share. Healthy emotional regulation includes deciding the right time and place to express feelings, such as waiting until after a meeting to talk about frustration. The difference is that feelings are eventually acknowledged and processed rather than permanently buried.

Some people notice that suppression works differently for different emotions. They may express anger easily but hide sadness, or show warmth but never frustration. Paying attention to which feelings feel off limits can point to the messages learned growing up and show where change would help most.

What It Is Not

  • Not the same as healthy self-control. Choosing when to express feelings differs from never acknowledging them.
  • Not a sign of strength or weakness. It is a learned coping strategy.
  • Not the absence of feelings. Suppressed emotions are still present and often affect the body.
  • Not always harmful. Brief suppression in specific situations can be adaptive.
  • Not something you must reverse by sharing everything. Change can happen gradually and privately.

How It Is Addressed and Supported

Moving away from chronic suppression often starts with noticing emotions, including physical signs such as a tight chest or clenched jaw, and naming them without judgment. Journalling, mindfulness, and emotion vocabulary lists can help. Therapy offers a safe place to practise feeling and expressing emotions. Emotion-focused therapy helps people access and work with feelings. Acceptance and commitment therapy teaches making room for emotions rather than fighting them. Dialectical behaviour therapy skills help with regulating intense feelings once they are allowed. Cognitive reappraisal, changing how a situation is interpreted, is often a healthier regulation strategy than suppression. Gradually sharing feelings with trusted people builds confidence.

Common Questions About Emotional Suppression

What does it mean to bottle up emotions?

Bottling up, or emotional suppression, means pushing down, hiding, or avoiding feelings rather than noticing, expressing, or working through them.

Is suppressing emotions bad for you?

Occasional suppression can be useful, but chronic suppression is linked with more stress, lower wellbeing, and relationship difficulties.

Why did I learn to keep my feelings hidden?

Many people learned early that certain feelings were unsafe or unwelcome, or fear being overwhelmed or burdening others.

Can suppressing emotions cause physical symptoms?

Yes. Suppression can increase physiological stress and is linked with tension, headaches, and digestive problems.

Why do I suddenly explode after holding things in?

Emotions that are repeatedly pushed down can build up and spill over when stress or triggers become too much.

What is the difference between suppression and emotional regulation?

Regulation involves acknowledging feelings and managing how you express them. Suppression involves pushing them away without processing.

How does emotional suppression affect relationships?

It can make people seem distant or closed off, and partners may feel shut out or unsure what is wrong.

How can I start expressing my emotions more?

Begin by noticing and naming feelings, journalling, and sharing small things with trusted people. Therapy can help too.

What is cognitive reappraisal?

Cognitive reappraisal is changing how you interpret a situation, which can shift emotional responses more effectively than suppression.

Which therapies help people who suppress their feelings?

Emotion-focused therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy, and DBT skills are commonly used to build emotional awareness and expression.
