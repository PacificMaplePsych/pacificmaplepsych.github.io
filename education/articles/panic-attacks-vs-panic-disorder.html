Panic Attacks vs. Panic Disorder

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear or discomfort that peaks within minutes, often with a pounding heart, shortness of breath, dizziness, and a sense of losing control or dying. Panic attacks are common, and many people have one or two in their lives without developing a disorder. Panic disorder is diagnosed when attacks happen repeatedly and unexpectedly, and the person develops ongoing worry about future attacks or changes their life to avoid them. Both are treatable, and knowing the difference helps people get the right support.

Chest pain, shortness of breath, or fainting can also be signs of a medical emergency. If you are unsure, or if these symptoms are new, call 911 or seek urgent medical care.

Why They Are Easy to Confuse

The terms are often used interchangeably, and panic attacks are the main feature of panic disorder, so the line can seem blurry. Both involve the same intense physical symptoms, such as a racing heart, feeling unable to breathe, trembling, and fear of something terrible happening. Panic attacks can also occur in many other conditions, including social anxiety, phobias, PTSD, and depression, which adds to the confusion.

Key Differences

Frequency. A panic attack can be a one-time or occasional event. Panic disorder involves recurrent attacks.

Predictability. Many panic attacks are triggered by a specific situation, such as a feared object or public speaking. Panic disorder involves at least some unexpected attacks that seem to come out of nowhere.

Fear of fear. In panic disorder, people develop persistent worry about having another attack or about what attacks mean, such as fearing a heart attack or losing their mind.

Behaviour change. Panic disorder often leads to avoidance, such as avoiding exercise, caffeine, travel, or places where attacks have happened.

Impact. Occasional panic attacks may cause little ongoing disruption. Panic disorder can significantly affect work, relationships, and daily life.

When Both Are Present

Panic disorder is defined by panic attacks, so people with the disorder experience both. Panic disorder also commonly occurs with agoraphobia, where fear of panic leads to avoiding places that feel hard to escape. It frequently co-occurs with depression, other anxiety disorders, and substance use, sometimes because people use alcohol to manage fear. Some people have panic attacks as part of another condition, such as social anxiety or PTSD, without meeting criteria for panic disorder.

Why the Distinction Matters

Understanding the pattern shapes treatment. An occasional situational attack may improve with learning what panic is and a few coping strategies. Panic disorder is best treated with cognitive behavioural therapy, which helps people understand the panic cycle, reduce fear of physical sensations through interoceptive exposure, drop safety behaviours, and return to avoided situations. Medication prescribed by a physician can also help some people. When panic is part of another condition, treating that condition is key. A medical assessment is important, especially for first attacks, to rule out heart, thyroid, or other causes.

Many people with panic disorder see substantial improvement within a few months of CBT. The fear that panic is dangerous is usually the main thing keeping it going, and learning otherwise is a turning point.

Common Questions About Panic Attacks and Panic Disorder

What does a panic attack feel like?

A panic attack brings sudden intense fear with symptoms such as a pounding heart, shortness of breath, dizziness, sweating, trembling, and a sense of doom, peaking within minutes.

Does having a panic attack mean I have panic disorder?

No. Many people have occasional panic attacks without panic disorder. The disorder involves recurrent unexpected attacks and ongoing fear or avoidance.

How long does a panic attack last?

Most panic attacks peak within about ten minutes and ease within half an hour, though people may feel shaken or tired afterward.

What is fear of fear in panic disorder?

It is persistent worry about having another panic attack or about what the sensations mean, which can itself trigger more attacks and avoidance.

Can panic attacks happen for no reason?

Some panic attacks feel unexpected, with no obvious trigger. These unexpected attacks are a key feature of panic disorder.

How do I know if it is a panic attack or a heart problem?

Symptoms can overlap. If chest pain or breathing trouble is new or you are unsure, seek urgent medical care. A doctor can check for heart and other medical causes.

Is panic disorder linked to agoraphobia?

Yes. Many people with panic disorder develop agoraphobia, avoiding places where escape or help would be difficult if panic occurred.

What is interoceptive exposure?

Interoceptive exposure involves deliberately bringing on harmless sensations like a fast heartbeat or dizziness to learn they are not dangerous, reducing fear of panic symptoms.

Can panic disorder go away?

Yes. Panic disorder responds well to cognitive behavioural therapy, and many people become panic-free or experience far fewer attacks.

What should I do during a panic attack?

Remind yourself it is panic and will pass, slow your breathing, stay where you are if safe, and let the wave peak and fade rather than fighting it.
