What Is the Gottman Method

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

The Gottman Method is an approach to couples therapy developed by psychologists John Gottman and Julie Schwartz Gottman, based on decades of research observing how couples interact. It aims to strengthen friendship and intimacy, help partners manage conflict constructively, and build a shared sense of meaning. The method offers practical tools that couples can use at home, and it pays close attention to patterns that research has linked with relationship distress, such as criticism and defensiveness. The Gottman Method can help couples in many situations, from those wanting to strengthen a good relationship to those facing serious difficulties.

How the Gottman Method Works

The method is built around a model called the Sound Relationship House, which describes the building blocks of a healthy relationship:

  • Love maps. Knowing your partner's inner world, including their worries, hopes, and daily life.
  • Fondness and admiration. Expressing appreciation and respect.
  • Turning toward. Responding to each other's small bids for attention and connection, such as a comment or a look.
  • Positive perspective. Giving each other the benefit of the doubt.
  • Managing conflict. Handling disagreements with gentleness, repair, and compromise.
  • Making life dreams come true. Supporting each other's goals and hopes.
  • Creating shared meaning. Building rituals, values, and a sense of purpose together.

These are supported by trust and commitment.

The Gottman research also identified four patterns, called the Four Horsemen, that predict relationship difficulties when they become frequent: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling. The method teaches antidotes to each, such as starting conversations gently, building a culture of appreciation, taking responsibility, and self-soothing when overwhelmed. It also helps couples recognize that some problems are perpetual, rooted in differences in personality or values, and learn to talk about them with understanding rather than trying to solve them completely. Understanding each partner's conflict style, as described on the page about conflict styles, is part of this work.

What Sessions Are Like

Therapy usually begins with an assessment. This often includes a joint session to hear the couple's story, individual sessions with each partner, and questionnaires about the relationship. The therapist then shares observations about strengths and challenges and suggests goals.

Later sessions combine conversation and practice. The therapist may coach partners through difficult conversations in the session, teach skills such as softened start-ups and repair attempts, and help them recognize when one or both partners are flooded, meaning too physiologically stressed to listen well, so they can take a break. Couples often receive exercises to try at home, such as daily check-in conversations or sharing appreciations.

The length of therapy varies. Some couples attend weekly for a few months, while others attend intensive sessions over a few days. Therapists trained in the Gottman Method may also integrate other approaches, such as emotionally focused therapy.

Who It Can Help

The Gottman Method can help couples who want to improve communication, reduce conflict, rebuild trust after a betrayal, reconnect after emotional distance, or adjust to life changes such as a new baby. It is also used with couples preparing for marriage, as described on the page about premarital counselling. Research on the method suggests it can improve relationship satisfaction for many couples, though, as with all therapies, outcomes vary.

Couples caught in cycles such as the pursue-withdraw pattern often find its practical tools helpful. The method is not suitable when there is ongoing abuse, coercive control, or serious safety concerns, where individual support and safety planning come first, and resources are available on the page about abuse resources. When one partner is struggling with significant depression, addiction, or trauma, individual therapy alongside couples work may be needed.

Common Questions About the Gottman Method

Who developed the Gottman Method?

It was developed by psychologists John Gottman and Julie Schwartz Gottman, based on decades of research on couples.

What are the Four Horsemen in the Gottman Method?

They are criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling, patterns that predict relationship difficulties when frequent.

What is the Sound Relationship House?

It is a model describing the building blocks of a healthy relationship, from knowing each other to creating shared meaning.

What does turning toward mean in a relationship?

It means responding warmly to a partner's small bids for attention and connection.

What are perpetual problems in couples?

They are recurring differences rooted in personality or values that couples learn to discuss rather than fully solve.

What happens during a Gottman Method assessment?

It usually includes a joint session, individual sessions with each partner, and questionnaires about the relationship.

What is the antidote to criticism in the Gottman Method?

Starting conversations gently, describing your feelings and needs rather than attacking your partner.

Can the Gottman Method help after an affair?

Yes. The method includes approaches for rebuilding trust after betrayal.

Are there intensive Gottman sessions?

Some therapists offer intensive sessions over a few days as an alternative to weekly appointments.

When is Gottman couples therapy not recommended?

It is not suitable when there is ongoing abuse, coercive control, or serious safety concerns.
