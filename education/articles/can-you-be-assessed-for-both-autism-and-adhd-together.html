Can You Be Assessed for Both Autism and ADHD Together

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Yes, you can be assessed for both autism and ADHD at the same time, and combined assessments are common. The two conditions often occur together, and their traits can overlap, such as difficulty with attention, executive functioning, emotional regulation, and social situations. A combined assessment looks at both conditions, along with other possible explanations, to understand which best describe your experience. It may take somewhat longer than a single assessment, but it can give a more complete picture and avoid the need for a second assessment later. Since 2013, diagnostic criteria have allowed autism and ADHD to be diagnosed together.

How It Works

Interview and history. The clinician asks about attention, activity level, impulsivity, organization, social communication, sensory experiences, routines, and interests, from childhood to now.

Questionnaires. Rating scales for both ADHD and autism traits are completed by you and often by someone who knows you well.

Standardized measures. These may include tests of attention and executive functioning, and structured observation of social communication.

Untangling overlap. The clinician considers whether difficulties are best explained by ADHD, autism, both, or something else. For example, social difficulties in ADHD may come from impulsivity or missing details, while in autism they often relate to differences in social communication. Similarly, routines may be a coping strategy in ADHD and a core need in autism.

Other conditions. Anxiety, depression, trauma, and learning differences are also considered, since they can affect results.

Report and feedback. Findings and recommendations for both conditions are explained, as described on the page about feedback sessions.

Practical Details

Time. Combined assessments often involve more appointments than a single assessment. Ask the clinic about the expected timeline.

Cost. A combined assessment usually costs more than a single one, but often less than two separate assessments. Private assessments are not covered by MSP, though some extended health plans cover part of the cost.

Referrals. Private combined assessments can usually be booked without a referral, as described on the page about referrals for ADHD and autism assessments.

Possible outcomes. You may be diagnosed with ADHD, autism, both, neither, or another condition.

Medication. If ADHD is diagnosed, a physician or nurse practitioner can discuss medication. Some autistic people with ADHD respond differently to medication, so close follow-up helps.

How to Prepare

  • Describe both sets of experiences. Attention and organization difficulties, as well as social, sensory, and routine-related experiences.
  • Gather childhood information. Report cards and memories from family members are especially helpful.
  • Note masking and coping strategies. These can hide both ADHD and autism traits.
  • Request accommodations. Such as breaks or sensory adjustments.
  • Ask about the process. How many appointments, what is included, and how results will be shared.

Common Questions About Combined Autism and ADHD Assessments

Is a single evaluation for AuDHD possible?

Yes. A single combined evaluation for autism and ADHD is common, since the two often occur together and their traits can overlap.

Can someone be diagnosed with both autism and ADHD?

Yes. Since 2013, diagnostic criteria have allowed autism and ADHD to be diagnosed together, and many people have both.

Why do autism and ADHD overlap?

Autism and ADHD can both involve differences in attention, executive functioning, emotional regulation, and social experiences, so their traits overlap.

How does a clinician tell autism and ADHD apart?

A clinician looks at why difficulties happen. For example, social challenges may come from impulsivity in ADHD or from social communication differences in autism.

Does a combined autism and ADHD assessment take longer?

A combined autism and ADHD assessment often involves more appointments than a single assessment, but it can avoid a second assessment later.

How does the fee for a joint autism and ADHD evaluation compare?

A combined assessment usually costs more than a single one, but often less than separate autism and ADHD assessments.

What are the possible results of a combined assessment?

A combined assessment may result in a diagnosis of ADHD, autism, both, neither, or another condition that better explains your experience.

Do autistic people respond differently to ADHD medication?

Some autistic people with ADHD respond differently to medication, so close follow-up with the prescriber is helpful.

Can I book a dual autism and ADHD evaluation on my own in BC?

Usually yes. Private dual autism and ADHD evaluations in BC can generally be booked directly, without a referral.

What should I gather before being evaluated for both autism and ADHD?

Describe both attention and social or sensory experiences, gather childhood information, and note masking strategies before a combined autism and ADHD assessment.
