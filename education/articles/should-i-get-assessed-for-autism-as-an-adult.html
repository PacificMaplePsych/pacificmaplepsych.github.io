Should I Get Assessed for Autism as an Adult

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

An adult autism assessment may be worth considering if you have long felt different from others, find social situations exhausting or confusing, experience strong sensory sensitivities, rely heavily on routines, or recognize yourself in descriptions of autistic people, and these patterns go back to childhood. Many adults seek assessment after learning more about autism, after a child or relative is diagnosed, or after burnout makes long-standing difficulties impossible to ignore. A formal diagnosis can bring clarity, self-understanding, and access to support. It is also a personal choice, and some people find self-understanding without one. Knowing what to expect can help you decide.

What Clinicians Look For

Before deciding, it may help to know what an assessment explores:

  • Social communication. Differences in conversation, reading cues, and forming relationships, and how much effort socializing takes.
  • Routines and repetitive behaviours. Preference for predictability, distress with change, and repetitive movements or stimming.
  • Intense interests. Deep, focused interests.
  • Sensory experiences. Sensitivity to sound, light, touch, smell, or taste.
  • A lifelong pattern. Signs present in childhood, even if unrecognized.
  • Masking. How much you hide or compensate for differences, as explained on the page about autism masking.
  • Other explanations. Social anxiety, ADHD, trauma, and other conditions that can look similar or co-occur.

Many adults were missed in childhood because they did not fit older stereotypes, as described on the page about signs of autism in adults.

What the Process Involves

An assessment usually includes an intake, questionnaires, a detailed interview about your current life and childhood, input from someone who knew you growing up where possible, screening for other conditions, and a feedback meeting with a written report. The steps are described in more detail on the page about how autism is diagnosed in adults.

Practical factors often shape the decision. In BC, publicly funded adult autism assessments can be limited, and private assessments vary in cost and wait time. Some extended health plans cover part of the cost of psychological assessment. It helps to ask providers what is included, whether they assess for ADHD at the same time, and how they approach masking in adults. If you are also wondering about ADHD, the page about getting assessed for ADHD as an adult may help.

Some people find it useful to reflect before deciding. What would change if I knew? Am I hoping for self-understanding, accommodations, therapy that fits, or all of these? Would a result either way be helpful?

What a Diagnosis Can and Cannot Tell You

For many adults, a diagnosis brings relief and a new way of understanding their past, including struggles that were blamed on shyness, rudeness, or anxiety. It can reduce self-blame, guide neurodiversity-affirming therapy, and support requests for accommodations at work or school. Decisions about sharing a diagnosis at work are discussed on the page about weighing workplace disclosure. Many people also find connection in autistic communities.

A diagnosis cannot capture everything about who you are, and it does not predict your abilities. Some people choose not to pursue assessment because of cost, wait times, worries about stigma, or because self-understanding and community already meet their needs. These are valid choices. If an assessment does not find autism, it can still clarify what is going on and point toward helpful support. Talking it through with a therapist can help you decide.

Common Questions About Deciding on an Adult Autism Assessment

How do I know if I should get assessed for autism as an adult?

If long-standing differences in social communication, sensory experiences, and routines affect your life and go back to childhood, an autism assessment may help.

I have coped for decades. Is an autism assessment still worthwhile at my age?

Often yes. Many adults are assessed for autism later in life and find it explains years of feeling different, even if they have coped well on the surface.

What could I gain from having my autism confirmed formally?

An adult autism diagnosis can bring self-understanding, reduce self-blame, guide therapy that fits, and support accommodation requests.

Is it reasonable to identify as autistic and skip the formal assessment?

Many people make that choice because of cost, wait times, or stigma concerns, or because self-understanding and autistic community meet their needs. Some accommodations may still require a formal autism diagnosis.

Can my child's autism diagnosis mean I might be autistic too?

Autism often runs in families, so many parents recognize autistic traits in themselves after a child's diagnosis.

Will my extended health plan help pay for an autism evaluation?

Some extended health plans cover part of the cost of psychological assessment, including autism assessment. Check your plan details.

How do I choose a good clinician for an adult autism evaluation?

Ask about their experience with autistic adults, how they approach masking, what the autism assessment includes, the cost and wait time, and whether ADHD is also assessed.

Should my autism evaluation also look at ADHD?

It often makes sense, since autism and ADHD commonly co-occur. Ask providers whether they can assess both in the same process.

What if my autism assessment says I am not autistic?

The autism assessment can still clarify what is behind your experiences and point toward helpful support.

Could talking with a therapist help me weigh whether an autism assessment is right for me?

Yes. A neurodiversity-affirming therapist can help you explore your questions and weigh the pros and cons of an autism assessment.
