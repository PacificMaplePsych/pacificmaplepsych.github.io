Understanding Health Anxiety vs. Somatic Symptom Disorder

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

New, severe, or changing physical symptoms, such as chest pain, sudden weakness, trouble breathing, or a severe headache, should always be checked by a doctor. Call 911 in an emergency.

Health anxiety and somatic symptom disorder are closely related conditions that both involve significant worry about health. Health anxiety is a common everyday term. Clinically, it overlaps with illness anxiety disorder, in which people fear having or developing a serious illness, even though physical symptoms are mild or absent. Somatic symptom disorder, by contrast, centres on physical symptoms that are distressing or disruptive, along with excessive thoughts, feelings, and behaviours about them. Understanding the difference helps people and clinicians choose the most helpful approach, although treatment for both shares a lot in common.

Why They Are Easy to Confuse

Both conditions involve intense attention to the body, worry about what sensations might mean, and frequent checking or reassurance-seeking. People with either condition may keep googling symptoms, visit doctors often, or avoid medical care altogether out of fear. Both can bring distress, sleep problems, and strain on relationships. Everyday language also blurs them, since many people use health anxiety to describe any strong worry about health.

Key Differences

  • Role of physical symptoms. In somatic symptom disorder, one or more physical symptoms are prominent and distressing. In illness anxiety, symptoms are absent or mild, and the fear of illness is central, as in worrying that you are seriously ill.
  • Focus of worry. Somatic symptom disorder focuses on the symptoms themselves and their impact. Illness anxiety focuses on the possibility of having a disease.
  • Response to reassurance. In both, reassurance brings only brief relief, but in illness anxiety a clear test result may be quickly followed by fear of a different illness.
  • Medical diagnoses. Somatic symptom disorder can occur alongside a known medical condition when the response is excessive. Illness anxiety usually involves no significant medical explanation for the fear.

More detail on the symptom-focused condition is on the page about somatic symptom disorder.

When Both Are Present

The two can overlap. A person might have persistent pain or fatigue, which fits somatic symptom disorder, while also fearing that it signals cancer or another serious illness. Anxiety itself produces physical sensations, such as a racing heart, tension, or stomach upset, which can then become a new focus of worry. Many people also experience physical symptoms doctors cannot explain, which can intensify both symptom focus and fear. Clinicians look at the overall pattern rather than fitting someone neatly into one box.

Both conditions can also occur alongside other anxiety disorders, depression, or obsessive-compulsive patterns. For example, repeated checking of the body or searching online can look similar to compulsions in OCD. A careful assessment helps identify which patterns are most important to address.

Why the Distinction Matters

Knowing which pattern is most prominent helps shape treatment. When fear of illness is central, therapy often focuses on reducing checking, reassurance-seeking, and avoidance, and on tolerating uncertainty. When physical symptoms are central, therapy may also address pain management, activity pacing, and gradually returning to avoided activities. In both, cognitive behavioural therapy is the most studied treatment, and working with one trusted primary care provider helps balance appropriate medical care with reducing unnecessary tests.

Common Questions About Health Anxiety and Somatic Symptom Disorder

Is health anxiety the same as illness anxiety disorder?

Health anxiety is an everyday term. Clinically, it overlaps most with illness anxiety disorder, where fear of illness is central and symptoms are mild or absent.

How do I know whether I have health anxiety or somatic symptom disorder?

If distressing physical symptoms are prominent, somatic symptom disorder may fit. If fear of having a disease is central with few symptoms, illness anxiety may fit.

Can someone have both conditions at once?

Yes. Some people have prominent symptoms and also fear serious illness. Clinicians look at the overall pattern.

Why does reassurance from my doctor not last?

Reassurance eases worry briefly, but anxiety returns and often shifts to a new symptom or illness, which keeps the cycle going.

Can anxiety create physical symptoms that I then worry about?

Yes. Anxiety causes real sensations, such as a racing heart or stomach upset, which can become a new focus of worry.

Do people with illness anxiety sometimes avoid doctors?

Yes. Some people check constantly, while others avoid medical care altogether because they fear what they might hear.

If a doctor finds a medical cause, does that rule out a somatic pattern?

No. Somatic symptom disorder can be diagnosed alongside a medical condition when the thoughts and behaviours around symptoms are excessive and disabling.

Which treatment works for both health anxiety and somatic symptoms?

Cognitive behavioural therapy has the strongest evidence for both, often adapted to focus on fear of illness or on symptom management.

Why is seeing one regular doctor recommended?

A single trusted primary care provider can monitor new concerns while reducing repeated tests and conflicting advice.

Is checking my body a form of compulsion?

Frequent body checking or searching online can work like a compulsion, bringing brief relief while strengthening anxiety over time.
