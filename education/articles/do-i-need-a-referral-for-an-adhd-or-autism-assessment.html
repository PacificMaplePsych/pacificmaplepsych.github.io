Do I Need a Referral for an ADHD or Autism Assessment

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, you usually do not need a referral to book a private ADHD or autism assessment with a psychologist. You can contact the clinic directly. A referral is generally needed for publicly funded assessments, such as those for children and youth through provincial programs, and for seeing a psychiatrist. Some extended health benefit plans also ask for a physician's referral before reimbursing assessment costs. Even when a referral is not required, involving your family doctor can be helpful, especially if medication may be part of the plan after an ADHD diagnosis.

How It Works

  1. Decide on private or public. Private assessments are usually faster to access but paid out of pocket or through benefits. Public options are funded but may have long waits and limited availability, especially for adults. The page about private versus public assessment in BC compares them.
  2. For private assessments, contact the clinic. Ask about the process, timing, and fees. Self-referral is common.
  3. For public assessments, see your doctor. A family doctor, nurse practitioner, or paediatrician usually makes the referral.
  4. Check your benefits plan. Find out whether psychological assessments are covered and whether a referral is needed for reimbursement.
  5. Book and prepare. Once booked, the clinic may send intake forms and questionnaires.

Practical Details

Children and youth. In BC, publicly funded autism assessments for children and youth are available through the provincial assessment network, usually with a referral from a physician or nurse practitioner. Wait times can be long, which is why some families choose a private assessment. Private diagnoses from qualified professionals can also be used to apply for provincial autism funding, though families should confirm current requirements.

Adults. Public assessment options for adult ADHD and autism are limited in BC. Many adults seek private assessments, which do not require a referral.

Psychiatry. A psychiatrist can assess and diagnose ADHD, and psychiatry is covered by MSP, but a referral is required and wait times vary.

Benefits. Some plans cover part of a psychological assessment, and some require a physician's note. Plans also have annual maximums, so check how much an assessment would use.

Medication afterward. If you are diagnosed with ADHD and want to consider medication, you will need a prescriber such as a family doctor or psychiatrist. Bringing your report helps, as described on the page about what happens after an ADHD diagnosis.

How to Prepare

  • Read your benefits plan. Look for coverage of psychological assessments and any referral requirement.
  • Talk with your doctor if useful. Even if you do not need a referral, they can share health information and support follow-up care.
  • Ask the clinic questions. About fees, timing, what the assessment includes, and whether it covers ADHD, autism, or both.
  • Gather records. School report cards, past assessments, and medical notes are useful.
  • Start preparing early. The page about preparing for an ADHD or autism assessment offers a checklist.

If you are deciding whether to pursue assessment, the pages about ADHD assessment as an adult and autism assessment as an adult may help.

Common Questions About Referrals for ADHD and Autism Assessments

Do I need a referral for a private ADHD assessment in BC?

Usually not. Private ADHD assessments with a psychologist in BC can generally be booked directly without a referral.

Do I need a referral for an adult autism assessment?

A private adult autism assessment can usually be booked without a referral, though public programs and some benefit plans may require one.

Who makes a referral for a publicly funded autism assessment for a child?

A family doctor, nurse practitioner, or paediatrician usually makes the referral for a publicly funded child autism assessment in BC.

Why would an insurer ask for a doctor's note before covering ADHD testing?

Some benefit plans require a physician's referral before reimbursing an ADHD or autism assessment, so check your plan details.

Can a psychiatrist assess ADHD without a referral?

No. Seeing a psychiatrist for an ADHD assessment generally requires a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner in BC.

Is going through a psychiatrist the only referral route for adult ADHD?

No. Adults can also self-refer to a private psychologist for ADHD assessment. Public routes through psychiatry require a referral and are limited.

Should I tell my doctor I am getting an ADHD or autism assessment?

It can help. Your doctor can share health information and support follow-up care, such as medication after an ADHD diagnosis.

Does a referral matter for a child's autism funding application?

A referral is not usually needed for a private child autism assessment, and a qualified professional's diagnosis may support a funding application. Confirm current requirements with the ministry.

Do I need a prescriber after an ADHD assessment?

If you want ADHD medication after an assessment, you will need a prescriber such as a family doctor, nurse practitioner, or psychiatrist.

What should I ask a clinic before booking an ADHD or autism assessment?

Ask about fees, timing, what the assessment includes, referral needs, and whether it can assess ADHD, autism, or both.
