What Is the Difference Between a Psychologist and a Psychiatrist

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

The main difference is that a psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can diagnose mental health conditions and prescribe medication, while a psychologist has doctoral-level training in psychology and focuses on psychological assessment and therapy, without prescribing. Psychiatrists often focus on medical management, especially for more complex or severe conditions. Psychologists usually spend more time providing talk therapy and conducting detailed assessments, such as for ADHD, autism, or learning differences. Many people benefit from seeing both, with the psychologist providing therapy and the psychiatrist or family doctor managing medication.

How It Works

Psychologists. In BC, registered psychologists generally hold a doctoral degree in psychology and are regulated by the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC. They diagnose mental health conditions, provide evidence-based therapies such as cognitive behavioural therapy, and conduct standardized psychological assessments.

Psychiatrists. Psychiatrists complete medical school and then specialized residency training in psychiatry. They are regulated by the College of Physicians and Surgeons of BC. They assess and diagnose, prescribe and monitor medication, order medical tests, and may provide some therapy, though appointments are often focused on medication and treatment planning.

Working together. A psychologist may suggest that you talk to your family doctor about a psychiatric referral if medication might help, and a psychiatrist may recommend therapy with a psychologist or counsellor.

Practical Details

Referrals. You can usually book directly with a psychologist in private practice. Seeing a psychiatrist generally requires a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner, as described on the page about referrals to see a psychologist.

Cost. Psychiatrist visits are usually covered by MSP. Private psychologist services are not covered by MSP and are paid privately or through extended health benefits.

Wait times. Waits for psychiatry in BC can be long, while private psychologists may be available sooner, though availability varies.

Session style. Therapy with a psychologist usually involves regular sessions of about 50 minutes. Psychiatry appointments may be a longer initial consultation followed by shorter follow-ups.

Other providers. Registered clinical counsellors and other therapists also provide talk therapy, and family doctors often prescribe common medications for anxiety and depression.

How to Decide Who to See

  • If you want talk therapy, a psychologist or counsellor is usually the place to start.
  • If you need a formal assessment, such as for ADHD, autism, or learning differences, a psychologist is often the right choice. Psychiatrists may also diagnose ADHD.
  • If you are wondering about medication, start with your family doctor, who can prescribe or refer you to a psychiatrist. The page about medication versus therapy for depression discusses how these options compare.
  • If symptoms are severe or complex, such as possible bipolar disorder or psychosis, psychiatric input is usually important, often alongside therapy.
  • If you are not sure, a family doctor or an initial consultation with a psychologist can help clarify the best path.

If you are in crisis, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency. For health advice, you can call 8-1-1 to speak with HealthLink BC.

Common Questions About Psychologists and Psychiatrists

What is the main difference between a psychologist and a psychiatrist?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who can prescribe medication. A psychologist has doctoral training in psychology and focuses on assessment and therapy without prescribing.

Can a psychologist prescribe medication in BC?

No. Psychologists in BC do not prescribe medication. Medication is prescribed by physicians, including psychiatrists and family doctors, or nurse practitioners.

Do I need a referral to see a psychiatrist?

Usually yes. Seeing a psychiatrist in BC generally requires a referral from a family doctor or nurse practitioner.

Is a psychiatrist covered by MSP while a psychologist is not?

Generally yes. Psychiatrist visits are usually covered by MSP, while private psychologist sessions are paid privately or through extended health benefits.

Should I see a psychologist or psychiatrist for an ADHD assessment?

Both can diagnose ADHD. A psychologist usually provides a detailed standardized assessment, while a psychiatrist may focus more on diagnosis and medication.

Can I see a psychologist and a psychiatrist at the same time?

Yes. Many people see a psychologist for therapy while a psychiatrist or family doctor manages medication, with care coordinated through consent.

Does a psychiatrist provide therapy like a psychologist does?

Some psychiatrists provide therapy, but appointments are often focused on diagnosis, medication, and treatment planning, while psychologists usually focus on therapy.

How is a psychologist's training different from a psychiatrist's?

Psychologists usually complete a doctoral degree in psychology. Psychiatrists complete medical school and a residency in psychiatry.

Who regulates psychologists and psychiatrists in BC?

Psychologists are regulated by the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC, and psychiatrists by the College of Physicians and Surgeons of BC.

When is it important to involve a psychiatrist in care?

Involving a psychiatrist is often important for severe or complex symptoms, such as possible bipolar disorder or psychosis, or when medication needs are complicated.
