What Happens After Your Child Is Diagnosed With Autism

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

After your child is diagnosed with autism, the next steps usually involve understanding the report, applying for provincial supports such as BC Autism Funding, choosing services that match your child's needs, connecting with your child's school, and making sure you and your family have support too. There is no single right path. The most helpful plan is built around your child's strengths, challenges, and interests, and around what matters to your family. Many parents feel relief, grief, worry, or a mix of all three after a diagnosis, and all of these reactions are normal. Taking things one step at a time helps.

Step 1: Understand the Report

Your child's assessment report describes the diagnosis, your child's strengths and needs, and recommendations. A feedback session is a chance to ask questions, as described on the page about what a feedback session after a psychological assessment is. It is fine to read the report several times and write down questions as they come up. Keep copies of the report, since you will need it for funding, school, and other services.

Step 2: Apply for Autism Funding in BC

In BC, the Ministry of Children and Family Development provides Autism Funding for eligible children and youth with a diagnosis. Current amounts are up to $22,000 per year for children under 6 and up to $6,000 per year for ages 6 to 18. Funding can be used for services such as behaviour consultants, speech-language pathologists, occupational therapists, social skills programs, and family counselling, depending on your child's age and the rules in place. For children under 6, many services must come from professionals listed on the Registry of Autism Service Providers. The province has announced that the program is transitioning to a new model, so check current details with the Autism Funding Unit and keep an eye on updates. The disability resources page also lists other benefits, such as the Disability Tax Credit.

Step 3: Build a Care Team

Depending on your child's needs, a care team might include:

  • A speech-language pathologist for communication.
  • An occupational therapist for sensory, motor, and daily living skills, as described on the page about occupational therapy for sensory processing.
  • A behaviour consultant and behaviour interventionist for skill building and support with challenging situations.
  • A psychologist or counsellor for anxiety, emotional regulation, or family support.
  • Your family doctor or paediatrician for health, sleep, and medical needs.

The page about understanding autism support options for children explains different approaches. It helps to choose one or two priorities to start with rather than trying everything at once, and to choose providers who respect your child and build on their interests, as described on the page about neurodiversity-affirming therapy.

Step 4: Connect With School

Share the report with your child's school to support a ministry designation and an Individual Education Plan (IEP), which sets out goals, adaptations, and supports. Meeting with the school team early helps everyone work together.

Step 5: Care for Yourself and Your Family

A diagnosis affects the whole family. Parents may grieve expectations, feel overwhelmed by paperwork, or worry about the future. Siblings may need information and attention too. Connecting with other parents, accepting help, and getting your own support can make the journey more sustainable. The page about what parental burnout is describes signs to watch for.

What a Diagnosis Does Not Mean

An autism diagnosis does not change who your child is. It gives you language and access to support. Autistic children grow, learn, and develop throughout their lives, and their path will be their own. Some parents also recognize autistic traits in themselves after their child's diagnosis, as described on the page about whether to get assessed for autism as an adult.

Common Questions After a Child's Autism Diagnosis

What are the first steps for BC families after an autism diagnosis?

First steps for BC families include understanding the report, applying for Autism Funding, choosing one or two service priorities, and connecting with the school.

How much is Autism Funding in BC?

BC Autism Funding currently provides up to $22,000 per year for children under 6 and up to $6,000 per year for ages 6 to 18.

What can BC Autism Funding be used for?

BC Autism Funding can be used for services such as behaviour consultants, speech-language therapy, occupational therapy, social skills programs, and family counselling.

What is the Registry of Autism Service Providers?

The Registry of Autism Service Providers lists professionals whose services can be paid for with Autism Funding for children under 6 in BC.

Who should be on my child's autism care team?

An autism care team may include a speech-language pathologist, occupational therapist, behaviour consultant, psychologist or counsellor, and family doctor.

How does sharing an autism diagnosis with school help my child?

Sharing your child's autism diagnosis with school can support a ministry designation and an Individual Education Plan with appropriate supports.

Is it normal for parents to have mixed feelings when a child is diagnosed with autism?

Yes. Parents often feel relief at having answers alongside grief, worry, or uncertainty when a child is diagnosed with autism, and these mixed feelings are normal.

Do I need to start every therapy right away after an autism diagnosis?

No. It usually helps to choose one or two priorities first and build from there based on your child's needs.

What is an IEP for autistic students in BC?

An Individual Education Plan sets out goals, adaptations, and supports for an autistic student in BC schools.

Is counselling helpful for parents after a child is diagnosed with autism?

Yes. Counselling, parent support programs, and connecting with other families can help parents adjust after a child is diagnosed with autism.
