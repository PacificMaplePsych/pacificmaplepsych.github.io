How Often Should Couples Attend Therapy

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Most couples attend therapy weekly or every other week when they start, then move to every three or four weeks as things improve, before ending or shifting to occasional check-ins. Frequent sessions early on help build momentum, reduce the chance that a fight between sessions undoes progress, and allow the therapist to understand your patterns. The right frequency depends on how distressed the relationship is, the concern you are working on, the therapist's approach, your schedules, and your budget. If there is a crisis, such as a recent discovery of an affair, more frequent or longer sessions may be helpful for a period.

How It Works

Starting phase. Weekly or every-other-week sessions are common for the first two to three months. This is when assessment happens and new skills are introduced.

Middle phase. As communication improves and conflict de-escalates, many couples move to every two to three weeks, giving more time to practise at home.

Later phase. Monthly sessions or occasional tune-ups help maintain progress and catch problems early.

Adjusting as you go. Frequency is not fixed. You and your therapist can increase sessions during hard times and space them out when things are steady. The overall length of therapy is covered on the page about how long couples therapy usually takes.

Practical Details

Longer sessions. Some couples find 75- or 90-minute sessions less often work better than shorter weekly ones, because important conversations have time to settle. Some approaches, such as the Gottman Method, also offer intensive formats.

Scheduling. Finding times that suit both partners is often the hardest part. Virtual sessions can help, as explained on the page about virtual couples therapy.

Budget. Fees and coverage vary. Some extended health plans cover couples therapy with eligible providers. If cost is a concern, talk with your therapist about a frequency that balances progress and affordability.

Individual sessions. These may be added at certain points, as explained on the page about whether both partners need to attend every session.

Too far apart. Sessions spaced more than about a month apart in the early phase can make it hard to build on progress.

How to Plan Your Schedule

  • Ask the therapist for a recommendation. Based on your goals and the level of distress.
  • Agree as a couple. Choose a frequency both of you can commit to.
  • Protect the time. Treat sessions as a priority, and arrange childcare in advance if needed.
  • Practise between sessions. Progress happens at home as well as in the room.
  • Review regularly. Every few months, discuss whether to keep the same pace, increase, or taper.

If you are unsure whether therapy is helping, the page about how to know if therapy is working offers signs to watch for.

Common Questions About How Often Couples Attend Therapy

How often should couples go to therapy at the beginning?

Most couples go to therapy weekly or every other week at the beginning, which helps build momentum and understanding.

When can couples space out therapy sessions?

Couples often space out therapy sessions once conflict de-escalates and communication improves, moving to every two to four weeks.

Is every other week enough for couples therapy?

Every other week is enough for many couples, especially when partners practise between sessions. Couples in crisis may need more frequent sessions.

Are longer couples sessions better than weekly ones?

Some couples find longer 75- or 90-minute sessions less often work better than shorter weekly ones, though this depends on the couple and approach.

Should couples attend more often during a crisis?

Yes. During a crisis, such as discovering an affair, couples may benefit from more frequent or longer sessions for a period.

What if our schedules make it hard to attend couples therapy?

Virtual sessions, early or late appointments, and agreeing on a regular time can help couples with busy schedules attend therapy consistently.

Can monthly couples therapy sessions work?

Monthly sessions often work well in the later phase of couples therapy, but early on they may be too far apart to build progress.

How do partners and their therapist settle on a session schedule?

The couple and therapist decide together, based on the relationship's needs, goals, schedules, and budget, and review it over time.

Can couples therapy frequency change over time?

Yes. Couples can increase sessions during difficult periods and space them out when the relationship feels steadier.

How can couples make progress between therapy sessions?

Couples make progress between sessions by practising the skills and conversations from therapy at home and noticing what helps.
