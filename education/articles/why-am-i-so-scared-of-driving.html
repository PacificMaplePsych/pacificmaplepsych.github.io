Why Am I So Scared of Driving

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Your hands grip the wheel, your heart pounds, and your chest tightens as traffic speeds up around you. Highways, bridges, merging, or driving in the dark feel almost impossible. You might plan routes to avoid certain roads, ask others to drive, or stop driving altogether. It can feel embarrassing, especially when driving seems effortless for everyone else, and it can limit your independence in a big way.

Fear of driving is more common than many people realize. It can develop at any age, even in experienced drivers, and it responds well to treatment.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

A past accident or near miss. Being in, or witnessing, a collision can leave strong fear and memories that return behind the wheel. This can be part of trauma, sometimes with startling easily or nightmares about the event.

Lack of experience or confidence. New drivers, or people returning after a long break, may feel unsure of their skills.

Panic attacks. If you have had a panic attack while driving, you may fear it happening again, especially where it is hard to pull over. Physical sensations like dizziness can feel especially frightening at the wheel.

General anxiety. People who worry about many things may find driving triggers thoughts of everything that could go wrong.

Related fears. Fear of driving can be linked with fear of heights on bridges, fear of enclosed spaces in tunnels, or fear of losing control.

Medical or vision changes. Changes in vision, reaction time, or health can reasonably affect confidence. A check with a doctor or eye doctor can help.

When It Looks More Like a Specific Phobia

When fear of driving is intense, persistent, and out of proportion to the actual risk, it may be a specific phobia, sometimes called amaxophobia. Like other phobias, described in Why Do I Feel Terrified of a Specific Thing Everyone Else Seems Fine With, it tends to grow with avoidance.

Signs of a driving phobia include:

  • Intense fear or panic before or during driving.
  • Avoiding highways, bridges, or certain routes, or not driving at all.
  • Dread in the hours or days before you need to drive.
  • Relying heavily on others for transportation.
  • Fear that affects work, family responsibilities, or social life.

Every time you avoid driving, the relief teaches your brain that driving is dangerous, which keeps the fear strong, as explored in Why Do I Avoid Situations That Make Me Nervous.

What Can Help

  • Start small. Practise on quiet streets or at quiet times, then gradually build to more challenging drives.
  • Take a refresher course. Lessons with a patient instructor can rebuild skills and confidence.
  • Use calming skills. Slow breathing and relaxing your grip can reduce physical tension.
  • Stay in the situation when safe. Letting anxiety rise and fall while you keep driving helps retrain your fear response.
  • Plan, but do not over-plan. Knowing your route helps, but avoiding every challenging road keeps fear going.

Common Questions About Fear of Driving

Why am I suddenly scared of driving?

A sudden fear of driving can follow an accident or near miss, a panic attack behind the wheel, a stressful period, or changes in health or vision. It can happen even to experienced drivers.

Is there a name for an intense fear of driving?

Yes. Amaxophobia is a term for an intense fear of driving or being in a vehicle. It is considered a type of specific phobia and is very treatable.

Can a car accident cause fear of driving?

Yes. Being in or witnessing a collision can lead to fear, avoidance, and sometimes trauma symptoms such as flashbacks, nightmares, and being easily startled.

Why do highways make me panic?

Highways involve high speed, heavy traffic, and fewer chances to pull over, which can make people feel trapped. For those prone to panic, this can trigger anxiety.

What should I do if I have a panic attack while driving?

If you can, pull over safely. Slow your breathing, relax your grip, and remind yourself the panic will pass. Therapy can help reduce future panic attacks.

How can I get over my fear of driving on bridges?

Gradual practice, starting with shorter or lower bridges, can help. Pairing this with calming skills and therapy, if needed, can make it easier over time.

Can I overcome fear of driving as an adult?

Yes. Adults of any age can overcome fear of driving with gradual practice, refresher lessons, and therapy approaches like exposure therapy.

Does avoiding driving make the fear worse?

Usually, yes. Avoidance brings short-term relief but teaches the brain that driving is dangerous, which keeps the fear strong or makes it grow.

Is fear of driving after an accident a sign of PTSD?

It can be part of PTSD if it comes with flashbacks, nightmares, avoidance, and feeling on edge that last more than a month. Trauma-focused therapy is effective.

What therapy helps with fear of driving?

Exposure therapy, cognitive behavioural therapy, and trauma-focused therapies like EMDR can help, depending on the cause. Many people improve in a relatively short time.
