Do Both Partners Need to Attend Every Session

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In couples therapy, both partners usually attend most sessions together, because the focus is the relationship and the patterns between you. That said, not every session has to include both people. Many couples therapists schedule one individual session with each partner early on to understand each person's history and goals, and individual sessions may be used at other points if they serve the shared work. If one partner cannot attend a scheduled joint session, the therapist may hold an individual session, reschedule, or adjust the plan. How this works depends on the therapist's approach and policies, so it is worth discussing at the start.

How It Works

Joint sessions are the core. Approaches such as the Gottman Method and emotionally focused therapy work mainly with both partners in the room, so the therapist can see and help change how you interact.

Individual sessions in the assessment phase. Many therapists meet each partner alone once early in therapy. This allows each person to speak openly about their background, commitment, and any safety concerns.

Individual sessions later on. These may be used when one partner is stuck on something personal, such as past trauma or strong reactions, that is affecting the relationship work.

Keeping the balance. Couples therapists try to stay balanced and avoid becoming one partner's ally. Too many individual sessions with one person can make that harder, so they are usually planned with both partners' agreement.

Practical Details

Secrets and confidentiality. Therapists differ in how they handle information shared privately in an individual session. Many use a "no secrets" policy, meaning they will not hold information that undermines the couples work, such as an ongoing affair, and will help the partner share it. Ask about this policy before individual sessions begin.

Missed sessions. If one partner is sick or unavailable, ask whether the therapist prefers to reschedule or meet with one person. Cancellation policies usually apply in the same way.

Virtual and mixed formats. Some couples attend virtually, together in one location, or occasionally from two locations, as explained on the page about virtual couples therapy. Both partners generally need to be in BC.

Safety concerns. If there is abuse, coercive control, or fear of a partner, joint sessions may not be safe or appropriate. A therapist may recommend individual support first. Resources are available on the abuse resources page.

One partner unwilling. If only one partner wants therapy, individual therapy focused on relationship patterns can still help, as described on the page about individual therapy for relationship patterns.

How to Prepare

  • Ask about the attendance structure. Will there be individual sessions, and when?
  • Clarify the confidentiality policy. Know how private information from individual sessions will be handled.
  • Agree on scheduling as a couple. Choose times both of you can reliably attend.
  • Talk about what you each hope for. Shared goals make joint sessions more productive.
  • Plan for disruptions. Decide together what you will do if one of you cannot make a session.

Common Questions About Attending Couples Therapy Sessions

Do both partners have to attend every couples therapy session?

Not always. Both partners usually attend most couples therapy sessions, but individual sessions are common early on and at times when they help the shared work.

What is the purpose of a one-on-one meeting during couples counselling?

Meeting each partner alone lets the couples therapist hear each person's history, goals, commitment, and any safety concerns openly.

What happens if my partner cannot make a couples session?

The therapist may reschedule, meet with one partner, or adjust the plan. Ask about the policy for missed couples sessions at the start.

What is a no secrets policy in couples therapy?

A no secrets policy means the couples therapist will not hold private information that undermines the relationship work and will help the partner share it.

Can too many individual sessions affect couples therapy?

Yes. Too many individual sessions with one partner can make it harder for the therapist to stay balanced, so they are usually planned with both partners.

Can we attend couples therapy from two different locations?

Some therapists allow partners to join virtually from two locations occasionally, though attending together is common. Both partners generally need to be in BC.

When should partners avoid attending sessions together?

Joint sessions may not be safe when there is abuse or coercive control. A therapist may recommend individual support and safety planning first.

What if my partner refuses to come to therapy at all?

If your partner will not attend, individual therapy focused on relationship patterns can still help you understand and change your part in the relationship.

Do individual sessions in couples therapy cost the same as joint sessions?

Fees for individual and joint sessions in couples therapy vary by clinic, so ask how each type of session is billed.

How should we decide on scheduling as a couple?

Choose session times both partners can reliably attend, and agree in advance on what to do if one of you has to miss a session.
