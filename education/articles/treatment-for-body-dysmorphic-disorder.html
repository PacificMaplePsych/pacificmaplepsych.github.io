Treatment for Body Dysmorphic Disorder

Body dysmorphic disorder (BDD) is treated most effectively with cognitive behavioural therapy designed for BDD, which includes exposure and response prevention, and in some cases with medication prescribed by a physician or psychiatrist. BDD involves intense preoccupation with perceived flaws in appearance that others see as minor or do not notice, along with repetitive behaviours such as mirror checking, comparing, grooming, or seeking reassurance. Treatment helps people reduce these behaviours, relate differently to appearance-focused thoughts, and rebuild a life that is not dominated by how they look. Cosmetic procedures rarely relieve BDD and can sometimes make it worse.

The Main Treatment Options

CBT for BDD. CBT adapted for BDD has the strongest research support. It helps people understand the BDD cycle, notice and question appearance-related beliefs, and shift attention away from perceived flaws. It often includes perceptual retraining, which teaches people to look at their whole appearance in a mirror in a neutral, descriptive way rather than zooming in on one area.

Exposure and response prevention. ERP involves gradually facing situations that trigger appearance anxiety, such as going out without heavy makeup or being in photos, while resisting rituals such as checking, camouflaging, or asking for reassurance. BDD is closely related to OCD, and ERP works in a similar way. A therapist with experience in this area, as described on the page about finding an OCD-informed therapist, can make a real difference.

Medication. Certain antidepressant medications, similar to those used for OCD, can reduce BDD symptoms for many people. They are prescribed and monitored by a physician or psychiatrist and often take several weeks to show benefit.

Other approaches. Acceptance and commitment therapy and compassion-focused approaches can help with shame and self-criticism. Family involvement can help loved ones stop providing reassurance.

The condition itself is explained on the page about body dysmorphic disorder.

How a Treatment Plan Is Chosen

A clinician will explore which areas of appearance are the focus, how much time is spent on preoccupations and rituals, how much insight you have into the beliefs, and how BDD affects work, relationships, and daily life. They will also screen for depression, suicidal thoughts, social anxiety, eating disorders, and substance use, which often occur alongside BDD. Severity matters. Moderate to severe BDD often benefits from CBT combined with medication. Some people have strong beliefs that the flaw is real, which can make them reluctant to seek mental health treatment. A good therapist works with this gently. BDD overlaps with broader body image distress, and a clinician will help clarify which applies.

What Progress Usually Looks Like

Early progress in BDD treatment often looks like spending less time checking or camouflaging, being able to leave the house more easily, and noticing that appearance thoughts are less gripping. Many people see meaningful improvement over several months of weekly CBT. Medication, if used, often takes eight to twelve weeks to show its full effect. Some preoccupation may remain, but it tends to take up less space and cause less distress. Setbacks, such as during stress or after triggering events, are common and are part of recovery.

Supporting Your Own Progress

Practising exposures and resisting rituals between sessions is central to treatment. Setting limits on mirror time, reducing photo checking, and cutting back on comparing yourself to others online can help. Telling trusted people how to support you, such as not offering reassurance about appearance, makes a difference. Spending time on activities that have nothing to do with appearance helps rebuild a fuller sense of self. It is also wise to pause any plans for cosmetic procedures while in treatment and discuss them with your therapist.

Common Questions About BDD Treatment

Which kind of therapy has the best track record for BDD?

CBT designed for BDD, including exposure and response prevention, has the strongest research support, sometimes combined with medication.

How does exposure and response prevention help with BDD?

ERP for BDD involves facing appearance triggers while resisting rituals like mirror checking and camouflaging, which weakens the BDD cycle.

What is perceptual retraining in BDD treatment?

It teaches people with BDD to look at their whole appearance in a mirror in a neutral, descriptive way instead of focusing on one perceived flaw.

Should I hold off on a cosmetic procedure while I am being treated for BDD?

Usually yes. Cosmetic procedures rarely relieve BDD and can sometimes make the preoccupation worse, so it is wise to pause and discuss them with your therapist.

Could a prescription from my doctor ease my BDD symptoms?

Certain antidepressant medications similar to those used for OCD can reduce BDD symptoms and are prescribed by a physician or psychiatrist.

Why is BDD treated like OCD?

BDD is closely related to OCD, with repetitive appearance-focused thoughts and rituals, so ERP and similar medications often help.

How long does it take to improve with BDD treatment?

Many people see meaningful BDD improvement over several months of weekly CBT, and medication often takes eight to twelve weeks.

How can I cut down on mirror checking during BDD treatment?

Set limits on mirror time, reduce photo checking, and practise resisting the urge with your therapist's guidance.

How can family members help someone in treatment for BDD?

Family can learn to stop reassuring about appearance and support the person in resisting BDD rituals.

What if I truly believe my appearance flaw is real?

Many people with BDD feel certain the appearance flaw is real. A good therapist works with this gently, focusing on the distress and rituals it causes.
