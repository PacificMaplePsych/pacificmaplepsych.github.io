Why Do I Feel So Angry Since Having a Baby

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, or you are worried you might act on your anger, call or text 9-8-8 (Canada's Suicide Crisis Helpline) at any time. If anyone is in immediate danger, call 911 or go to your nearest emergency department.

Feeling angry since having a baby is more common than many parents realize, and it is often a sign of being overwhelmed rather than a sign of being a bad parent. Severe sleep loss, constant demands, hormonal shifts, and changes in relationships and identity can leave people with very little buffer, so frustration flares quickly. Anger can also be a symptom of postpartum depression or anxiety, which often show up as irritability and rage rather than sadness. Both are treatable.

Parents describe snapping at a partner over small things, feeling rage when the baby will not settle, slamming doors, or feeling furious and then ashamed. Many hide these feelings because they do not match the image of new parenthood.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Sleep deprivation. Broken sleep reduces the brain's ability to regulate emotion, making anger quicker and harder to control.

Overload. Feeding, soothing, household tasks, and little time for yourself can leave you running on empty.

Unequal load. Feeling that you carry more of the work can lead to resentment toward a partner.

Loss of identity and freedom. Grief for your old life, work, or independence can come out as frustration.

Hormonal changes. Shifts after birth and during breastfeeding can affect mood and irritability.

A difficult birth. A traumatic or frightening delivery can leave people on edge and angry.

Feeling touched out. Constant physical contact can make any further demand feel unbearable, as described on the page about feeling touched out as a parent.

When It Looks More Like Postpartum Depression or Anxiety

Postpartum depression and anxiety do not always look like sadness or worry. For many parents, anger and irritability are the main signs. Consider whether you also notice:

  • Rage that feels out of proportion and hard to control.
  • Low mood, numbness, or feeling disconnected from your baby. The page on not feeling bonded with your baby explores this.
  • Constant worry or a sense of dread, similar to feeling anxious all the time since having a baby.
  • Trouble sleeping even when you have the chance.
  • Guilt, shame, or feeling like a failure as a parent.
  • Crying often, or withdrawing from others.

Postpartum mood and anxiety conditions can affect birthing parents and partners, and can begin anytime in the first year or so after birth. They are not a character flaw. Anger that is followed by shame often feeds a cycle of hiding and isolation, so reaching out is an important step.

What Can Help

  • Protect some sleep. Sharing night duties or arranging a longer stretch of sleep even a few times a week can reduce irritability.
  • Notice early signs. Tension, heat, or clenched jaws often come before anger. Stepping away when you notice them helps.
  • Put the baby down safely. If anger is rising, place the baby in a safe spot like the crib and take a few minutes to calm down.
  • Talk about the load. Discuss how tasks are shared and ask for specific help.
  • Make time for yourself. Even short breaks alone can restore some capacity.
  • Tell a health professional. Your doctor, midwife, or public health nurse can screen for postpartum depression and anxiety.

Common Questions About Anger After Having a Baby

How common is anger in the months after giving birth?

Yes. Many new parents experience anger due to sleep loss, overload, and big life changes. It becomes a concern when it is intense, frequent, or comes with other signs of low mood or anxiety.

What do parents mean when they talk about postpartum rage?

Postpartum rage is a term parents use for intense anger after birth. It is not a separate diagnosis but is often linked with postpartum depression or anxiety and is treatable.

Can postpartum depression show up as anger instead of sadness?

Yes. Irritability and anger are common signs of postpartum depression. Some parents feel angry far more than sad, which can delay recognizing depression.

Why do I get so angry at my partner since the baby arrived?

Sleep loss, an uneven share of tasks, and less time together can build resentment. Talking openly about the load and seeking support can reduce conflict.

What should I do if I feel angry at my baby?

Place the baby in a safe spot like the crib and step away to calm down. Feeling anger does not make you a bad parent. If you fear acting on it, reach out for help right away.

Can sleep deprivation make new parents irritable?

Very much so. Sleep loss reduces emotional regulation, making anger quicker and harder to manage. Protecting some sleep often helps noticeably.

Can partners experience anger and depression after a baby?

Yes. Non-birthing parents can also experience postpartum depression and anxiety, which may show up as irritability, withdrawal, or anger.

How long does anger after having a baby last?

It varies. Anger linked to exhaustion often eases as sleep improves. If anger persists or worsens over weeks, it is worth talking to a health professional.

Who can I talk to about postpartum anger?

Your family doctor, midwife, public health nurse, or a perinatal mental health professional can help. They hear these concerns often and can offer support without judgment.

How does therapy help with anger after having a baby?

Therapy can treat postpartum depression or anxiety, help you understand triggers, build strategies for managing anger, and reduce the shame that often surrounds it.
