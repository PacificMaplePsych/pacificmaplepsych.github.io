Finding an OCD-Informed Therapist

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

An OCD-informed therapist is a mental health professional with specific training and experience in treating obsessive-compulsive disorder, most often using exposure and response prevention (ERP), a form of cognitive behavioural therapy. Finding one matters because OCD works differently from general anxiety. Approaches that feel supportive, such as offering reassurance, analyzing the meaning of intrusive thoughts, or helping someone avoid triggers, can unintentionally keep OCD going. A therapist who understands OCD knows how to spot hidden compulsions, including mental ones, and guides people step by step toward facing fears without ritualizing. Knowing what to look for and which questions to ask can save time and frustration.

Why OCD Experience Matters

OCD involves unwanted, distressing thoughts, images, or urges, called obsessions, and behaviours or mental acts, called compulsions, that people use to reduce the distress or prevent a feared outcome. The page about what OCD really is explains the cycle. Compulsions bring short-term relief but teach the brain that the thought was dangerous, so the cycle grows stronger.

OCD can be hard to recognize. Many compulsions happen silently in the mind, such as reviewing, praying, counting, or mentally checking, as described on the page about mental compulsions. Some themes, such as fears of harming someone or doubts about a relationship, are frightening to share. A therapist without OCD training may misunderstand harm OCD as a risk of violence, or treat relationship OCD as a question to be answered. An OCD-informed therapist recognizes these themes, responds without alarm, and focuses on the obsession-compulsion cycle rather than the content of the thought.

What to Look For

Training in ERP

ERP has the strongest research support for OCD. Look for a therapist who describes specific training in ERP or CBT for OCD, not only general anxiety treatment. Some also integrate acceptance and commitment therapy or inference-based approaches.

Understanding of Reassurance and Mental Compulsions

An informed therapist will explain why reassurance-seeking and mental rituals keep OCD going and will help you reduce them gradually, rather than offering reassurance in sessions.

A Clear, Collaborative Plan

Good OCD treatment usually involves building a hierarchy of feared situations, practising exposures in and between sessions, and tracking progress. You should understand the plan and agree to each step.

Comfort With All OCD Themes

Whether your fears involve contamination, harm, sexuality, religion, relationships, or something else, an informed therapist will respond calmly and without judgment.

Coordination With Other Care

Medication prescribed by a physician can help some people with OCD. A good therapist can work alongside your doctor with your consent.

Questions to Ask

  • How much of your practice involves treating OCD?
  • Do you use exposure and response prevention?
  • How do you handle reassurance-seeking in sessions?
  • How do you work with mental compulsions?
  • How would you involve family members, if at all?

Common Obstacles

Specialized OCD therapists can be harder to find, and wait times and costs vary. Virtual therapy can widen options within BC. Some people feel too ashamed to describe their intrusive thoughts, especially taboo ones. It can help to know that OCD-informed therapists hear these themes often and understand that the thoughts are unwanted. Others worry that ERP will be overwhelming. In practice, exposures start small and move at a pace you agree to.

Some people have tried therapy before and found it did not help, often because it focused on talking through fears rather than changing the compulsion cycle. A different, OCD-specific approach may make a real difference. Family members may also unintentionally support compulsions by offering reassurance or helping with rituals. An informed therapist can guide families on how to respond.

When More Intensive Care Is Needed

If OCD is severely limiting daily life, if you cannot leave home or care for yourself, or if weekly therapy has not helped, more intensive options, such as more frequent sessions or specialized programs, may be recommended. If OCD comes with significant depression or thoughts of suicide, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, and talk with your doctor about additional support. OCD that begins after childbirth is described on the page about postpartum OCD.

Common Questions About Finding an OCD Therapist

Why does OCD need a therapist with specific OCD training?

OCD responds best to exposure and response prevention, and general approaches like reassurance or analyzing thoughts can keep the obsession-compulsion cycle going.

Which questions tell me whether a therapist really understands OCD?

Ask whether they use ERP, how much of their work involves OCD, and how they handle reassurance-seeking and mental compulsions in sessions.

Can a regular talk therapist make my OCD worse?

Sometimes. Well-meaning reassurance or long discussions about the meaning of intrusive thoughts can act like compulsions and strengthen OCD.

Will an OCD therapist be shocked by my intrusive thoughts?

No. OCD-informed therapists hear taboo and frightening intrusive thoughts often and understand that they are unwanted and do not reflect your intentions.

How does an OCD specialist work with rituals that happen only in my head?

They help you notice mental compulsions such as reviewing or counting, then gradually practise not doing them during exposures.

Is ERP for OCD done all at once or step by step?

Step by step. You and your therapist build a hierarchy and start with exposures that feel manageable before moving up.

Can I see an OCD-informed therapist online in BC?

Yes. Virtual sessions can widen your options, and many ERP exercises can be planned and practised by video.

Should my family be involved in my OCD treatment?

Often it helps. Family members can learn how to reduce reassurance and stop helping with rituals, with your agreement.

What if I tried therapy for OCD before and it did not help?

Earlier therapy may not have targeted the compulsion cycle. An ERP-based approach with an OCD-informed therapist may work better.

When does OCD need more than weekly therapy sessions?

If OCD severely limits daily life or weekly therapy has not helped, more frequent sessions or specialized programs may be recommended.
