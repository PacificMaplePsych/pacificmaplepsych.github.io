Why Do I Feel Like I'm Failing as a Parent

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You lose your temper at bedtime again. Dinner is from a box, screen time went way over, and your child is struggling with something you cannot seem to fix. At night you replay the day, listing everything you did wrong. Other parents seem calm, organized, and endlessly patient, and you wonder why it feels so much harder for you. Somewhere along the way, you started believing you are not a good enough parent.

Feeling like you are failing as a parent is extremely common. The fact that you worry about it usually says more about how much you care than about how well you are actually doing.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Exhaustion. Sleep deprivation and constant demands make everyone less patient. When you have nothing left to give, it is hard to parent the way you want to.

Unrealistic expectations. Social media, parenting advice, and your own ideals can set a standard no one could meet.

Comparison. Seeing only the best moments of other families can make your ordinary struggles feel like failure, especially if you tend to compare yourself to everyone around you.

A child with extra needs. Parenting a child with ADHD, autism, anxiety, or other challenges takes more energy and different strategies. Struggling does not mean you are doing it wrong.

Your own history. If you grew up with harsh criticism or unpredictable caregiving, you may be parenting without a model of what you want, which is hard.

Perfectionism. High standards and a sense of being never good enough can carry into parenting.

When It Looks More Like Parental Stress, Anxiety, or Depression

Parenting stress becomes a bigger concern when self-criticism is constant and starts affecting your mood, relationships, and enjoyment of your children. Depression and anxiety in parents are common, including well beyond the baby years, and they often show up as harsh self-judgment.

Signs that something more may be going on:

  • Persistent guilt and self-blame that do not ease after good days.
  • Frequently snapping at your kids and feeling unable to stop.
  • Feeling numb, detached, or unable to enjoy time with your children.
  • Constant worry about harming your child's development.
  • Feeling like your family would be better off without you.

If you are having thoughts of suicide or of harming yourself or your child, or feel unable to keep yourself safe, please call or text 9-8-8, the Suicide Crisis Helpline, available anywhere in Canada at any time, or call 911.

What Can Help

  • Aim for good enough. Children do not need perfect parents. They need parents who are mostly warm and responsive, and who repair after mistakes.
  • Repair after hard moments. Apologizing and reconnecting after losing your temper teaches children that relationships can recover.
  • Notice what you do well. Take a moment each day to recognize small things that went right.
  • Share the load. Ask for help from a partner, family, or friends, and accept offers of support.
  • Take breaks. Even short periods of rest can improve patience and perspective.

Common Questions About Feeling Like a Failing Parent

Why do I feel like a bad parent all the time?

Feeling like a bad parent often comes from exhaustion, unrealistic expectations, comparison, perfectionism, or depression and anxiety. Worrying about your parenting usually reflects how much you care.

Is parental guilt normal?

Yes. Most parents feel guilty at times. It becomes a concern when guilt is constant, harsh, and affects your mood or ability to enjoy your family.

What does good enough parenting mean?

Good enough parenting is the idea that children thrive with parents who are mostly warm and responsive, not perfect. Mistakes followed by repair can actually help children learn resilience.

How do I repair after yelling at my child?

Once you are calm, reconnect with your child, acknowledge what happened, apologize, and let them know it was not their fault. This models emotional repair and strengthens your relationship.

Can depression make me feel like a failing parent?

Yes. Depression often brings harsh self-criticism, guilt, low energy, and less enjoyment, which can make you feel like you are failing. Treatment can help both your mood and your parenting.

Why does parenting feel harder for me than for other parents?

You may be seeing only other families' best moments, parenting a child with extra needs, carrying more responsibilities, or dealing with stress, depression, or your own history. Everyone struggles in different ways.

How can I stop comparing my parenting to others?

Limit social media that triggers comparison, remind yourself that you see only snapshots of other families, and focus on what works for your own child and values.

Is parenting a child with ADHD or autism harder?

It often requires more energy, patience, and different strategies. Feeling stretched is understandable. Support, education, and connection with other parents can help.

Can my own childhood affect how I parent?

Yes. Early experiences shape how we respond to stress and emotions. Many parents work to do things differently from how they were raised, which can be challenging but is very possible.

How can therapy help parents who feel like they're failing?

Therapy can help reduce self-criticism, manage stress and emotions, address depression or anxiety, and build practical parenting strategies. It offers a space focused on your wellbeing too.
