Autism Assessment for Adults: What Age Is It For

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

There is no upper age limit for an autism assessment. Adult assessments are generally available from age 18 onward, and people are assessed in their twenties, forties, sixties, and beyond. Many adults seek assessment after a child or relative is diagnosed, after reading about autism, during burnout, or after years of feeling different without knowing why. Autism is lifelong, so the traits were present in childhood even if they were masked or missed. Being assessed later in life can still be meaningful, offering self-understanding, access to accommodations, and a framework for managing energy and wellbeing.

How It Works at Different Ages

Young adults. Assessment may be prompted by challenges with post-secondary education, first jobs, or independent living. Accommodations at school or work are often a goal.

Midlife. Many adults seek assessment after burnout, a child's diagnosis, relationship difficulties, or a growing sense that their experiences fit autism. The page about autistic burnout describes a common trigger.

Older adults. Later-life assessment can bring clarity to lifelong patterns. Clinicians take care to consider other explanations, such as hearing changes, health conditions, or cognitive changes, which may also need medical assessment.

Across all ages. Assessment looks at both childhood and current life, often with input from someone who knew you when you were young. If parents are not available, siblings, old friends, school records, or your own memories can help. The page about what an autism assessment appointment looks like explains the steps.

Practical Details

Who provides adult assessments. Registered psychologists with experience in adult autism, and sometimes psychiatrists, provide adult assessments. Ask about experience with your age group and with people who mask.

Public versus private. Public adult autism assessment options in BC are limited, so many adults choose a private assessment, as compared on the page about private versus public assessment.

Referrals and cost. Private assessments can usually be booked without a referral and are not covered by MSP. Some extended health plans cover part of the cost.

Teenagers. Youth under 18 are usually assessed through child and youth services or clinicians who specialize in that age group.

Co-occurring conditions. ADHD, anxiety, and depression are common at any age and may be assessed at the same time.

How to Prepare

  • Reflect on your history. Childhood friendships, school, sensory experiences, routines, and interests.
  • Find childhood sources. A parent, sibling, old report cards, or photos and diaries can help.
  • Describe masking. The effort you put into fitting in is important information, as explained on the page about autism masking.
  • Note current challenges and strengths. At work, home, and in relationships.
  • Think about your reasons. Self-understanding, accommodations, or support needs.

If you are still deciding whether to pursue assessment, the page about getting assessed for autism as an adult may help.

Common Questions About Age and Adult Autism Assessment

Is there an age limit for an adult autism assessment?

No. There is no upper age limit for an adult autism assessment, and people are assessed in their twenties, forties, sixties, and beyond.

Can I get an autism assessment in my fifties or sixties?

Yes. Adults in their fifties, sixties, and older can be assessed for autism, and a later diagnosis can still be meaningful.

At what age do adult autism assessments start?

Adult autism assessments are generally available from age 18, while younger people are usually assessed through child and youth services.

Why do some adults seek an autism assessment later in life?

Adults often seek an autism assessment after burnout, a child's diagnosis, relationship struggles, or years of feeling different.

What if my parents cannot share my childhood history for an autism assessment?

Siblings, old friends, school records, or your own memories can provide childhood information for an adult autism assessment.

How is autism assessment different for older adults?

For older adults, autism assessment carefully considers other explanations, such as hearing, health, or cognitive changes, alongside lifelong patterns.

What can a diagnosis offer someone assessed for autism after retirement age?

For many older adults, an autism diagnosis brings self-understanding, a new way to look at lifelong patterns, and better ways to manage energy and wellbeing.

Can teenagers have an adult autism assessment?

Youth under 18 are usually assessed for autism through child and youth services or clinicians who specialize in that age group.

Who provides autism assessments for adults in BC?

Registered psychologists with adult autism experience, and sometimes psychiatrists, provide autism assessments for adults in BC.

Does autism start in adulthood?

No. Autism is lifelong, so traits were present in childhood, even if they were masked or missed until an adult autism assessment.
