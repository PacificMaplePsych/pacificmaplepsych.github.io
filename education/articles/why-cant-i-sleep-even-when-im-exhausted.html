Why Can't I Sleep Even When I'm Exhausted

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You have been tired all day. You could barely keep your eyes open in the afternoon. Then you get into bed, and suddenly you are wide awake. Your body feels heavy but your mind starts racing, or you simply lie there, waiting for sleep that does not come. The clock ticks on, and the more you think about how tired you will be tomorrow, the more awake you feel.

Feeling exhausted but unable to sleep is frustrating and surprisingly common. Tiredness and the ability to fall asleep are controlled by different systems in the body, so it is possible to feel drained and still be too alert to drift off.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Stress and a busy mind. When your mind has been busy all day, bedtime may be the first quiet moment it has to process worries. Many people know the feeling of a brain that never turns off.

Being wired and tired. Long-term stress keeps the body's alert system switched on. You can be exhausted from stress but too activated to sleep.

Timing and habits. Napping, sleeping in, late caffeine, screens, and irregular bedtimes can weaken your natural drive to sleep at night.

Medical and hormonal causes. Pain, sleep apnea, restless legs, thyroid problems, night sweats, and some medications can all interfere with sleep. Perimenopause is a common cause for many people, as explored in Why Has My Anxiety Gotten Worse in Perimenopause.

Mood changes. Depression and anxiety both disrupt sleep. Some people struggle to fall asleep, while others wake up with racing thoughts in the early morning.

When It Looks More Like Insomnia

Insomnia is a sleep disorder involving difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early, at least three nights a week for three months or more, despite having the chance to sleep. It often starts during a stressful period and then continues after the stress has passed, because of habits and worries about sleep itself.

Signs that insomnia may be involved:

  • You regularly take more than half an hour to fall asleep, or lie awake for long stretches during the night.
  • You feel sleepy in the evening but alert as soon as you get into bed.
  • You worry about sleep during the day and dread bedtime.
  • You spend long periods in bed trying to catch up on sleep.
  • Poor sleep affects your mood, concentration, or daily functioning.

That last bed-and-alertness pattern is common in insomnia. Over time, the brain learns to link the bed with frustration and wakefulness, which keeps the cycle going.

What Can Help

  • Keep a regular wake time. Getting up at the same time every day, even after a bad night, strengthens your sleep drive.
  • Get up if you cannot sleep. If you are awake for a long time, go to another room and do something calm until you feel sleepy.
  • Wind down earlier. Give yourself time before bed to relax, and write down worries or tasks earlier in the evening.
  • Limit naps and late caffeine. Both can make it harder to fall asleep at night.
  • Stop trying so hard. Sleep cannot be forced. Letting go of the effort often helps it come more easily.

Common Questions About Being Too Tired to Sleep

Why am I tired but can't fall asleep?

Tiredness and the ability to fall asleep are controlled by different systems. Stress, a busy mind, poor sleep habits, medical issues, and insomnia can keep you alert even when you feel exhausted.

What does wired but tired mean?

Wired but tired describes feeling physically drained while your mind or body feels activated. It often happens with long-term stress, anxiety, or burnout, when the body's alert system stays switched on.

When does trouble sleeping become insomnia?

Insomnia is usually diagnosed when difficulty falling or staying asleep happens at least three nights a week for three months or more and affects daytime functioning, despite having the opportunity to sleep.

Why do I feel awake as soon as I get into bed?

When you have spent many nights awake in bed, the brain can start to link the bed with alertness and frustration. Getting up when you cannot sleep helps break that link.

How does CBT-I help when I am exhausted but cannot sleep?

CBT-I, cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia, is a structured therapy that targets the thoughts and habits keeping insomnia going. It includes regular sleep schedules, stimulus control, and addressing worry about sleep. It is a recommended first-line treatment.

Should I nap if I'm exhausted from not sleeping?

Long or late naps can make it harder to sleep at night. If you nap, keep it short and earlier in the day. People with insomnia often do better avoiding naps.

Can stress hormones keep me awake?

Yes. Stress raises hormones and nervous system activity that promote alertness. When stress is ongoing, these can stay elevated at night and make falling asleep difficult.

What should I do when I can't sleep in the middle of the night?

If you have been awake for a while, get up and do something calm and non-stimulating in dim light until you feel sleepy, then return to bed. Avoid checking the clock or your phone.

Are sleeping pills a good long-term solution?

Sleep medications can help in the short term, but they are generally not recommended for long-term use. CBT-I tends to have longer-lasting benefits. Discuss options with your doctor.

When should I see a doctor about not being able to sleep?

See a doctor if sleep problems last several weeks, affect daily life, or come with loud snoring, pauses in breathing, restless legs, night sweats, or low mood.
