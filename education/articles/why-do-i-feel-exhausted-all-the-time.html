Why Do I Feel Exhausted All the Time

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You wake up tired, drag yourself through the day, and collapse in the evening with no energy left for anything you enjoy. Weekends are spent recovering, and even holidays do not seem to fix it. Small tasks feel like climbing a hill. You might tell yourself you just need a good night's sleep, but that stopped working a long time ago.

Ongoing exhaustion is one of the most common reasons people see a doctor. It has many possible causes, physical and emotional, and often more than one is involved at once.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Medical causes. Anemia, thyroid problems, diabetes, vitamin deficiencies, long COVID, and many other conditions can cause fatigue. A check-up and blood tests are a good first step.

Sleep problems. Insomnia, sleep apnea, and irregular sleep can leave you drained, as explored in Why Am I So Tired During the Day Even After Sleeping.

Depression. Fatigue is one of the most common symptoms of depression, often along with low motivation and loss of interest.

Anxiety. Constant worry and a body that is always on edge use up a lot of energy.

Life load. Caregiving, parenting young children, shift work, and financial stress can leave little room for rest.

ADHD or autism. Constantly compensating for attention difficulties or masking social differences can be deeply tiring.

When It Looks More Like Burnout

Burnout is a state of physical and emotional exhaustion caused by long-term stress, most often related to work, though caregiving and other roles can lead to it too. It develops gradually and is not fixed by a single weekend off.

Signs that exhaustion may be burnout:

  • Exhaustion that has built up over months of high demands.
  • Feeling detached, cynical about your job, or resentful toward people you work with.
  • A sense that your efforts do not make a difference.
  • Feeling flat even on days off, with rest no longer restoring you.
  • Getting sick more often, or noticing headaches, stomach problems, and muscle tension.
  • Difficulty concentrating and making decisions.

Burnout and depression share many symptoms, and prolonged burnout can lead to depression. A professional can help you understand what is going on and what kind of support will help most.

What Can Help

  • Rule out medical causes. See your doctor to check for physical reasons for fatigue.
  • Reduce the load where possible. Look for tasks to delegate, drop, or postpone, even temporarily.
  • Protect sleep. Consistent sleep and wake times improve energy over time.
  • Take real breaks. Short breaks through the day and time fully away from work help recovery.
  • Move gently. Light, regular activity often increases energy, even when it feels counterintuitive.
  • Set boundaries. Limiting after-hours work and saying no to extra commitments protects your energy.

Common Questions About Constant Exhaustion

Why am I always exhausted no matter how much I rest?

Constant exhaustion can come from medical conditions, sleep problems, depression, anxiety, heavy life demands, or burnout. When rest does not help, it is worth seeing a doctor and looking at emotional causes too.

How do I know if my exhaustion is burnout?

Burnout usually builds over months of stress and includes exhaustion, detachment or cynicism, and feeling less effective. It is often tied to work or caregiving and does not lift with short breaks.

What medical conditions cause constant fatigue?

Common causes include anemia, thyroid problems, diabetes, vitamin deficiencies, sleep apnea, long COVID, and chronic illnesses. A doctor can check with an exam and blood tests.

Can depression cause physical exhaustion?

Yes. Depression often causes heavy, persistent fatigue along with low mood, loss of interest, and changes in sleep or appetite. Energy often improves as depression is treated.

Why does anxiety make me so tired?

Anxiety keeps the body's stress response active, which uses a lot of energy. Muscle tension, poor sleep, and constant worry all add to exhaustion.

Can caring for a family member leave me exhausted all the time?

Yes. Caring for a family member with illness, disability, or dementia is demanding and can lead to burnout, especially without breaks or support. Respite and support services can help.

Does masking autism or ADHD cause exhaustion?

Many autistic and ADHD adults describe deep fatigue from constantly compensating, masking, and managing sensory or attention demands. Recognizing this can help you plan for rest and support.

Will exercise help if I'm exhausted all the time?

Gentle, regular activity often improves energy and mood over time. Start small and build gradually. If exercise makes you feel much worse for days afterward, talk to a doctor.

Should I take time off work for burnout?

Sometimes a period of leave is needed for recovery. A doctor or therapist can help you decide. Lasting recovery usually also involves changes to workload or boundaries when you return.

How can therapy help with feeling exhausted all the time?

Therapy can help identify what is draining you, address depression, anxiety, or burnout, and build strategies for boundaries, rest, and sustainable routines.
