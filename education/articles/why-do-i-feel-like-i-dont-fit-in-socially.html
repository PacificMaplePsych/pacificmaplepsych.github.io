Why Do I Feel Like I Don't Fit In Socially

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You are at a gathering, surrounded by people who seem to know exactly how to talk to each other. Jokes land, conversations flow, and everyone seems comfortable. You feel a step behind, unsure when to speak, what to say, or whether you are doing it right. Even with people you like, you often feel slightly on the outside. It may have felt this way for as long as you can remember.

Feeling like you do not fit in is painful and more common than many people realize. Understanding why can help you find people and places where you feel more at home, and ease the sense that something is wrong with you.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Social anxiety. Fear of being judged can make you feel awkward and self-conscious, even when others do not notice anything unusual. It often leads to replaying conversations afterward.

Different interests or values. If your interests, humour, or values differ from those around you, it can be hard to find common ground.

Life changes. Moving, changing jobs, or reaching a different life stage than friends can leave you feeling out of step, as with loneliness after moving.

Past rejection or bullying. Being excluded or bullied earlier in life can leave lasting beliefs that you do not belong.

ADHD. Interrupting, losing track of conversations, or feeling rejected intensely can make social life harder.

Low mood. Depression can make you feel disconnected from everyone around you, even among friends.

When It Looks More Like Autism

Many autistic adults describe feeling like they do not fit in, often for their whole lives. This is not because they do not care about connection, but because their natural ways of communicating, processing information, and experiencing the world differ from what is expected.

Signs that autism may be part of the picture include:

  • Feeling different from others since childhood, sometimes described as feeling like an alien or outsider.
  • Finding unwritten social rules confusing, or taking things literally.
  • Preferring deep conversations about interests to small talk.
  • Feeling like you are performing in social situations, and exhausted afterward.
  • Sensory sensitivities, a need for routine, or intense interests.
  • Connecting more easily with other neurodivergent people.

Many autistic adults, especially women, are identified later in life. For many, understanding they are autistic brings relief and a new way of making sense of lifelong experiences.

What Can Help

  • Find your people. Interest-based groups, clubs, and online communities can connect you with people who share your passions.
  • Value quality over quantity. A few close, genuine connections often matter more than fitting into a large group.
  • Challenge harsh beliefs. Notice thoughts like "No one likes me" and look for evidence that balances them.
  • Be yourself in small steps. Sharing more of your real interests with safe people can build deeper connection.
  • Explore neurodivergent communities. If autism or ADHD may be involved, connecting with others who share those experiences can be affirming.

Common Questions About Not Fitting In Socially

Why have I always felt like I don't fit in?

Lifelong feelings of not fitting in can come from social anxiety, past rejection, different interests, ADHD, or autism. When it has been present since childhood, a neurodevelopmental assessment may help.

Is feeling like an outsider a sign of autism?

Many autistic adults describe feeling like outsiders all their lives. It can also happen for other reasons, so it is best understood alongside other experiences, such as sensory differences and social communication.

Can social anxiety make me feel like I don't belong?

Yes. Social anxiety can make you feel awkward and judged, which can lead to feeling like you do not fit in, even when others see you positively.

How can I find people I fit in with?

Interest-based groups, classes, volunteer work, and online communities can connect you with people who share your passions and values.

Why do I connect better with other neurodivergent people?

Many neurodivergent people share communication styles and experiences, which can make connection feel easier and more natural.

Can past bullying make me feel like I don't fit in as an adult?

Yes. Being excluded or bullied can leave lasting beliefs about not belonging, which can shape how you approach social situations as an adult.

Is it okay to have only a few friends?

Yes. Many people feel more fulfilled with a small number of close, genuine friendships than a large social circle.

Why is small talk so hard for me?

Small talk can feel pointless or confusing for some people, including many autistic people, who often prefer deeper conversations. Anxiety can also make it harder.

Can ADHD make it harder to fit in socially?

Yes. Interrupting, losing focus in conversations, and sensitivity to rejection can make social life harder for some adults with ADHD.

Can therapy help with feeling like I don't fit in?

Yes. Therapy can help with social anxiety, self-esteem, and self-acceptance, and an assessment can clarify whether autism or ADHD may be involved.
