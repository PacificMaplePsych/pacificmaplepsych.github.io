Why Do My Feelings About People Flip So Quickly

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

When feelings about people flip quickly, from warmth and admiration to hurt, anger, or disappointment, it usually means a strong emotional reaction is shaping how you see the whole person in that moment. Everyone does this to some degree when they feel let down. When it happens often, intensely, and mostly with the people you are closest to, it can reflect stress, attachment patterns, past relational trauma, or a pattern sometimes called splitting, which is common in borderline personality disorder. It is a painful experience, and it is one that people can learn to manage.

People often describe it this way: a friend feels like the only person who truly understands them, then one late reply or offhand comment and that same friend suddenly seems cold, selfish, or untrustworthy. Later, the warmth may return, leaving confusion and sometimes shame about the swing.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

A real change in the relationship. Sometimes a shift in feeling is a reasonable response to learning something new about a person. Not every change is a pattern.

Stress and exhaustion. When people are depleted, they have less room to hold mixed feelings, and small disappointments can feel much larger.

Rejection sensitivity. People who feel rejected intensely by small comments, including many adults with ADHD, may have sudden swings of hurt that colour how they see someone.

Attachment anxiety. An anxious attachment pattern can make any sign of distance feel like abandonment, prompting a quick shift toward anger or withdrawal.

Mood conditions. Periods of depression or elevated mood can change how people view others. If shifts come in longer episodes lasting days or weeks, mood swings may be a better fit than relationship-driven changes. The page on dramatic mood swings covers this.

When It Looks More Like a Borderline Personality Pattern

Splitting describes seeing someone as all good or all bad, with little room for the in-between. It is one way the mind protects itself from the pain of mixed feelings, especially when closeness feels risky. In borderline personality disorder (BPD), splitting often appears alongside:

  • Emotions that feel intense and change quickly, often within hours.
  • A strong fear of abandonment and efforts to prevent people from leaving.
  • Relationships that feel very close and then very painful, sometimes in a repeating cycle.
  • An unstable sense of identity, or chronic emptiness.
  • Impulsive actions during emotional distress.

These patterns often develop in the context of early environments where emotions were invalidated or relationships felt unpredictable, combined with a naturally high emotional sensitivity. They are not a choice or a character flaw. Borderline personality disorder is also far more treatable than older stereotypes suggest, and many people see major improvement with therapy.

Only a clinician can assess whether BPD is present. Many people experience splitting at times without meeting criteria for any diagnosis.

What Can Help

  • Pause before acting. When feelings flip, try waiting before sending a message or making a decision. Intense reactions usually soften with time.
  • Name the emotion, not the person. "I feel hurt and scared right now" is easier to work with than "they are awful."
  • Hold both sides. Deliberately recall something good and something difficult about the person. Practising "both can be true" loosens black-and-white thinking.
  • Look for the trigger. Noticing what happened just before the shift, such as a delayed reply or a perceived slight, helps you spot the pattern sooner.
  • Care for your baseline. Sleep, food, and lower stress make emotional swings less extreme.

Common Questions About Feelings About People Changing Quickly

Why do I go from loving someone to hating them so fast?

Strong emotions can take over how the whole person is seen in that moment. This can come from stress, rejection sensitivity, attachment fears, or a pattern called splitting, especially when the relationship feels important and uncertain.

What does splitting mean in psychology?

Splitting describes seeing people, or yourself, as entirely good or entirely bad, without the middle ground. It often protects against the pain of mixed feelings and is common in borderline personality disorder, though not limited to it.

Does idealizing people and then feeling let down by them mean I have BPD?

Not on its own. Many people idealize and then feel disappointed at times. Borderline personality disorder involves a broader, long-standing pattern, and only a clinician can assess whether it fits.

Why do my feelings about someone change after one small comment?

When a relationship matters a lot and trust feels fragile, a small comment can feel like proof of rejection. The emotional reaction can then colour the whole picture of the person until it settles.

How does black-and-white thinking about people affect relationships?

It can create cycles of closeness and conflict, leave others feeling confused, and make it hard to repair after disagreements. Learning to hold mixed feelings usually makes relationships steadier.

Can childhood experiences make me see people as all good or all bad?

They can contribute. Growing up with unpredictable or invalidating relationships can make it harder to trust that someone can be both caring and imperfect. Temperament and stress also play roles.

Is quickly changing how I feel about people the same as bipolar mood swings?

Usually not. Bipolar mood episodes last days or weeks and affect energy and sleep broadly. Feelings about people that flip within hours in response to interactions point more toward emotional or relational patterns.

What can I do in the moment when my view of someone suddenly turns negative?

Pause before acting, name the emotion you feel, and remind yourself of one positive and one difficult thing about the person. Waiting even a few hours often softens the reaction.

How does DBT help with splitting?

Dialectical behaviour therapy teaches emotion regulation, distress tolerance, and ways of holding two truths at once. These skills help people notice all-or-nothing thinking and respond more steadily in relationships.

When should I get an assessment for swinging feelings about people?

Consider an assessment if the pattern is long-standing, affects many relationships, comes with intense fear of abandonment or impulsive actions, or is causing you significant distress.
