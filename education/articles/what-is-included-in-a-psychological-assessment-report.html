What Is Included in a Psychological Assessment Report

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

A psychological assessment report is a written document that summarizes why you were assessed, the background information gathered, the tests and methods used, the results, any diagnosis, and specific recommendations. It brings together interviews, questionnaires, standardized tests, and sometimes input from family members or teachers into a clear picture of strengths and challenges. Reports are usually reviewed with you in a feedback session, and you receive a copy to keep. A good report should be understandable, explain how conclusions were reached, and offer practical next steps for home, school, work, and treatment.

What to Expect in a Report

  1. Reason for referral. The questions the assessment set out to answer, such as whether ADHD, autism, a learning disorder, or another condition explains current difficulties.
  2. Background information. Developmental, medical, educational, work, family, and mental health history gathered from interviews and records.
  3. Assessment methods. A list of the interviews, rating scales, and standardized tests used, and who provided information.
  4. Behavioural observations. Notes on how you approached the tasks, such as attention, effort, and communication, which help in interpreting results.
  5. Results. Scores and descriptions of areas such as intellectual abilities, memory, attention, academic skills, executive functioning, social communication, and emotional wellbeing, depending on the assessment.
  6. Summary and diagnostic impressions. How the findings fit together, whether diagnostic criteria are met, and other explanations that were considered.
  7. Recommendations. Specific suggestions for treatment, therapy, accommodations, learning strategies, and further assessment if needed.

Practical Details

Timing. Reports often take a few weeks to complete after testing, because scoring, interpretation, and writing take time.

The feedback session. The clinician typically meets with you to explain the results and recommendations before or when you receive the report, as described on the page about feedback sessions.

Length. Reports vary from several pages to much longer, depending on the type and complexity of the assessment.

Privacy. The report belongs in your confidential health record. It is shared with others, such as a doctor, school, or employer, only with your consent, except in rare situations where the law requires disclosure.

Accommodations. Schools, post-secondary institutions, and employers may ask for a report or a summary letter to support accommodations. Some will accept the recommendations section rather than the full report.

Cost. The report is normally part of the overall assessment fee. Ask the clinic what is included.

How to Use Your Report

  • Read it after the feedback session. Note any questions or anything that seems unclear or inaccurate.
  • Choose priorities. Start with the recommendations that matter most to you.
  • Share selectively. Decide who needs the full report and who may only need a summary.
  • Bring it to your doctor. This helps with medication decisions and coordinated care.
  • Keep it safe. You may need it later for school, work, or benefits applications.

Common Questions About Psychological Assessment Reports

What sections are included in a psychological assessment report?

A psychological assessment report usually includes the reason for referral, background, methods, observations, results, diagnostic impressions, and recommendations.

How long does it take to receive a psychological assessment report?

A psychological assessment report often takes a few weeks after testing, because scoring, interpretation, and writing take time.

Will someone explain my assessment results to me?

Yes. A feedback session is usually held to explain your assessment results, diagnosis, and recommendations in plain language.

Who can see my psychological assessment report?

Your psychological assessment report is confidential and is shared only with your consent, except in rare situations where the law requires disclosure.

Can I use my assessment report to get school or work accommodations?

Yes. Schools and employers often accept an assessment report or a summary letter to support accommodations.

What are behavioural observations in an assessment report?

Behavioural observations describe how you approached testing, such as attention, effort, and communication, which helps the clinician interpret assessment results.

Does every assessment report include a diagnosis?

Not always. An assessment report states whether diagnostic criteria are met, and sometimes the findings point to strengths or challenges without a diagnosis.

What should I do if something in my assessment report seems wrong?

Raise it with the clinician. Factual errors in an assessment report can often be corrected, and questions about interpretation can be discussed.

Do I need to share the full assessment report with my employer?

Not usually. Many employers only need a summary or the recommendations from an assessment report, so you can share selectively.

Is the assessment report included in the assessment fee?

The report is normally part of the overall assessment fee, but it is worth confirming with the clinic what is included.
