Why Do I Feel Tense in My Shoulders and Jaw

You notice it at the end of the day, or suddenly in the middle of a task: your shoulders are up near your ears, your jaw is clenched, and your teeth are pressed together. Maybe you wake with a sore jaw or a headache at your temples. A massage helps for a day, then the tightness comes back. It can feel like your body is bracing for something, even when you are just sitting at your desk.

The shoulders and jaw are two of the most common places people hold tension. Sometimes the cause is physical, but very often, stress and anxiety are playing a big part.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Posture and workstation set-up. Long hours at a computer or looking down at a phone can strain the neck and shoulders.

Teeth grinding. Grinding or clenching, especially during sleep, can cause jaw pain, worn teeth, and morning headaches. A dentist can check for signs and discuss a night guard.

Jaw joint problems. Temporomandibular disorders cause pain, clicking, or limited movement in the jaw joint and are worth assessing with a dentist or doctor.

Poor sleep. Sleeping poorly lowers pain tolerance and can increase clenching, and tension can in turn make sleep harder.

Other physical causes. Injuries, arthritis, and some medications can contribute to muscle tension or jaw clenching.

When It Looks More Like Anxiety

When you feel threatened, the body prepares to act by tightening muscles. Anxiety keeps this response switched on, so muscles never fully relax. Over time, the shoulders and jaw can become chronically tight, often without you noticing until they ache.

Signs that anxiety may be behind the tension:

  • Tension gets worse during stressful periods and eases on holidays or relaxed days.
  • You notice clenching when concentrating, worrying, or in difficult conversations.
  • You often feel on edge or unable to relax.
  • Other body symptoms appear too, such as headaches or stomach aches, shallow breathing, or a racing heart.
  • Your mind feels busy, and overthinking is common.

Muscle tension is actually one of the recognized symptoms of generalized anxiety disorder. Chronic tension can also feed anxiety, since the brain reads a tight, braced body as a sign that something is wrong.

What Can Help

  • Check in often. A few times a day, notice your shoulders and jaw. Drop your shoulders, let your teeth part, and rest your tongue on the floor of your mouth.
  • Try progressive muscle relaxation. Tensing and releasing muscle groups teaches the body what relaxation feels like.
  • Breathe from the belly. Slow, deep breathing helps calm the stress response and loosens tight muscles.
  • Move regularly. Gentle stretching, walking, and breaks from sitting reduce build-up.
  • Review your set-up. Adjusting screen height and chair position can reduce strain.
  • See a professional. A dentist, physiotherapist, or doctor can assess physical causes and treatment options.

Common Questions About Shoulder and Jaw Tension

Can anxiety cause shoulder and jaw tension?

Yes. Anxiety keeps the body's threat response active, which tightens muscles. The shoulders, neck, and jaw are common places for this tension to build up.

Why do I clench my jaw without realizing it?

Jaw clenching often happens automatically during concentration, stress, or sleep. Many people do not notice until they feel pain or someone points it out.

How do I stop clenching my jaw during the day?

Regular check-ins help. Let your teeth part, relax your lips, and rest your tongue gently in your mouth. Reminders on your phone or desk can prompt you to notice.

Is teeth grinding at night caused by stress?

Stress is a common contributor to night-time grinding, though sleep problems, certain medications, and dental factors can also play a role. A dentist can assess and suggest a night guard if needed.

Can muscle tension be a symptom of generalized anxiety disorder?

Yes. Muscle tension is one of the recognized symptoms of generalized anxiety disorder, along with restlessness, fatigue, irritability, trouble concentrating, and sleep problems.

Why do my shoulders creep up toward my ears?

Raised shoulders are part of the body's protective bracing response. Stress, cold, and long periods at a desk can all trigger it, often without awareness.

What is progressive muscle relaxation?

Progressive muscle relaxation is a technique where you tense and then release muscle groups one at a time. It helps you notice tension and teaches the body to relax more deeply.

Can jaw tension cause headaches?

Yes. Clenching and tension in the jaw and neck can cause tension headaches, often felt at the temples, forehead, or back of the head.

When should I see a doctor or dentist about jaw pain?

See a professional if jaw pain is persistent, if your jaw clicks, locks, or is hard to open, if your teeth show wear, or if you have frequent headaches.

Does therapy help with physical tension from anxiety?

Yes. Therapy can reduce the anxiety driving the tension and teach relaxation, breathing, and mindfulness skills. Many people notice physical symptoms ease as anxiety improves.
