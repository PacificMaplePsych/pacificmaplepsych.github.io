What Is Mentalization-Based Treatment (MBT)

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Mentalization-based treatment (MBT) is a structured, evidence-based psychotherapy developed by Anthony Bateman and Peter Fonagy, originally for borderline personality disorder (BPD). Mentalizing means understanding behaviour in terms of thoughts, feelings, needs, and intentions, both your own and other people's. Everyone mentalizes, but under intense emotion, especially in close relationships, the ability can drop away, leading to misunderstandings, jumping to conclusions, and impulsive reactions. MBT helps people notice when this happens and rebuild the ability to stay curious about what is going on in their own mind and in others' minds. Research shows MBT can reduce self-harm, hospital use, and other BPD symptoms.

How MBT Works

Mentalizing Under Stress

When people feel threatened, rejected, or overwhelmed, they may assume they know exactly what others think, such as "She is ignoring me because she hates me," or lose track of their own feelings. MBT views these moments as a temporary loss of mentalizing rather than a character flaw.

A Curious, Not-Knowing Stance

The therapist models curiosity by asking questions such as "What do you think was going on for him?" or "What were you feeling just before you sent that message?" rather than offering interpretations or advice.

Slowing Things Down

When emotions run high in a session, the therapist helps slow the conversation, "rewind" to a key moment, and explore it step by step.

Using the Relationship

Misunderstandings between client and therapist are explored openly, giving real-time practice in repairing relationships.

What Sessions Are Like

MBT often combines individual sessions with group sessions, usually over 12 to 18 months in its full form, though shorter and individual-only adaptations are also used. Early sessions focus on understanding BPD and mentalizing, building a shared formulation, and creating a crisis plan. Later sessions explore recent emotional events, relationship difficulties, and moments in therapy when mentalizing was lost. There is little homework compared with skills-based therapies. The focus is on building a flexible way of thinking about minds that carries into everyday life.

A Brief Example

Imagine someone tells their therapist that a friend did not reply to a message, and they responded by deleting the friend's number in anger. In MBT, the therapist might slow things down and ask what the person thought the silence meant, what they felt in their body at the time, and what else might explain the friend's silence. The therapist might also ask what the friend could have been thinking or feeling. The aim is not to decide who was right, but to help the person notice how quickly they reached a conclusion under strong emotion and to practise considering other possibilities. Over time, this kind of reflection becomes more automatic in everyday life.

Who It Can Help

MBT was designed for BPD and has the strongest evidence there. It has also been adapted for adolescents, families, people with antisocial personality patterns, eating disorders, and parents. Anyone who finds that relationships trigger intense, confusing reactions may benefit from mentalization-based work, as described on the page about why feelings about people flip so quickly.

How MBT Compares With DBT

Dialectical behaviour therapy teaches concrete skills for regulating emotions and tolerating distress, with structured homework. MBT focuses more on understanding minds and relationships, with less emphasis on formal skills. Both have good evidence for BPD, and some people benefit from elements of each. Common Questions About Mentalization-Based Treatment

What is MBT therapy in simple terms?

MBT therapy helps you pause during intense emotions and get curious about what you and others are really thinking and feeling.

Is mentalizing just another word for empathy?

Not quite. Mentalizing means understanding behaviour in terms of thoughts, feelings, and intentions, both your own and other people's, while empathy focuses on sharing others' feelings.

Who developed mentalization-based treatment?

Mentalization-based treatment was developed by Anthony Bateman and Peter Fonagy, originally for borderline personality disorder.

What does research show about MBT for self-harm in BPD?

Research shows mentalization-based treatment can reduce self-harm, hospital use, and other symptoms in people with borderline personality disorder.

Is mentalization-based treatment a long-term therapy?

Yes. Full mentalization-based treatment often lasts 12 to 18 months, though shorter adaptations are also used.

Should I choose MBT or DBT for borderline personality disorder?

Both have good evidence. DBT suits people who want structured skills practice, while MBT suits those who want to focus on understanding minds and relationships.

Does MBT combine individual and group sessions?

Often, yes. Full mentalization-based treatment usually combines individual and group sessions, though individual-only adaptations are also used.

Does MBT involve homework?

Mentalization-based treatment involves little formal homework compared with skills-based therapies such as DBT.

Can MBT help with relationship problems?

Yes. MBT can help with relationship problems by improving understanding of what you and others think and feel during conflicts.

Is MBT used for teenagers?

Yes. Mentalization-based treatment has been adapted for adolescents and families as well as adults.
