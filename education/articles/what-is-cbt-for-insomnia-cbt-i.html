What Is CBT for Insomnia (CBT-I)

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a structured, short-term treatment that helps people fall asleep more easily, stay asleep, and feel less anxious about sleep, by changing the habits and thoughts that keep insomnia going. It is recommended in major clinical guidelines as the first treatment for chronic insomnia, ahead of sleeping medication, because its benefits tend to last after treatment ends. CBT-I usually involves four to eight sessions and includes sleep scheduling, rebuilding the link between bed and sleep, calming a busy mind, and practical sleep habits. It takes effort, especially in the first weeks, but many people see significant improvement.

How CBT-I Works

Chronic insomnia often begins with a trigger, such as stress, illness, or a life change, but continues because of habits and worries that develop in response. People may spend longer in bed hoping to catch up, nap during the day, lie awake frustrated, or worry about how they will cope tomorrow. Over time, the bed can become linked with wakefulness and stress rather than sleep. The page about dreading going to bed describes how this can feel.

CBT-I targets these patterns with several components:

  • Sleep restriction or sleep compression. Temporarily limiting time in bed to the amount of time you actually sleep, then gradually increasing it. This builds sleep drive and makes sleep deeper and more consistent.
  • Stimulus control. Using the bed only for sleep and intimacy, getting up if you cannot sleep after about twenty minutes, and returning when sleepy, so the bed becomes linked with sleep again.
  • Cognitive work. Examining worries about sleep, such as "if I do not sleep eight hours, tomorrow will be a disaster," and developing more balanced expectations.
  • Relaxation techniques. Slow breathing, muscle relaxation, and wind-down routines to calm the body and mind.
  • Sleep hygiene. Adjusting habits such as caffeine, alcohol, screens, and bedroom environment, which support the other components.

CBT-I builds on the principles of cognitive behavioural therapy, adapted specifically for sleep.

What Sessions Are Like

CBT-I usually begins with a sleep assessment and a request to keep a sleep diary for one or two weeks, recording bedtime, wake time, time to fall asleep, night wakings, and how rested you feel. The therapist uses this to set a personalized sleep window. Weekly or biweekly sessions then review the diary, adjust the window, and introduce new skills.

The first two weeks can be challenging. Sleep restriction may leave you feeling more tired at first, which is a sign the process is building sleep pressure. People who drive or operate machinery need to take care during this phase and discuss safety with their therapist. Most people notice improvements within a few weeks, and the full course often takes six to eight weeks. CBT-I can be delivered individually, in groups, online, or through guided digital programs.

Who It Can Help

CBT-I is effective for chronic insomnia on its own and when insomnia occurs alongside depression, anxiety, chronic pain, or other health conditions. Improving sleep often supports mood and mental health, as described on the page about sleep and mental health. It can also help people who want to reduce or stop sleeping medication, with guidance from their physician.

CBT-I may need adapting or be delayed for some people, including those with bipolar disorder, seizure disorders, or untreated sleep apnea, since sleep restriction can affect these conditions. Anyone who snores loudly, stops breathing during sleep, or feels very sleepy during the day should be checked by a doctor for sleep disorders first.

Common Questions About CBT for Insomnia

Why is CBT-I recommended before sleeping pills for chronic insomnia?

CBT-I is recommended first because it treats the habits and worries that keep insomnia going, and its benefits tend to last after treatment ends.

What does sleep restriction in CBT-I involve?

Sleep restriction in CBT-I temporarily limits time in bed to how long you actually sleep, then gradually increases it as sleep improves.

Why does CBT-I ask me to get out of bed when I cannot sleep?

Getting up after about twenty minutes awake helps your bed become linked with sleep again, a CBT-I technique called stimulus control.

Why do I need a sleep diary for CBT-I?

A sleep diary shows your real sleep patterns, which your therapist uses to set a personalized CBT-I sleep window and track progress.

Will CBT-I make me more tired at first?

Many people feel more tired in the first week or two of CBT-I as sleep pressure builds, so take care with driving during this phase.

How quickly does CBT-I improve insomnia?

Many people notice better sleep within a few weeks of CBT-I, and a full course often takes six to eight weeks.

Can CBT-I help insomnia caused by anxiety or depression?

Yes. CBT-I is effective when insomnia occurs alongside anxiety, depression, chronic pain, or other health conditions.

Can I do CBT-I online instead of in person?

Yes. CBT-I can be delivered online, in groups, or through guided digital programs as well as in person.

Can CBT-I help me stop taking sleeping pills?

CBT-I can support people who want to reduce sleeping medication, with guidance from their physician on tapering safely.

Who should check with a doctor before starting CBT-I for insomnia?

People with bipolar disorder, seizure disorders, or signs of sleep apnea, such as loud snoring or stopped breathing, should see a doctor before CBT-I.
