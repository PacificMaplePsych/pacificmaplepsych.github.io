What Is a Feedback Session After a Psychological Assessment

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

A feedback session is a meeting held after a psychological assessment, in which the psychologist explains the results, whether any diagnosis was made, what the findings say about strengths and challenges, and what they recommend next. It is a conversation rather than a one-way presentation, so you can ask questions, share reactions, and clarify anything that feels confusing. Feedback sessions usually last about an hour and may happen in person or virtually. The written report is often provided at or soon after this meeting. For many people, especially adults assessed for ADHD or autism, the feedback session is an important moment of understanding.

What to Expect

  1. A reminder of the questions. The psychologist reviews why the assessment was done and what it set out to answer.
  2. An overview of results. Key findings are explained in plain language, often with examples from your own history and testing.
  3. Diagnostic conclusions. Whether criteria for a condition were met, which alternatives were considered, and why.
  4. Strengths and challenges. A balanced picture, including areas where you do well.
  5. Recommendations. Practical next steps, such as therapy, medication discussion with a doctor, accommodations, learning strategies, or further assessment.
  6. Time for questions. You can ask about anything, including how to share the results with others.

Practical Details

Timing. Feedback is usually scheduled a few weeks after testing is complete, once scoring and report writing are done.

Who can attend. Adults can bring a partner or support person if they wish. For children, parents usually attend, and the psychologist may share a child-friendly explanation with the child separately or at the end.

The report. The written report sets out the details discussed, as described on the page about what is included in an assessment report. Ask when you will receive it.

Emotional reactions. Relief, validation, surprise, grief, or disagreement are all common. It is okay to take time to absorb the results and follow up with questions later.

Format and cost. Feedback is usually part of the overall assessment, and may be in person or by video. Ask the clinic what is included.

How to Prepare

  • Write down your questions. For example, what the diagnosis means for daily life, which recommendations to start with, and who should see the report.
  • Consider bringing someone. A trusted person can help you remember details and offer support.
  • Take notes. Information can be a lot to absorb in one meeting.
  • Plan time afterward. Give yourself some space to process, especially if the results are significant.
  • Think about next steps. Who you may want to share results with, and what support you might want.

Common Questions About Assessment Feedback Sessions

What happens in a feedback session after an assessment?

In a feedback session, the psychologist explains assessment results, any diagnosis, strengths and challenges, and recommendations, and answers your questions.

How long is an assessment feedback session?

An assessment feedback session usually lasts about an hour, though it can vary depending on the complexity of the results.

When is the feedback session scheduled after testing?

The feedback session is usually scheduled a few weeks after testing, once scoring and report writing are complete.

Can I bring someone to my assessment feedback session?

Yes. Adults can usually bring a partner or support person to a feedback session to help remember details and offer support.

Do children attend their own assessment feedback session?

Parents usually attend the feedback session, and the psychologist may give the child a simple, age-appropriate explanation separately or at the end.

Will I get my written report at the feedback session?

The written report is often provided at or soon after the feedback session. Ask the clinic when you will receive it.

Is it normal to feel emotional after hearing assessment results?

Yes. Relief, validation, surprise, grief, or disagreement are common reactions to assessment results, and it is okay to take time to absorb them.

What should I ask during an assessment feedback session?

Ask what the findings mean for daily life, which recommendations to start with, and who should see the report from the feedback session.

Can I ask questions after the feedback session is over?

Many clinicians welcome follow-up questions after the feedback session. Ask how best to contact them once you have reviewed the report.

Can a feedback session be done virtually?

Yes. Many feedback sessions can be held by secure video, though some people prefer to meet in person to discuss assessment results.
