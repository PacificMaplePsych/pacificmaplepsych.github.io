Mindfulness-Based Therapies: MBSR and MBCT Explained

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) and mindfulness-based cognitive therapy (MBCT) are structured, usually eight-week group programs that teach people to pay attention to the present moment with openness and curiosity. MBSR was developed in the late 1970s to help people cope with stress, chronic pain, and illness. MBCT was developed later, combining MBSR with elements of cognitive behavioural therapy, specifically to help people who have had repeated episodes of depression stay well. Both involve guided meditation, gentle movement, and home practice. Research supports MBSR for stress and quality of life, and MBCT for reducing the risk of depression returning.

How These Approaches Work

Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)

MBSR is built on the idea that much stress comes from being caught in automatic reactions, such as worrying about the future, replaying the past, or tensing against discomfort. Mindfulness helps people notice these reactions as they happen and respond with more choice. Through practices such as the body scan, sitting meditation, and mindful movement, people learn to observe sensations, thoughts, and emotions without immediately trying to fix or avoid them. This can reduce the extra suffering that comes from struggling against difficult experiences, including chronic pain, as described on the page about the mind-body connection in chronic pain.

Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy (MBCT)

MBCT was designed for people who have recovered from depression but are at risk of it returning. Researchers noticed that, even after recovery, small dips in mood can trigger old patterns of negative thinking and rumination that spiral into another episode. MBCT teaches people to recognize these early patterns and step out of them, treating thoughts as passing mental events rather than facts. It combines mindfulness practice with cognitive understanding of how depression works. This fits well with relapse prevention planning.

What Sessions Are Like

MBSR Sessions

MBSR is usually taught in a group of around ten to thirty people, meeting weekly for about two to two and a half hours over eight weeks, often with a longer day of practice partway through. Sessions include guided meditation, gentle yoga or stretching, and group discussion about experiences. Participants are asked to practise at home for around forty-five minutes a day, often with audio recordings.

MBCT Sessions

MBCT follows a similar eight-week group format, with weekly sessions of about two hours and daily home practice. In addition to meditation, it includes exercises that explore how thoughts affect mood, identifying personal warning signs of depression, and creating an action plan for difficult times. Some programs are offered online, and some therapists adapt MBCT principles for individual sessions.

In both programs, the teacher guides practices and invites reflection, but the focus is on direct experience rather than discussion of personal history.

Who They Can Help

MBSR is used for stress, chronic pain, anxiety, illness-related distress, and general wellbeing. Research suggests it can reduce stress and improve quality of life for many people. The broader range of stress strategies is described on the page about stress management techniques.

MBCT has strong evidence for reducing depression relapse in people who have had three or more past episodes, and it is included in some clinical guidelines for this purpose. It can be an alternative or addition to ongoing medication for relapse prevention, discussed with a physician. It is usually recommended when someone is currently well or only mildly low, rather than in the middle of a severe episode. Treatment for current depression is described on the page about how depression is treated.

Mindfulness practice is not suitable for everyone in every situation. Some people with trauma, psychosis, or severe dissociation may find extended meditation distressing, and adapted or trauma-sensitive approaches may be better.

How They Compare

MBSR and MBCT share the same core practices and format. MBSR is broader, focusing on stress, pain, and wellbeing for a wide range of people. MBCT is more targeted, combining mindfulness with cognitive work to prevent depression from returning. Both differ from acceptance and commitment therapy, which uses mindfulness as one part of a values-focused approach delivered in regular therapy sessions.

Common Questions About MBSR and MBCT

Should I choose an MBSR course or an MBCT course?

It depends on your goal. MBSR focuses on stress, pain, and wellbeing, while MBCT combines mindfulness with cognitive therapy to prevent depression relapse.

How long does an MBSR or MBCT program last?

Both MBSR and MBCT usually run as eight-week group programs with weekly sessions of about two hours.

How much home practice do mindfulness-based programs require?

MBSR and MBCT usually ask participants to practise mindfulness at home for around forty-five minutes a day.

Who is MBCT designed for?

MBCT is designed for people who have recovered from depression, especially after several episodes, to reduce the risk of it returning.

Why was mindfulness-based stress reduction first offered to people with long-term pain?

MBSR was developed partly for people with chronic pain, since mindfulness can help people relate differently to pain and reduce the distress around it.

Should I start MBCT while I am severely depressed?

MBCT is usually recommended when you are well or only mildly low, not in the middle of a severe depressive episode.

Can MBCT be used instead of antidepressants to prevent relapse?

MBCT can be an alternative or addition to ongoing medication for relapse prevention, a decision made with your physician.

What happens in an MBSR class?

An MBSR class usually includes guided meditation, gentle yoga or stretching, and group discussion about mindfulness experiences.

Is mindfulness meditation safe for people with trauma?

Some people with trauma find extended mindfulness meditation distressing, so trauma-sensitive or adapted approaches may be better.

How is ACT different from MBCT and MBSR?

ACT uses mindfulness as one part of a values-focused therapy in regular sessions, while MBSR and MBCT are structured mindfulness programs.
