How Much Does Therapy Cost in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, private therapy usually costs somewhere between about $120 and more than $200 per session, depending on who you see. Fees vary mainly with the clinician's credentials and experience. Registered psychologists tend to charge the most, registered clinical counsellors generally charge somewhat less, and supervised doctoral students in psychology often offer the lowest fees. A standard session is usually about 50 minutes. Therapy with a psychologist or counsellor is not covered by MSP, but many extended health plans reimburse part of the cost, and some programs, such as ICBC, WorkSafeBC, and the Crime Victim Assistance Program, may pay for therapy in specific situations.

What Affects the Price of Therapy

  • Credentials. Registered psychologists have doctoral-level training and typically charge the highest fees. Registered clinical counsellors usually have a master's degree and often charge less. The differences between professions are described on the page about psychologists, clinical counsellors, and psychotherapists in BC.
  • Experience and specialization. Clinicians with many years of experience or specialized training, such as in trauma or couples work, may charge more.
  • Type of service. Couples sessions, longer sessions, and assessments are priced differently from standard individual therapy.
  • Format. Virtual and in-person sessions are usually priced the same, though virtual sessions save travel time and cost.

Ways to Pay Less

Extended Health Benefits

Many workplace and private plans reimburse therapy with a registered psychologist, and many also cover registered clinical counsellors. Coverage limits vary widely, so check your plan's annual maximum and which professions it includes, as described on the page about how extended health benefits work for therapy in BC.

Tax Credits

Fees paid to a psychologist that are not reimbursed can generally be claimed as a medical expense on your income tax return. Check current Canada Revenue Agency rules for other professions.

Programs That May Pay for Therapy

  • ICBC generally covers psychology treatment after a crash, as described on the page about psychological support after a car crash in BC.
  • WorkSafeBC may cover therapy for work-related injuries.
  • The Crime Victim Assistance Program may cover counselling for victims of violent crime.
  • Employee assistance programs often offer a small number of free sessions.

Lower-Cost and Free Options

Supervised student clinicians, community counselling agencies, and public mental health services through your health authority can reduce costs. Youth aged 12 to 24 can access free services through Foundry centres across BC.

Is a More Expensive Therapist Better?

Not necessarily. The relationship between you and your therapist is one of the strongest predictors of progress, and a good fit matters more than price. A supervised doctoral student can be an excellent choice for many concerns, while a psychologist may be a better fit for complex situations or specialized treatment. The page about how to choose the right therapist for you offers guidance, and the page about what MSP covers explains public coverage.

How Many Sessions Will I Need?

The total cost depends on how many sessions you attend. Some concerns improve within 6 to 12 sessions, while others benefit from longer support, as described on the page about how long therapy usually takes. Talking with your therapist about goals and frequency can help you plan.
