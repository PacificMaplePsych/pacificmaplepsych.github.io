Why Do I Feel So Lonely After Moving to a New City

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

You moved for a job, a relationship, school, or a fresh start. The new city was exciting at first. Then the novelty wore off, and you realized you had no one to call for a spontaneous coffee, no one who really knows you nearby. Weekends feel long. You scroll through photos of friends back home and feel a pang. Even when you meet people, the connections feel surface-level compared with what you left behind.

Loneliness after moving is extremely common, including for people who moved by choice and are glad they did. Building a life somewhere new takes time, and it is normal for it to feel hard along the way.

It Could Be a Lot of Things

Loss of your support network. Friends and family you relied on are suddenly far away, and phone calls cannot fully replace in-person connection.

Loss of routines and familiarity. Favourite places, daily habits, and knowing your way around all provide a sense of belonging. Without them, even simple tasks can feel tiring.

Friendships take time. Research suggests it takes many hours of shared time to turn an acquaintance into a close friend. That time simply has not happened yet.

Culture shock. Moving to a new country, or even a very different region, can bring differences in language, customs, and social norms that make connecting harder.

Social anxiety. Meeting new people can feel daunting, and nerves may lead you to avoid social situations that could help.

Low mood. Loneliness and depression often feed each other. If you have lost interest in things you used to enjoy, low mood may be part of the picture.

When It Looks More Like a Difficult Life Transition

Moving is one of life's major transitions, affecting relationships, identity, and daily life all at once. Many people go through a period of feeling lost after a big life change before things settle. Signs that the transition is weighing on you include:

  • Feeling homesick often, or idealizing the place you left.
  • Spending most free time alone, even when you would prefer company.
  • Feeling like an outsider or that you do not belong.
  • Questioning whether moving was the right decision.
  • Low energy or motivation to explore or meet people.

For most people, this eases as routines form and relationships deepen. If loneliness stays intense for many months, or you feel lonely all the time regardless of what you try, extra support can help.

What Can Help

  • Repeat, do not just try. Going to the same class, club, or café regularly builds familiarity, which is how friendships often start.
  • Join something structured. Sports leagues, volunteer groups, faith communities, and hobby clubs make it easier to meet people naturally.
  • Take the first step. Suggesting a coffee or follow-up plan often feels awkward, but most people are glad to be asked.
  • Keep old connections alive. Regular calls or visits with people back home provide support while you build new ties.
  • Explore your new place. Finding favourite spots helps the city start to feel like home.

Common Questions About Loneliness After Moving

Is it normal to feel lonely after moving to a new city?

Yes. Loneliness is one of the most common experiences after moving, even when the move was a positive choice. It usually eases as routines form and relationships develop.

How long does it take to make friends in a new city?

It often takes months, and deeper friendships can take a year or more. Research suggests close friendships need many hours of shared time, so regular contact matters more than one-off meetings.

Why do I feel homesick even though I wanted to move?

Homesickness reflects missing familiar people, places, and routines. It can happen alongside excitement about a new life and does not mean the move was a mistake.

Where can I meet people after moving somewhere new?

Classes, sports leagues, volunteer groups, faith communities, hobby clubs, and neighbourhood events are good places to start. Going regularly helps turn acquaintances into friends.

What is culture shock?

Culture shock is the disorientation that can come from adjusting to a new culture's language, customs, and norms. It often includes frustration, homesickness, and loneliness before adjustment sets in.

How can I make friends as an adult when it feels awkward?

Start with shared activities, show up consistently, and take small steps like suggesting a coffee. Most adults are also looking for connection and appreciate being invited.

Why do my new friendships feel shallow compared with old ones?

Old friendships have years of shared history. New ones have not had time to develop that depth yet. With time and regular contact, many new connections grow closer.

How can I cope with social anxiety in a new city?

Start with lower-pressure situations, prepare a few conversation openers, and focus on the other person rather than how you come across. Therapy can help if anxiety holds you back.

Can moving cause depression?

Moving is a major stressor and can contribute to depression, especially with loneliness, loss of support, or other life changes. Persistent low mood deserves attention and support.

Should I move back if I'm lonely after relocating?

Loneliness is often strongest in the first months and tends to ease. It can help to give it time and actively build connections. If you are still unhappy after a longer period, reassessing is reasonable.
