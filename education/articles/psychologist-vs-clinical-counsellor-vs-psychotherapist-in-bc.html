Psychologist vs. Clinical Counsellor vs. Psychotherapist in BC

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

In British Columbia, psychologists, registered clinical counsellors, and psychotherapists all provide talk therapy, but they differ in training, regulation, and scope. Registered psychologists usually hold a doctoral degree, are regulated by a provincial college, and can diagnose mental health conditions and conduct standardized psychological assessments. Registered clinical counsellors (RCCs) usually hold a master's degree in counselling or a related field and provide therapy. "Psychotherapist" is a general term in BC rather than a protected title, so it can describe professionals with very different backgrounds. All can be excellent therapists. The right choice depends on whether you need an assessment, the type of concern, your benefits coverage, and fit.

How Each Works

Registered psychologists. In BC, psychologists generally complete a doctoral degree in psychology, supervised training, and licensing exams. They are regulated by the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC, and "psychologist" is a protected title. They provide therapy, diagnose mental health conditions, and conduct assessments such as for ADHD, autism, and learning disorders, as described on the page about psychological assessments.

Registered clinical counsellors. RCCs usually hold a master's degree in counselling, psychology, or a related field with supervised clinical practice. In BC, the RCC designation has been granted through a professional association, and the province has been working toward formal regulation of counselling professions. Check current status if this matters to you. RCCs provide individual, couples, and family therapy for a wide range of concerns.

Psychotherapists. In BC, "psychotherapist" is not a protected title, so it may be used by psychologists, counsellors, social workers, or others with varying training. Unlike some other provinces, BC does not have a separate regulated psychotherapist profession. Always ask about specific credentials.

Others. Clinical social workers, psychiatrists, and some other professionals also provide therapy. Psychiatrists are medical doctors who can prescribe, as explained on the page about psychologists versus psychiatrists.

Practical Details

Assessments and diagnosis. If you need a formal diagnosis or a psychological assessment, a psychologist is typically the right choice.

Therapy. For therapy alone, psychologists, RCCs, and other qualified clinicians can all be effective. Experience with your concern and fit often matter more than the designation.

Benefits coverage. Extended health plans list which designations they cover. Many cover psychologists, and many also cover RCCs. Check your plan, as described on the page about extended health benefits for therapy.

Fees. Fees often differ by designation and experience. None of these private services are covered by MSP.

Referrals. You can usually book with any of these providers directly, without a doctor's referral.

How to Choose

  • Need an assessment or diagnosis? Look for a registered psychologist.
  • Need therapy for a specific concern? Look for a psychologist or RCC with relevant training and experience.
  • Check your benefits. Confirm which designations are covered and how much.
  • Verify credentials. Ask about degree, registration, and the body they are registered with.
  • Prioritize fit. Feeling understood and respected is one of the strongest predictors of benefit.

For more on what to look for, see the pages about choosing the right therapist and therapy versus counselling.

Common Questions About Psychologists, Clinical Counsellors and Psychotherapists in BC

What is the difference between a psychologist and a clinical counsellor in BC?

In BC, psychologists usually hold a doctorate, are regulated by a provincial college, and can diagnose and assess. Clinical counsellors usually hold a master's degree and provide therapy.

Is psychotherapist a protected title in BC?

No. In BC, psychotherapist is not a protected title, so it can be used by professionals with different training. Ask about specific credentials.

What does RCC mean after a counsellor's name?

RCC stands for registered clinical counsellor, a designation for counsellors in BC who usually hold a master's degree and supervised clinical training.

Which college oversees the psychologist title in BC?

Psychologists in BC are regulated by the College of Health and Care Professionals of BC, and psychologist is a protected title.

Can a clinical counsellor diagnose ADHD or autism in BC?

Formal ADHD or autism diagnosis through psychological assessment is typically done by a psychologist or physician rather than a clinical counsellor.

Is a psychologist better than a counsellor for therapy?

Not necessarily. For therapy, a psychologist or counsellor with relevant experience and good fit can be equally effective.

Does extended health cover registered clinical counsellors?

Many extended health plans cover registered clinical counsellors as well as psychologists, but coverage varies, so check your plan.

Can I book a registered clinical counsellor directly without seeing my doctor first?

Yes. You can usually book with a registered clinical counsellor or psychologist in BC directly, although some benefit plans ask for a referral.

How do I check a counsellor's or psychologist's credentials in BC?

Ask about the therapist's degree, designation, and registration body, and confirm registration with the college or association they name.

Are clinical counsellors regulated in BC?

In BC, clinical counsellors have been registered through a professional association, and the province has been working toward formal regulation. Check current status.
