What to Bring to Your First Appointment

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

For a first therapy or assessment appointment, it helps to bring a short list of your main concerns and goals, a list of current medications, any relevant past reports or diagnoses, your extended health benefits information, and a few questions for the clinician. If the clinic sent intake forms, completing them beforehand saves time. For virtual appointments, the most important things are a private space, a reliable device and connection, and headphones. You do not need to arrive with everything figured out. Clinicians are used to people feeling unsure or nervous, and the first appointment is as much about getting started as it is about information.

What to Bring

  • Notes on your concerns. What is bothering you, when it started, examples of how it affects daily life, and anything that makes it better or worse.
  • Your goals. Even a rough idea of what you would like to be different.
  • A medication list. Names and doses of prescriptions, supplements, and any recent changes.
  • Health information. Relevant medical conditions and the contact information for your family doctor or other providers, if you would like care coordinated.
  • Past reports. Previous psychological assessments, diagnoses, or discharge summaries, if you have them.
  • Benefits details. Your extended health plan information so you can submit receipts.
  • Questions for the clinician. About their approach, session frequency, fees, and what to expect next.
  • Identification or forms. Anything the clinic asked you to bring or complete.

Practical Details

For assessments. Assessments often benefit from extra material, such as school report cards, earlier testing, or questionnaires from family members. On testing days, bring glasses or hearing aids, snacks, water, and any usual medications unless advised otherwise. The page about preparing for an ADHD or autism assessment has more detail.

For couples therapy. Both partners can bring their own notes about what they hope will change. Agree on logistics, such as travel or a shared space for virtual sessions.

For virtual sessions. Test your video link in advance, find a private room or use a parked car, and keep headphones handy. You generally need to be in BC at the time of a virtual session with a BC clinician.

Referral letters. A referral is usually not needed to see a private psychologist, but bring one if your benefits plan requires it, as explained on the page about referrals to see a psychologist.

Payment. Ask the clinic in advance how payment works and whether receipts are provided for insurance.

How to Prepare

  • Confirm the details. Date, time, location or video link, and parking or transit.
  • Arrive or log in a few minutes early. This gives you time to settle.
  • Plan something gentle afterward. First sessions can bring up emotions, so a short walk or quiet time can help.
  • Remember you can go at your own pace. You only need to share what you feel ready to share.

To learn what typically happens once you are in the room, see the pages about initial consultations and treatment plans. If you are in crisis before your appointment, call or text 9-8-8 at any time, or call 911 in an emergency.

Common Questions About What to Bring to a First Appointment

What should I bring to my first therapy appointment?

Bring notes on your concerns and goals, a medication list, any past reports, your benefits details, and questions for the clinician to your first appointment.

Do I need to bring past assessment reports to a first session?

It helps to bring past assessment reports or diagnoses to a first session if you have them, but it is not required to get started.

What should I bring to a psychological assessment testing day?

On a testing day, bring glasses or hearing aids, snacks, water, any requested records, and take usual medications unless advised otherwise.

What tech setup is needed if my first appointment is by video?

Before a first virtual session, test the video link, find a private space, keep headphones handy, and make sure you are located in BC.

Should I bring a list of my medications to my first appointment?

Yes. A list of medication names, doses, and supplements helps the clinician understand your health at a first appointment.

Do I need a referral letter for my first appointment with a psychologist?

Usually not. Bring a referral letter to your first appointment only if your benefits plan requires one for reimbursement.

Is it okay to bring notes to read from in my first session?

Yes. Many people bring notes to a first session so they remember what they want to say, especially if they feel nervous.

What questions should I bring to ask a new therapist?

Bring questions about the therapist's approach, experience with your concern, session frequency, fees, and what to expect next.

Should couples bring anything to a first couples therapy appointment?

Each partner can bring notes about what they hope will change, and both should agree on logistics before the first appointment.

Do I need to complete intake forms before my first appointment?

If the clinic sends intake forms, completing them before your first appointment saves time and helps the clinician prepare.
