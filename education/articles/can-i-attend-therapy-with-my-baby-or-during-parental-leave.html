Can I Attend Therapy With My Baby or During Parental Leave

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

If you are having thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, or you feel confused or out of touch with reality, seek help right away. Call or text 9-8-8 (Canada's Suicide Crisis Helpline), call 911, or go to the nearest emergency department.

Yes, many people attend therapy during parental leave, and it can be an especially helpful time to get support. Some therapists are comfortable with a young baby being present during sessions, particularly babies who are not yet mobile, and virtual sessions can make it easier to attend from home around feeds and naps. It is worth asking the clinic about their approach before booking. Parental leave can bring joy alongside exhaustion, isolation, identity changes, relationship strain, and sometimes postpartum depression or anxiety. Getting support during this time is a positive step for both you and your baby.

How It Works

Babies in the room. When a therapist is open to it, a young baby can sit on your lap, nap in a car seat, or feed during the session. You can pause whenever your baby needs you. As babies become mobile, many parents find it easier to arrange care or switch to virtual sessions.

Virtual sessions. Attending from home removes travel and lets you respond to your baby. Choose a quiet spot and have supplies nearby.

Flexibility. Sessions may be interrupted, and that is okay. Therapists who work with new parents expect this.

What therapy focuses on. Common themes include sleep deprivation, adjusting to a new identity, birth experiences, intrusive thoughts, relationship changes, returning to work, and conditions such as postpartum anxiety and postpartum depression.

Practical Details

Ask about policies. Policies about babies in sessions vary. Some therapists welcome infants, while others prefer sessions without children so you can focus fully.

Older babies and toddlers. Once a baby is crawling or very active, it can be harder to focus. Childcare, a partner's help, or nap-time virtual sessions may work better.

Location. Virtual sessions with a BC clinician generally require you to be in BC at the time of the session.

Timing. Booking around your baby's most predictable nap or a time when a partner is home can help.

Fees and benefits. Coverage varies. If you are on leave, your extended health plan may still be active, so check with your employer or plan provider. Ask the clinic about fees.

Partners. Partners can sometimes join a session, and the page about perinatal and hormonal mental health support describes what therapy at this stage often involves.

How to Prepare

  • Ask the clinic in advance. Whether your baby can attend, and whether virtual sessions are available.
  • Pack essentials. Feeding supplies, a change of clothes, a favourite toy, or a carrier.
  • Set up your space. For virtual sessions, a quiet room with your baby's things nearby.
  • Write down what you want to talk about. Interruptions are easier to manage when you have a few notes.
  • Be gentle with yourself. Arriving late or needing to pause is understandable.

Common Questions About Therapy With a Baby or During Parental Leave

Do therapists allow infants in the room during sessions?

Many therapists are comfortable with a young baby in a session, especially before the baby is mobile. Ask the clinic about its policy before booking.

Is parental leave a good time to start therapy?

Parental leave can be a helpful time to start therapy, since many new parents face exhaustion, isolation, identity changes, or postpartum mood concerns.

Can I breastfeed or bottle-feed my baby during therapy?

Yes. If your baby attends therapy, feeding during the session is generally fine, and you can pause whenever your baby needs you.

Are virtual sessions easier for parents of newborns?

Many parents of newborns find virtual sessions easier, since they avoid travel and can be scheduled around feeds and naps.

What if my baby interrupts the therapy session?

Interruptions are expected when a baby attends therapy. Therapists who work with new parents are used to pausing and picking up again.

Should I arrange childcare once my baby is crawling?

Once a baby is crawling or very active, childcare, a partner's help, or nap-time virtual sessions may make it easier to focus in therapy.

Does my extended health plan still work during parental leave?

Many extended health plans stay active during parental leave, but this varies, so check with your employer or plan provider.

What do new parents usually talk about in therapy?

New parents often talk about sleep loss, identity changes, birth experiences, intrusive thoughts, relationship strain, and returning to work.

Can therapy during parental leave help with postpartum depression?

Yes. Therapy during parental leave can help with postpartum depression and anxiety, sometimes alongside medical care.

What should I bring if my baby comes to therapy with me?

Bring feeding supplies, a change of clothes, a favourite toy, or a carrier, and a few notes about what you want to discuss with your baby present.
