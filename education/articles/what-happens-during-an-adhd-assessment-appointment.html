What Happens During an ADHD Assessment Appointment

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

During an ADHD assessment appointment, the clinician usually conducts a detailed interview about your attention, organization, restlessness, impulsivity, and how these have affected you from childhood to now. You will complete rating scales, and someone who knows you well may be asked to complete them too. Depending on the assessment, there may also be standardized tasks measuring attention, memory, and executive functioning, along with screening for anxiety, depression, sleep problems, learning differences, or autism that can affect attention. Most assessments take more than one appointment. There are no right or wrong answers, and the goal is an accurate, complete picture of how your mind works.

What to Expect

Clinical interview. Questions about school history, work, relationships, daily routines, time management, emotional regulation, and coping strategies. The clinician looks for symptoms present before age 12 and across more than one setting, as described on the page about how ADHD is diagnosed in adults.

Rating scales. Standardized questionnaires about current and childhood symptoms, completed by you and often by a partner, parent, or close friend.

Standardized tasks. Some assessments include computer-based attention tests or tasks measuring working memory, processing speed, and planning. These add information but cannot diagnose ADHD on their own.

Ruling out other explanations. Anxiety, depression, trauma, sleep problems, substance use, thyroid issues, and learning disorders can all affect attention. The clinician considers whether these explain your difficulties, or occur alongside ADHD, as discussed on the page about ADHD versus anxiety.

Feedback and report. After all appointments, you receive a feedback session and written report with findings and recommendations.

Practical Details

Number and length of appointments. Assessments often involve several appointments, with interviews of one to two hours and testing sessions that may be longer. The overall timeline is covered on the page about how long an ADHD or autism assessment takes.

Virtual or in person. Interviews can often be done virtually. Some testing works best in person.

Medication. If you already take ADHD medication, ask whether to take it on testing days.

Breaks. You can ask for breaks, water, or snacks during long appointments.

Fees and referrals. Private ADHD assessments can usually be booked without a referral and are not covered by MSP. Ask the clinic about fees.

Presentations. ADHD may be primarily inattentive, primarily hyperactive-impulsive, or combined, as explained on the page about the three ADHD presentations.

How to Prepare

  • Write down examples. Specific situations at school, work, and home where attention or organization caused problems.
  • Gather childhood records. Report cards and teacher comments are especially useful.
  • Ask a family member for input. They may remember childhood behaviours you do not.
  • Note coping strategies. Workarounds you use, such as alarms, lists, or overworking, can hide difficulties.
  • Rest and eat before testing. Being well rested helps results reflect your usual functioning.
  • Be honest. Do not try to look better or worse than usual.

Common Questions About ADHD Assessment Appointments

What does an ADHD assessment appointment involve from start to finish?

An ADHD assessment appointment usually includes a detailed interview, rating scales, sometimes standardized attention tasks, and screening for other conditions.

Can a computer test diagnose ADHD?

No. Computer-based attention tests add information to an ADHD assessment, but they cannot diagnose ADHD on their own.

Why does an ADHD assessment ask about symptoms before age 12?

ADHD is developmental, so diagnostic criteria require that some symptoms were present before age 12, even if they went unnoticed.

Who else fills out questionnaires for an adult ADHD assessment?

A partner, parent, or close friend is often asked to complete rating scales for an adult ADHD assessment, alongside your own.

How does an ADHD assessment rule out anxiety or depression?

The clinician considers whether anxiety, depression, sleep problems, or other conditions explain attention difficulties or occur alongside ADHD.

How long is each ADHD assessment appointment?

ADHD assessment interviews often last one to two hours, and testing sessions may be longer, usually across several appointments.

Can I take breaks during an ADHD assessment?

Yes. You can usually ask for breaks, water, or snacks during long ADHD assessment appointments.

Should I take my ADHD medication on the day of my assessment?

Ask the clinic whether to take ADHD medication on assessment days, since instructions can differ depending on the testing involved.

Why do coping strategies matter in an ADHD assessment?

Coping strategies like alarms, lists, or overworking can hide ADHD difficulties, so describing them helps the clinician understand your experience.

Could an ADHD evaluation end without an ADHD diagnosis?

Yes. An ADHD evaluation may find ADHD of a particular presentation, another condition, a combination, or no diagnosis, with recommendations in each case.
