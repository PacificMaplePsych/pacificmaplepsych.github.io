Do You Provide Support for Substance Use Concerns

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Stopping alcohol or some other substances suddenly after heavy, regular use can cause dangerous withdrawal. Talk to a doctor or call 8-1-1 before making major changes. In an emergency, including a suspected overdose, call 911. If you are having thoughts of suicide, call or text 9-8-8 at any time.

Outpatient therapy can help with many substance use concerns, particularly mild to moderate patterns of drinking, cannabis use, or other substance use, and use that is tied to stress, anxiety, depression, trauma, or relationship difficulties. Therapy may focus on cutting back, stopping, or reducing harm, depending on your goals. Some situations need more than weekly therapy, such as physical dependence that requires medically supervised withdrawal, frequent overdoses, or use that is putting your safety at serious risk. The best way to find out whether a particular clinic's services fit your needs is to contact the clinic and describe your situation. The clinician can then discuss whether therapy there, other services, or a combination would help most.

How It Works

Assessment. A clinician asks about what you use, how often, how it affects your life, what you have tried, and what you would like to change. They will also ask about mental health and safety.

Setting goals. Goals may include reducing use, stopping altogether, or using more safely. A harm reduction approach meets people where they are.

Therapy approaches. Motivational interviewing, cognitive behavioural therapy, acceptance-based approaches, and relapse prevention are commonly used, as described on the page about therapy approaches for substance use.

Addressing underlying concerns. Many people use substances to cope with anxiety, low mood, or trauma. Treating these alongside substance use often helps both, as discussed on the page about substance use as a coping mechanism.

Practical Details

When outpatient therapy may fit. You are medically stable, can attend regular sessions, and want help understanding and changing your use.

When more support may be needed. You have physical withdrawal symptoms, have tried to stop and could not, have had an overdose, or your use is causing serious harm. Medically supervised withdrawal, addiction medicine, or intensive programs may be recommended. A family doctor can help with referrals. For practical ideas on reducing alcohol use, see the page about cutting back on drinking.

Medication. Medications for alcohol or opioid use disorders are prescribed by physicians or nurse practitioners and can work well alongside therapy.

Confidentiality. Information about substance use is kept confidential, with limited legal exceptions, such as serious risk of harm to yourself or others or concerns about a child's safety.

Fees and coverage. Private therapy is not covered by MSP. Check your extended health plan and ask the clinic about fees. Publicly funded substance use services are also available through BC health authorities.

How to Prepare

  • Track your use for a week or two. Note what, how much, when, and why. This helps with assessment and goal setting.
  • Think about your goals. Cutting back, stopping, or using more safely are all valid starting points.
  • Note other concerns. Sleep, mood, anxiety, or relationship stress that may be linked.
  • Talk to your doctor. Especially before stopping alcohol or other substances after heavy use.
  • Be honest. Clinicians are there to help, not to judge.

Common Questions About Getting Support for Substance Use

Can outpatient therapy help with substance use concerns?

Yes. Outpatient therapy can help with many substance use concerns, especially mild to moderate use and use linked to stress, anxiety, depression, or trauma.

How do I know if a clinic can help with my substance use?

Contact the clinic and describe your substance use and goals. The clinician can tell you whether their services fit or suggest other options.

Can my goal in substance use therapy be cutting back rather than abstinence?

Yes. Therapy for substance use can focus on cutting back, stopping, or using more safely, depending on your goals.

When is weekly therapy not enough for substance use?

Weekly therapy may not be enough when there is physical withdrawal, repeated overdoses, failed attempts to stop, or serious harm from substance use.

Why is medical advice important before quitting alcohol after heavy use?

Stopping alcohol suddenly after heavy, regular use can cause dangerous withdrawal, so talk to a doctor or call 8-1-1 first.

What therapies are used for substance use concerns?

Motivational interviewing, CBT, acceptance-based approaches, and relapse prevention are common therapies for substance use concerns.

Is what I share about drug use in therapy confidential?

Information about drug use shared in therapy is confidential, with limited legal exceptions such as serious risk of harm or child safety concerns.

Can therapy treat anxiety and substance use together?

Yes. Treating anxiety, depression, or trauma alongside substance use often helps both, since many people use substances to cope.

Can prescriptions support recovery from alcohol use disorder?

Yes. Physicians or nurse practitioners can prescribe medications for alcohol use disorder, which often work well alongside therapy.

Are there public substance use services in BC?

Yes. BC health authorities offer publicly funded substance use services, and a family doctor can help with referrals.
