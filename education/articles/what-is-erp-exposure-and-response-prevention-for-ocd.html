What Is ERP (Exposure and Response Prevention) for OCD

This page reflects our understanding of mental health topics as of 2026. If you are reading this at a later date, some details may no longer be current, so please verify anything important against up to date sources. If you notice anything that needs updating, we would welcome you letting us know.

Exposure and response prevention (ERP) is a form of cognitive behavioural therapy and the first-line psychological treatment for obsessive-compulsive disorder. It involves gradually and deliberately facing the thoughts, images, objects, or situations that trigger obsessions (exposure), while choosing not to perform the compulsions that usually follow (response prevention). Over time, this teaches the brain that anxiety and uncertainty can be tolerated without rituals, and that feared outcomes are much less likely than OCD insists. ERP is done collaboratively, step by step, at a pace the person agrees to. Research shows many people experience substantial improvement with ERP.

How ERP Works

OCD runs on a cycle. An intrusive thought, image, or urge triggers anxiety or discomfort. A compulsion, such as washing, checking, seeking reassurance, or mentally reviewing, brings temporary relief. That relief teaches the brain that the compulsion was necessary, so the cycle grows stronger. Many compulsions are hidden, as described on the page about mental compulsions.

ERP breaks the cycle. By facing triggers without ritualizing, people learn several things: anxiety rises and then falls on its own, they can cope with discomfort and uncertainty, and the feared outcome usually does not happen. Researchers describe this as inhibitory learning, where new, safer learning competes with old fear. ERP also targets avoidance, which keeps fears untested.

ERP works for many OCD themes, including contamination, as described on the page about OCD and contamination fears, harm-related fears described on the page about harm OCD, and symmetry, religious, sexual, and relationship themes.

What Sessions Are Like

ERP begins with understanding your OCD, including obsessions, compulsions, triggers, and avoidance. Together, you and your therapist build a hierarchy, a list of exposures ranked from mildly to very anxiety-provoking. For contamination fears, the list might start with touching a doorknob and washing a little later than usual, and build toward touching a public surface without washing at all.

During sessions, you practise exposures with your therapist's support, staying with the discomfort while resisting compulsions. For intrusive thoughts, exposures may involve writing scripts about feared thoughts or saying feared words aloud. Your therapist helps you notice and resist hidden rituals, such as mental reviewing or seeking reassurance. Between sessions, you practise exposures in daily life, since repetition strengthens learning.

ERP often involves around twelve to twenty weekly sessions, though this varies. Some people attend intensive programs with more frequent sessions. It is normal to feel anxious during exposures. The discomfort is part of how change happens, and the pace is always agreed together. ERP is often combined with acceptance-based ideas and, for some people, medication prescribed by a physician.

Who It Can Help

ERP is recommended for adults, teens, and children with OCD, and it is also used for related conditions such as body dysmorphic disorder and illness anxiety. It can help with all OCD themes, including those that involve mostly mental rituals. Family involvement can support recovery, especially when family members have been providing reassurance or helping with rituals.

ERP may need adjustment or be combined with other treatment when OCD occurs with severe depression, trauma, or substance use. Finding a therapist with specific ERP training matters, as described on the page about finding an OCD-informed therapist. The full range of options is explained on the page about how OCD is treated.

Common Questions About ERP for OCD

What do the two parts of ERP, exposure and response prevention, each involve?

Exposure means gradually facing OCD triggers, and response prevention means choosing not to do the compulsions that usually follow.

Why does ERP work for OCD?

ERP teaches the brain that anxiety and uncertainty can be tolerated without rituals and that feared outcomes are less likely than OCD insists.

What is an ERP hierarchy?

An ERP hierarchy is a list of OCD exposures ranked from mildly to very anxiety-provoking, used to guide treatment step by step.

How does ERP work for intrusive thoughts without physical rituals?

For intrusive thoughts, ERP may involve writing scripts or saying feared words, while resisting mental rituals such as reviewing or neutralizing.

Is ERP for OCD too scary to try?

ERP starts with manageable exposures, and you agree on each step with your therapist, so OCD treatment moves at a pace you can handle.

Can ERP help with OCD reassurance-seeking?

Yes. ERP helps you gradually reduce reassurance-seeking, since reassurance acts as a compulsion that keeps OCD going.

How many weekly sessions does ERP for OCD typically involve?

ERP for OCD often involves around twelve to twenty weekly sessions, and some people attend more intensive programs.

Is ERP used for children with OCD?

Yes. ERP is recommended for children and teens with OCD, often with parents involved in supporting exposures at home.

Can ERP be combined with medication for OCD?

Yes. ERP is sometimes combined with medication prescribed by a physician, especially for more severe OCD.

What conditions besides OCD can ERP help?

ERP is also used for related conditions such as body dysmorphic disorder and illness anxiety, which share exposure-based treatment principles.
