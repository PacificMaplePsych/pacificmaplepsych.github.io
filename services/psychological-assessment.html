Why get a psychological assessment?

Psychological assessments identify the root causes of what you or your child are experiencing. They guide effective next steps, including accurate diagnosis, treatment planning, and the right supports at school, at work, and at home.

The benefits extend beyond immediate needs. With a careful evaluation and written report, you gain insight into possible contributors to stress, trauma, or other psychological challenges, and a framework for understanding neurodevelopmental differences such as ADHD, learning disorders (for example, dyslexia and dyscalculia), intellectual functioning, and cognitive giftedness.

You will receive a clear written report and practical recommendations that can support planning with schools, employers, and healthcare providers.

Types of assessment

Not sure which one you need? That is common. Complete the intake form and our team will recommend the right assessment for your questions.

Mental health assessment

Teens and adults

A foundational evaluation for anyone seeking a deeper understanding of themselves, regardless of how severe their concerns are. The report clarifies symptoms and diagnostic considerations so you can respond more effectively to stress.

  • Mood and anxiety disorders
  • PTSD and trauma
  • OCD, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia
  • Phobias and other concerns

Psychoeducational assessment

Children, teens, and adults

A detailed cognitive profile exploring the relationship between cognitive abilities and academic achievement. Psychoeducational and intellectual assessment is a particular specialty of our team, and children and youth can be assessed by school psychologist Ilona Oszadszky.

  • Learning disorders such as dyslexia, dyscalculia, and dysgraphia
  • Intellectual functioning and giftedness
  • Recommendations for school, IEPs, and post-secondary
Book this assessment

ADHD assessment

Children, teens, and adults

A careful, comprehensive process including a clinical interview, standardized questionnaires compared to established norms, and evaluation of executive functioning, so clinicians can reach well-supported conclusions and practical recommendations.

  • Attention, focus, and organization
  • Executive functioning in daily life
  • Ruling out other explanations such as anxiety or trauma

Integrated assessment

Children, teens, and adults

Combines several of the approaches above, for example an ADHD assessment alongside a mental health or psychoeducational assessment, when several questions need answering at once. The scope is tailored to your presentation, goals, and questions.

  • One coordinated process
  • Tailored level of detail
  • Collaboration with everyone involved

Independent medical evaluation

Adults

Also called psycho-legal assessments, IMEs provide an objective psychological opinion for insurance, legal, or workplace matters. Our psychologists provide comprehensive, unbiased reports that address the referral questions, completed efficiently without compromising quality.

  • Insurance and disability claims
  • Legal and workplace matters
  • Detailed written report

The assessment process

Most assessments include the steps below. Your psychologist will explain exactly what your assessment involves before you begin.

  1. Clinical interview

    A conversation to understand your concerns, history, and goals. Usually virtual, or in person if you prefer.

    1.5 to 2 hours

  2. Testing

    In-person testing at our downtown Vancouver office, plus online questionnaires (most people take 20 to 60 minutes).

    1 to 3 sessions, about 4 hours each

  3. Collateral information

    With your consent, input from family members or others who can add context. Often recommended for a thorough report.

    Optional

  4. Scoring and review

    Scoring and interpreting results and reviewing relevant records, such as report cards, resumes, or prior reports.

    About 1 to 2 hours

  5. Written report

    Findings are integrated into a contextual written report, with a diagnosis when applicable.

    About 6 hours of report writing

  6. Feedback session

    A meeting to review your results and tailored recommendations. Optional follow-ups can support school meetings or next steps.

    1 hour

What happens after you submit the intake form

Booking an assessment starts with a short online intake form. You do not need a referral, and you do not need to know exactly which assessment you need.

  1. We review your form and contact you

    Our practice manager typically follows up within one business day to clarify your questions, recommend the right assessment and clinician, and explain the cost.

  2. We schedule your appointments

    Most assessments begin with a virtual clinical interview. In-person testing is usually offered within one to seven weeks.

  3. You receive your report and feedback

    Your psychologist reviews the results with you and provides a written report with practical recommendations.

Faster access to a psychiatrist, if you need one

An assessment often raises new questions about treatment. Clients who complete an assessment with us remain eligible for an internal psychiatric referral for six months afterward.

If your clinician believes a medication review or a second diagnostic opinion would help, they can recommend an expedited consultation with one of our consulting psychiatrists and, with your consent, coordinate the referral. Psychiatric consultations are covered by MSP, and your ongoing care continues with the clinician who already knows you.

Available to active clients: people in ongoing therapy with us (usually every two weeks), people working with one of our doctoral students, and people who completed an assessment with us in the past six months. MSP-covered psychiatric care requires a physician referral. Read our referral policy.

Frequently asked questions

What is included in a psychological assessment at Pacific Maple Psych Centre?

Our psychological assessments typically include a clinical interview, one to three in-person testing sessions, online questionnaires, optional collateral information with your consent, scoring and interpretation, a written report, and a feedback session to review results and recommendations.

How long does a psychological assessment take?

The process usually begins with a 1.5- to 2-hour clinical interview, followed by one to three in-person testing sessions that average about four hours each. Scoring, interpretation, and report writing follow, and the process ends with a one-hour feedback session.

Do I need a referral for a psychological assessment?

No. You can request an assessment yourself. Some insurers or schools ask for a referral for reimbursement or accommodation purposes, so it is worth checking with them, but our clinic does not require one.

What kinds of concerns can a psychological assessment help clarify?

Assessments can clarify mood and anxiety disorders, PTSD, OCD, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, phobias, ADHD, learning disorders such as dyslexia, dyscalculia, and dysgraphia, intellectual functioning, giftedness, and other questions about cognitive or emotional functioning.

Do you offer ADHD assessments for adults and children in Vancouver?

Yes. Pacific Maple Psych Centre provides ADHD assessments for children, adolescents, and adults. ADHD assessments typically include a clinical interview, standardized questionnaires, and an evaluation of executive functioning, and they consider other explanations such as anxiety, trauma, or autism.

Do you offer psychoeducational assessments?

Yes. Our psychoeducational assessments evaluate learning disorders, intellectual functioning, and cognitive giftedness, and the report can support school accommodations, individual education plans (IEPs), and post-secondary disability services.

Do you offer autism assessments?

Yes. We assess children, adolescents, and adults for autism. Visit our autism assessment page for details about the process and what the report includes.

Can an assessment support disability benefits or accommodations?

Often, yes. Depending on the findings, your report may support school or workplace accommodations, testing accommodations, and applications for programs such as the Disability Tax Credit or provincial disability supports. Our disability resources page links to the main BC and federal programs.

What should I bring to my psychological assessment?

If you have them, bring previous reports from professionals, a resume or curriculum vitae, report cards, and a current medication list.

Is a psychological assessment covered by insurance?

Psychological assessments are not covered by MSP, but many extended health plans reimburse assessments by registered psychologists, and unreimbursed fees can be claimed as a medical expense on your taxes. Our team will explain the cost when we contact you about your intake form.

How quickly can I book a psychological assessment?

After you submit the intake form, our team typically follows up within one business day. Assessments are often offered within one to seven weeks, depending on availability and the type of assessment needed.

Can international visitors book an assessment?

Yes. We have provided assessments for international visitors, and people travelling to Vancouver for an assessment are welcome. You need to be physically in Canada for every part of the assessment, including any appointments held by video.

Will I receive a written report after the assessment?

Yes. You receive a written report that integrates the findings, includes a diagnosis when applicable, and sets out recommendations tailored to you, along with a feedback session to discuss the results.

What happens after my assessment?

Your report and feedback session give you practical next steps for treatment, accommodations, and decision-making. Many clients go on to therapy with our team, and clients who completed an assessment with us in the past six months can be referred internally to one of our consulting psychiatrists for an expedited, MSP-covered consultation.
