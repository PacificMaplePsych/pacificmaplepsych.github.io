When medication has not
been enough

Many people with depression try one or more antidepressants and still do not feel like themselves. Others find the side effects too difficult to live with. When that happens, it can feel as though the options are running out. They are not.

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) uses focused magnetic pulses to stimulate the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, an area at the front of the brain involved in mood, motivation, attention, and emotional regulation. In depression, this area is often less active than usual. Repeated stimulation over a course of sessions is thought to strengthen these networks and encourage healthier patterns of brain activity, a process known as neuroplasticity.

rTMS has been used in Canada for more than two decades, and Canadian clinical guidelines recommend it for adults whose depression has not improved enough with antidepressant medication. It is non-invasive, involves no anaesthesia, and you can return to your day immediately after each session.

About EXOMIND and ExoTMS technology

EXOMIND is an rTMS system made by BTL Industries. It uses the company's patented ExoTMS technology, which was designed with patient comfort and consistent stimulation in mind.

Targeted stimulation

The applicator focuses magnetic pulses on the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, the area of the brain most closely linked with mood and emotional regulation.

Shaped pulses

ExoTMS uses ramp-up shaped pulses, which BTL designed to make stimulation feel more comfortable on the scalp.

Dual-core applicator

Parallel coil wiring with dual cores delivers the magnetic field efficiently, supporting a full number of pulses within each session.

Air-cooled design

An advanced air-cooling system keeps the applicator at a steady temperature, so stimulation stays consistent from the beginning of a session to the end.

Comfortable, walk-in walk-out sessions

You lie back while the applicator rests against your head. Sessions typically take under 30 minutes, and there is no recovery time afterward.

Protocols tailored to you

Your psychiatrist sets the stimulation intensity and schedule, starting from a measurement of your individual motor threshold at your first session.

What a session feels like

You arrive, settle into a reclined treatment chair, and the applicator is positioned against your head. You stay awake the whole time and can talk, listen to music, or simply rest.

Most people feel a rhythmic tapping or pulsing on the scalp and hear a clicking sound, and earplugs are provided. Some feel mild scalp discomfort or a headache during the first few sessions, which usually eases as treatment continues. If anything feels uncomfortable, the position or intensity can be adjusted.

Afterward, you can drive, go back to work, or carry on with your day. There is no sedation and no recovery period.

Is rTMS right for me?

Only a psychiatric assessment can answer this for certain, but these lists can help you decide whether to join the waitlist.

rTMS may be worth exploring if

  • You are an adult living with depression
  • You have tried at least one antidepressant without enough improvement
  • Medication side effects have been hard to tolerate
  • Anxiety has come along with your depression
  • You live with OCD and want to add to your current treatment
  • You would like a treatment that fits around work and family life

Talk with us first if you have

  • Metal or electronic implants in or near the head, such as aneurysm clips, cochlear implants, or a brain stimulator
  • A pacemaker or other implanted medical device
  • A history of seizures, epilepsy, or significant head injury
  • A diagnosis of bipolar disorder
  • A current or planned pregnancy
  • Questions about your medications

Frequently asked questions

What is rTMS?

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is a non-invasive treatment that uses focused magnetic pulses to stimulate areas of the brain involved in mood and emotional regulation. It does not involve medication, anaesthesia, or surgery, and you are awake throughout each session.

What is EXOMIND?

EXOMIND is an rTMS system made by BTL that uses the company's ExoTMS technology. It is licensed by Health Canada for adults with major depressive disorder who have not improved enough with antidepressant medication, for reducing anxiety symptoms that occur alongside depression, as an add-on treatment for adults with obsessive-compulsive disorder, and for binge eating disorder.

Who is rTMS for?

rTMS is for adults. It is most often considered for depression that has not improved enough with at least one antidepressant, or for people who could not tolerate medication side effects. It may also be considered for anxiety alongside depression, as an add-on treatment for OCD, and for binge eating disorder. A psychiatrist will help you decide whether it is a good fit.

What does an rTMS session feel like?

You lie back comfortably while a padded applicator rests against your head. Most people describe a tapping or pulsing sensation on the scalp. You are awake, can talk, and can return to your usual activities, including driving, right after the session.

How many rTMS sessions will I need?

rTMS is given as a course of several sessions over a number of weeks, and the schedule is set by your psychiatrist based on your diagnosis and response. Each EXOMIND session typically takes under 30 minutes. Some people benefit from follow-up sessions later on to help maintain their progress.

What are the side effects of rTMS?

The most common side effects are scalp discomfort and mild headache, which usually ease during or shortly after a session. Seizures are a rare but serious risk, which is why everyone is screened carefully before starting. Rarely, rTMS can trigger hypomania in people with a history of bipolar disorder.

Who should not have rTMS?

rTMS may not be suitable if you have metal or electronic implants in or near your head, such as aneurysm clips, cochlear implants, or a deep brain stimulator, or a history of seizures or epilepsy. Pregnancy, certain medications, and some medical conditions also need discussion. Your psychiatrist will review your full history before treatment.

Can I keep taking my medication and seeing my therapist during rTMS?

Usually, yes. rTMS is often used alongside medication and psychotherapy. Do not stop or change any medication without speaking to your prescriber. If you are in therapy with us, your therapist and psychiatrist can coordinate your care with your consent.

Can rTMS help with ADHD, autism, or other concerns?

EXOMIND is licensed in Canada for the conditions listed on this page. If you are interested in rTMS for another reason, tell us on the waitlist form. A psychiatrist can talk with you about the current evidence and whether rTMS would be appropriate for you.

Is rTMS covered by MSP or insurance?

rTMS in a private clinic is generally not covered by MSP, although psychiatric consultations with a physician referral are. Some extended health plans may reimburse part of the cost, so it is worth checking with your provider. We will share fees with everyone on the waitlist when we reach out. See our policies.

Do I need a referral for rTMS?

You do not need a referral to join the waitlist. Because rTMS starts with a psychiatric assessment, a referral from your family physician or nurse practitioner will be needed for the MSP-covered consultation. Our team can explain the steps when we contact you.
