Signs of Animal Abuse

Animals are especially vulnerable to abuse because they cannot clearly communicate what is happening to them and often cannot escape without human help. Many people search for signs of animal abuse, animal neglect, or animal cruelty because they suspect something is wrong but are not sure what they are seeing. This page is meant to help identify common warning signs.

Animal cruelty can also be associated with broader patterns of violence. Someone who abuses animals may also pose a risk to vulnerable humans. That does not mean every concern proves a larger pattern, but it does mean signs of animal abuse should be taken seriously. In abusive relationships, some abusers harm or threaten pets to frighten and control a partner or children, and worry about a pet’s safety can make it harder for someone to leave. Learn more on our signs of domestic abuse and escape and safety planning pages.

If you are in immediate danger, call 911. For free, confidential support in British Columbia 24 hours a day, call or text VictimLinkBC at 1-800-563-0808.

Sometimes young children harm pets without fully understanding the consequences. That still needs to be addressed seriously, but the response may involve education, supervision, and support around empathy, emotional regulation, and safe handling. In other situations, a person may secretly abuse an animal for power, control, punishment, or emotional release.

If you suspect animal abuse: document what you saw as clearly as you can, including dates, injuries, behaviour changes, living conditions, and anything you directly witnessed. Then contact the BC SPCA Animal Helpline at 1-855-622-7722 (8 a.m. to 6 p.m. daily) or, outside BC, your local humane society, SPCA, or animal control service. Outside helpline hours, contact your local police or RCMP. Anonymous reports may be possible, but direct witness information can strengthen a case.

These lists are not all-inclusive. Additional signs not seen on this page may also indicate abuse or neglect.

Signs of Animal Abuse

Hitting, kicking, throwing, or other physical harm to an animal may lead to:

  • Tucked tail or flinching at human contact
  • Difficulty walking, unexplained fractures, or limping
  • Unprovoked aggression, whining, cowering, or whimpering
  • Overly submissive behaviour, such as rolling onto the back, tail tucked, or urinating out of fear
  • Attempts to bite or scratch when petted due to fear
  • Sudden avoidance of physical contact

Signs of Animal Neglect

Failing to provide adequate food, hygiene, shelter, or medical care may lead to:

  • Changes in appearance, such as fur loss, dull coat, or mange
  • Appearing filthy or having fleas or tick infestation
  • Very thin or sickly appearance, with bones visibly showing
  • Lethargic or aggressive behaviour related to dehydration or distress
  • Living area covered in debris or feces
  • Congested eyes or ears with visible discharge
  • Difficulty walking or inability to walk
  • Obvious untreated wounds or injuries
  • Excessive barking or whining for long periods
  • Begging for food or being unusually friendly with strangers out of need

Other signs of neglect may include:

  • Being left in a kennel or chained outside all the time
  • Having little room to stand or move while tied or caged
  • Being left in a car on a warm day, even with the windows cracked
  • Being abandoned in a vacated house or empty yard
  • Too many animals living together, which may indicate hoarding

Common Questions About Animal Abuse

How do I report animal abuse in BC?

Contact the BC SPCA Animal Helpline at 1-855-622-7722, open 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. every day except statutory holidays. Outside those hours, contact your local police or RCMP. If an animal or person is in immediate danger, call 911.

What information should I include in a report?

Write down dates, times, and locations, describe the animal and the people involved, and note injuries, living conditions, and anything you saw or heard directly. Photos or video taken safely and legally can help. Never put yourself at risk to gather evidence.

What is the difference between animal abuse and animal neglect?

Abuse usually means deliberately causing harm, such as hitting, kicking, or throwing an animal. Neglect means failing to provide what the animal needs, such as food, water, shelter, hygiene, exercise, or veterinary care. Both can cause serious suffering, and both should be reported.

Is animal abuse connected to domestic violence?

Often, yes. Research has found that abusers sometimes harm or threaten pets to frighten and control a partner or children, and that animal cruelty in a home can be a warning sign of other violence. See our signs of domestic abuse page for more.

I want to leave an abusive relationship but I am worried about my pet. What can I do?

Worry about a pet’s safety keeps many people from leaving. Include your pet’s food, medications, and ownership or vet records in your escape plan, and ask VictimLinkBC or a transition house about pet-friendly options or temporary foster care. Our escape and safety planning page has a full checklist.

What should I do if a child is hurting animals?

Stay calm, stop the behaviour, and supervise closely. Young children may need to be taught gentle handling and empathy. Repeated or deliberate cruelty is more concerning and can sometimes signal that a child is distressed or exposed to violence, so it is worth speaking with a doctor or child mental health professional. Our signs of child abuse page may also help.

Is it illegal to leave a dog in a hot car in BC?

BC’s animal protection law prohibits causing or allowing an animal to be in distress, which includes exposure to excessive heat. A car can become dangerously hot within minutes, even with the windows cracked. If an animal seems to be in distress in a vehicle, call the BC SPCA or police right away.

Can I report animal abuse anonymously?

You can ask the BC SPCA or police about anonymous reporting. Keep in mind that a report from a direct witness who can be contacted for follow-up is often more useful for an investigation.
